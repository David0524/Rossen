# Tease brief (fill in and paste as the prompt)

Replace everything in [brackets]. Delete any line you don't need; the defaults in the second half apply unless you change them.

---

Make a [25–30] second tease for [Wednesday / Friday]'s Jeff Rossen live show ([Wednesday 9/30, 5 PM ET]) about
[three car scams / the topics below], then append the existing [Wednesday 5 PM] LIVE TODAY loop, played twice.

**Title card** (first 1–2 s): the Rossen logo, the title "[3 CAR SCAMS]", and "[LIVE WEDNESDAY · 5 PM ET]".

**The stories** (tease only; don't explain them):
1. **[Story 1 name]:** [one sentence: what happens, the twist, the stake, e.g. "the $10,000 car looks worthless"].
2. **[Story 2 name]:** [one sentence].
3. **[Story 3 name]:** [one sentence].

**The hook:** [who shows what on the show, e.g. "a car expert shows the red flags"]. Don't show [the red flags / the fixes /
the answers]. Don't draw [the guest expert].

**Story spine** (one line per beat; name the visual gag):
1. HOOK: [the first image, on screen right after the title card].
2. [STORY 1]: [the gag, e.g. "the Scammer fans cash in his face while the sidekick pours oil under the hood"].
3. [STORY 2]: [the gag].
4. [STORY 3]: [the gag].
5. THE PROMISE: Jeff steps in with [the magnifier]: "[WE'LL SHOW YOU THE RED FLAGS.]" Then hand off into the loop.

**Suggested cards:** "[CARD 1]", "[CARD 2]", … (short, ALL CAPS).

**Characters:** [Jeff, the Scammer, the everyday man, …]. [Attached: a new character, a reference, not a frame; animate them
as a jointed paper-cutout puppet; the [prop] is a separate piece.]

**Transitions:** make [one or two] creative, caused by a character or prop (e.g. [the smoke billows across the frame and
clears onto the laptop]); keep the rest simple.

**Audio mood:** [sneaky and comic] for story 1, [slick, too good to be true] for story 2, [tense] for story 3, confident when
Jeff steps in. A beat of silence just before the promise card.

---

Defaults (keep unless you change them):

- Same "case file" screen-print style and approved palette as the other videos; one shot; the loop's resolution, aspect and
  frame rate; fun, cute and charming, with an investigative edge and real urgency.
- Reuse printkit.js, the puppets, both logos and the sample packs. If an image or repo asset is missing, stop and tell me.
  Don't create substitutes.
- The loop: use it as-is, don't re-render it; stop and ask if it's missing, there's more than one candidate, or it shows
  another time. The last bar hands off cleanly into its first frame; match its tempo and key.
- Cards: short, bold, ALL CAPS, readable on a phone with the sound off; level; still long enough to read twice; one at a time;
  nothing moves across the words.
- Rhythm: the loop's tempo, one idea per bar, whole bars, equal bars per story, landings about 0.14 s early, foley placed by
  its audible attack.
- 9:16 safe zone: text and key action clear of the top 15 %, bottom 25 % and right 15 %; scenery may run off the edges.
- Audio: an original score in code with warm, real instruments; recorded CC0 or commercial-use sound effects, each logged in
  audio_sources.txt; say which sounds are composed.
- Rules: no real car makes, models, logos, company or dealership names, or state plate designs; plates read ABC-0000; no real
  people except Jeff; no real phone numbers or URLs; stamps and labels fully opaque.
- Verify on the final mp4: equal story bars, hit sync within about 10 ms, every card readable for its time, a clean silence
  before the promise, handoffs with no frame jump or audio bump, the loop unchanged, a safe-zone overlay pass, a CRF 20–22
  preview under 30 MB, and a list of what was verified.

## Short follow-ups that work

After the first cut, one-line notes are enough, and specific beats vague:

- "The opening is crowded; push the car right, even off the edge."
- "I have no idea what the graphic at 0:23 is."
- "The plate doesn't look like a plate."
- "The first transition is jarring; slow it down or make it a fade."
- "It feels laggy; make it smoother."
