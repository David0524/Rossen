# Show shapes and slot fit

Load when checking a slate against the day, or deciding how many stories to
research. Shapes are inherited from `rossen-script-writer`, which is the authority;
this file is the pitch-stage view of the same constraint.

## Wednesday

**Ask how many stories the show is taking before you decide how many to research.**
There is no fixed count. Three shapes air:

| Shape | What it needs |
|---|---|
| **A+B — the default** | **Two stories.** One long A story, one shorter B story. Both are full segments with their own arcs and clips. |
| **3–5 story rundown** | A variant, not the norm. One dominant A story, then two to four lighter ones in descending weight. |
| **Single-topic umbrella** | **One subject.** `TOP 5 FACEBOOK SCAMS` — the whole body is numbered sub-stories of one topic. A slate can support this with one deep topic and five findable instances of it. |

What holds across all three:

| | |
|---|---|
| **The A story** | The strongest beat on the slate. 40–55% of the body and about half the clips. Needs a human victim and multiple clip beats. A story that can't carry seven or eight pages cannot be the A story, however good the hook. |
| **Everything after A** | Lighter, descending. A trailing story with no footage at all is a normal show, not a broken one — but say so on its page. |
| **Story type is not positional** | A non-scam money-saver can be the A story, the B story, or absent. There is no required good-news closer and no three-threats rule. Do not pitch a cheerful segment to fill a slot that does not exist. |

**A slate of nothing but scams is not a failure.** A Wednesday can run two threat
stories and end on one. The old rule that a slate needed "three victims and one
non-scam closer" was wrong — do not flag its absence.

**The real slate failures** are: no story strong enough to be the A story; fewer
researched stories than the confirmed shape needs; or an umbrella pitch whose
sub-stories aren't all sourced. Those go on page one.

Sponsor drops at a story boundary — after A, or between sub-stories on an umbrella
show. Not a pitch-stage concern, but worth noting when a sponsor's product connects
thematically to a story — mention it on the page.

## Friday

**One content story, written to full Wednesday standard.** Then the deals guest —
an Amazon expert, Trey Donovan or Nader Marcos depending on the episode — and the
rest of the show is live viewer requests.

So a Friday slate needs **one story, and it has to be strong enough to carry the
whole content block alone.** Two if a second is wanted as backup.

A Friday content story can survive with less footage than a Wednesday A story, because
several aired Fridays are built on a screen-share walkthrough instead of clip
beats — Jeff showing the click path on his own screen. That form is legitimate and
is often better for a story with no natural footage.

**But it must be decided at pitch stage, not discovered in the read.** If a story
is going to be Jeff, graphics and a screen-share, the page says exactly that under
`FOOTAGE — BE HONEST ABOUT THIS`, so the room agrees to it going in.

Amazon-adjacent content stories fit Friday naturally — the deals guest is an Amazon
expert and the audience arrives for Amazon. Not a rule, but a thumb on the scale
when slotting.

Never invent deals, products, or prices. The deals block is not this document's
job.

## The SLOT FIT line

Every story page carries one line testing that story against its proposed slot:

```
**SLOT FIT:** Wednesday A story — carries it. Victim interview available,
three clip beats, mechanics run deep enough for 7-8 pages.
```

```
**SLOT FIT:** Wednesday B story — fits. Would not carry the A slot;
single clip beat and the mechanics are thin, but it is a real segment.
```

```
**SLOT FIT:** Wednesday rundown #3 — fits a lighter slot only. Two pages
and no footage. Fine third in a five-story rundown, wrong for A+B.
```

```
**SLOT FIT:** Umbrella sub-story — one of five, sourced. Non-scam
money-saver, demo segment, no victim needed.
```

```
**SLOT FIT:** ⚠️ Proposed as Friday content, but there is no footage
and no screen-share path. Needs one or the other before it can be shot.
```

Be blunt. A story that doesn't fit its slot is more useful flagged than quietly
reslotted — the room may want it anyway and move something else.

## The slate verdict

Page one, under the shortlist. Say plainly what this slate does and does not
support:

> **SLATE FIT:** Supports one Wednesday A+B and one Friday. #1 is the A story,
> #2 the B. #3 and #4 are spare, or a second Wednesday's B if an A turns up.

> **SLATE FIT:** ⚠️ Four solid threat stories, none of which carries an A slot —
> all four are two-page mechanics with no victim. Shootable as a 4-story
> rundown, not as A+B. Confirm the shape before anyone drafts.

> **SLATE FIT:** Supports a single-topic umbrella — `TOP 5 GIFT CARD SCAMS`,
> all five sourced below. Does not support an A+B; no single one of them
> carries seven pages alone.

When the slate is short, add a `THE GAP — AND WHAT I'D RESEARCH NEXT` section at
the back: unresearched concepts generated against the clickability rule, ranked by
distance from what already aired, each one labelled clearly as **not researched**.
They are candidates for the next pass, not pitches, and must never be presented as
though they carry the same weight as a sourced story.
