# Ranking, the sexy beat, confidence, viral

Load this every run. Ranking is the sheet's whole output; everything else is
presentation.

## The sexy beat

Every candidate has a topic and a beat. The topic is what the story is about. The
beat is the thing that makes someone stop scrolling. **You pitch the beat.**

| Topic (what it is) | Sexy beat (what you pitch) |
|---|---|
| Airbnb check-in fraud | There is a camera in the room where you slept |
| EBT card skimming | Something is filming your hands at the grocery checkout |
| Loyalty-app credential stuffing | A stranger can walk into the restaurant and pay as you |
| Personalized pricing algorithms | They found out your baby is sick, so they raised the price |
| Moving-company disputes | They have your entire life in a truck and won't give it back |
| Counterfeit goods on a marketplace | The car seat you bought your kid is a fake |
| Robocall consent ruling vacated | Seven spam calls a day and nobody has to stop |

The pattern: **the mechanism is never the beat.** Credential stuffing is a
mechanism. Paying as you at a counter is a beat. When you catch yourself writing
the mechanism into the shortlist, you have written the topic and stopped early.

Reach for what the story does to a person's **body, home, children, or sense of
being watched.** Those click. Cash does not click at the same rate, however large
the number.

### The wallet demotion

A pure money story — prices, fees, coupons, hidden charges — is demoted, not cut. It
can be excellent, it can be the best-sourced thing on the slate, and it still
should not lead, because a dollar figure is abstract and a camera is not.

Wallet stories earn a later slot — the B story, or a lighter slot in a rundown.
**They are not a closer slot and there is no closer slot to fill.** A show can end
on a threat story; a wallet story can also be absent entirely.
When you demote one, **say why on its page** — a `WHY IT'S DEMOTED, NOT CUT`
section. It stops the room re-litigating it and it stops a good story reading like
a weak one.

Two things override the demotion and push a wallet story up:

- **A time window that closes.** Seasonal, or a deadline.
- **It's original Jeff reporting** rather than a reaction to someone else's
  footage. A price walk Jeff does himself is a segment nobody else has.

### Sanity check on the beat

Say the beat aloud as a thumbnail. If it needs a clause of explanation before it
lands, it's still a topic. *"Hidden cameras in the fitting room"* lands.
*"Retailer surveillance practices in changing areas"* does not.

## Ranking order

Rank the shortlist by clickability first. Then, among candidates that click about
equally, break ties in this order:

1. **A genuinely viral clip already exists.** Footage solved, beat pre-validated.
2. **Any strong clip exists** — local news package, bust footage, victim interview.
3. **Time-sensitivity**, scored against the airdate. Matters, but never promotes a
   dull story over a live one.
4. **Sourcing strength.**
5. **Distance from what already aired.**

Original Jeff reporting — a demo, a price walk, a screen-share he owns — is worth a
promotion at equal clickability, because it's a segment no other outlet has.

## Confidence: HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW

One blended rating, answering exactly one question: **will this make it to a show?**

Blend clickability, sourcing, footage, and timing into the single call.

| | Reads as |
|---|---|
| **HIGH** | Airs unless something goes wrong. Beat is strong, sourcing holds or the gaps are clearly fixable, footage exists or the segment works without it. |
| **MEDIUM** | Real candidate with a real problem. Beat is soft, or the footage is missing and the story needs it, or a load-bearing fact is one source deep. Nameable and fixable. |
| **LOW** | Wouldn't bet on it. Weak beat, or the sourcing collapses at exactly the point that makes it interesting, or it's too close to something that already ran. |

**The most common error is grading sourcing instead of airing odds.** A story with a
knockout beat and one unverified figure is HIGH with a flag. A perfectly documented
story nobody clicks is LOW. If the rating moves when the research improves but not
when the beat improves, you graded the wrong axis.

Sourcing risk is carried in the verification block, not in the rating. That
separation is deliberate: it lets a great story with a research gap stay at the top
of the sheet where the room can decide to go fix the gap.

## Viral flags

Preferred, not required. A viral clip means the footage problem is already solved
and the audience has pre-validated the beat.

**Bar:** roughly 500K+ views is worth flagging; 1M+ is a headline event. Weigh
velocity too — 200K in three days on a story breaking this week beats 2M over two
years. Use judgement; these are thresholds, not a gate.

**Record every one of these, or the flag is not usable:**

- URL
- Platform
- View count, and the date you observed it
- Upload date
- What the clip appears to show, **from its title, description, or transcript**

Then, every time: **you did not watch it.** No timecode, no outcue, no claim about
what's on screen. Nobody downstream should be able to mistake a description for a
confirmation.

Mark the strongest one on the shortlist — `🔥 VIRAL` in its own column — and give it
a callout on the story page. If a story's whole case rests on one viral clip, say
that, because the story dies if the clip turns out to be unclearable.

**Local news virality counts and is easy to undervalue.** A local package doing
huge numbers is often the best possible get: real reporting, real victim, real
clearance path. Affiliate wire packages syndicate across dozens of Nexstar and
Sinclair stations with near-identical titles — dedupe by fuzzy title and keep the
earliest upload, which is usually the originating station and is the one that
matters for clearance.

Trending-but-not-yet-viral is worth a note. A beat climbing right now may be viral
by airdate.

## The shortlist table

Page one. Columns, in order:

| # | Story | The sexy beat | Viral | Slot | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Story** — the headline as it would read on air. All caps, bold.
- **The sexy beat** — one sentence, plain language, second person where it works.
  *"The store watched your searches and raised the price because your baby is
  sick."* Not a topic label.
- **Viral** — `🔥` plus platform and rounded view count, or `—`.
- **Slot** — `Wednesday A story`, `Wednesday B story`, `Wednesday rundown #3`,
  `Umbrella sub-story`, `Friday content block`. Add `TIME-SENSITIVE` and the
  closing date where it applies.
- **Confidence** — HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW, plus a short reason where one helps:
  *"HIGH — no victim yet."*

Below the table: the slate-level slot-fit verdict. Does this fill the show, and if
not, what's missing.
