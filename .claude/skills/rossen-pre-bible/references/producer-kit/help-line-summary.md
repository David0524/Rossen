# The help line: what viewers are calling about

The help line is 866-9-ROSSEN. Matt triaged 338 voicemails left between March 4 and August 30, 2026, scored each one from 0 to 10 for the call-in show, and built a database.

**This page has no names or phone numbers on purpose.** Callers are private people, and some were in distress. The full database (`Rossen_Help_Line_Voicemail_Database.xlsx`) is with Matt. Ask him for access if you're booking callers. Refer to callers by ID number in emails and documents.

## Why producers should care

- It's the first place to look for a victim when you pitch. A pitch tied to a real caller is stronger.
- It shows what our viewers are actually being hit with, which is the best audience-fit test we have.

## Do not book

031, 032, 066 to 070, 264, 300, 022, 175. These callers were in distress or weren't describing a scam. If one calls back, route them to help.

## What viewers say about the show

- Two callers asked for less preamble before the how-to. One said the videos are too long.
- About ten callers couldn't find the DealSeek promo code.
- Viewers assume Jeff personally vetted any ad that runs on the videos and any product mentioned near the show. Anything that blurs reporting and advertising is a trust problem.

## Facts the help line taught us

- The Navient settlement checks from the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau are real. Don't let a script call them a scam.
- Safe deposit boxes aren't FDIC-insured.
- The "help" that calls after you've been scammed is often round two.

## Method, if you ever redo this

- Export the voicemails and re-transcribe from the audio. Google's own transcripts get things wrong.
- Caller ID is the authoritative phone number.
- Score: 9 to 10 is book now (real victim, large or in-progress stakes, articulate, reachable, lesson applies to our audience). 7 to 8 is a producer call this week. 5 to 6 is segment material. 3 to 4 is question fodder. 1 to 2 is fan mail. 0 is empty or do-not-book.
- All scores came from transcripts alone. Nobody had spoken to the callers when they were scored.
