# What Jeff and Ryan want, and what the data taught us

From the Rossen Reports producer kit (Matt Raub, Head of Content, updated
2026-09-29). These are Jeff's and Ryan's own words where quoted, with dates.
**They outrank anything in this skill that disagrees.** Load every pitch run.

Internal team material. It quotes Jeff and Ryan candidly. Never paste it into a
deliverable.

## What Jeff wants in an A story

1. **It has to be a scam.** "Fast food feels light for an A-story. Not scammy
   either." Consumer stories without a villain are B or C stories. (Sep 28, 2026)
2. **It has to carry the show.** "I'm mostly interested in the A's for packaging
   and it's the bulk of the show." (Sep 28)
3. **It has to be for everybody.** Car scams moved out of the A slot as "too
   niche for our audience"; Medicare: "Maybe too niche for an A story." (Sept)
4. **Never build a show around Target.** "Target BOMBS with our audience. We
   have been burned literally every single time." It draws boycott demands. A
   Target item can be a quick hit inside a bigger story.
5. **Deals stories belong to Friday.** The Prime Day price check "works for the
   Friday show."
6. **Bring more options.** "Kyle & David need to provide more options each
   week. Not just a few." Send four or five A candidates.
7. **Know the advice.** Ryan: "Jeff will just want to know what the advice to
   viewers is." If you can't state the takeaway in one line, the pitch isn't
   ready.
8. **Don't pitch what we've done.** "We already did fake CAPTCHA scam show
   recently before you came. We also did fake QR code." (Sep 4) Jeff remembers.
9. **Keep it producible.** He passed on a product-testing show as too much
   production.

How he works: he doesn't give notes on documents. He rewrites his own lines on
paper an hour before each show, so a script that already sounds like him saves
him work. Feedback comes as short plain emails. He reacts to stories, not
structure. He wants to be told what to do, not handed a report.

## What Ryan looks for (the rubric)

1. **Heat on other channels.** "I lean pretty heavily towards topics that are
   overperforming on other YouTube channels... they usually have a lot of local
   news clips available."
2. **Clips.** A story with no local news or body cam footage is hard to build.
3. **"This could happen to me."** He passed on cash-for-crash partly for this.
4. **Variety.** He liked a car story because the lineup was heavy on tech and
   finance.
5. **Victims, not perpetrators.** People scamming the lottery is not a story
   about viewers getting scammed.
6. **Save the A's.** "Saving A topics for another episode is always good."
7. **Judge us against ourselves.** Competitors' audiences are younger.

Heat thresholds: a local news clip over 200K or a creator video over 500K is
real heat for our lane. News stations, consumer channels, and real estate or
retirement creators (Epic Real Estate, Holy Schmidt, Bob Sharpe) are the signal.
Scam-baiting and entertainment channels (Scammer Payback, Kitboga, penguinz0)
are not. Body cam channels validate the body cam lane, not a scam topic. More
in `competitive-landscape.md`.

## Ryan's bible notes (Oct 8, 2026, on the 10/14 free-sample show)

Left as comments on the draft bible. They govern pitches and outlines too,
because the working tease and the case order start here.

1. **Frame the scam broadly enough for every case.** "FREE SAMPLE OR EXTREME
   DISCOUNT (that covers our bases with the variations of the scam)." And on
   "mall": "Drop the 'mall' here to keep it broad" / "Let's say 'store' since
   it's broader."
2. **Real loss leads; a near-miss follows.** "Move the $26k scam first since the
   victim actually lost money and that sets some real stakes. The 'almost lost
   $50k' story is a good warning but doesn't have the same impact."
3. **Useful information throughout, not only at the end.** "After each of the
   scams, spell out the 'tell' or give a quick tip rather than waiting for the
   guest for a solution... Our best-performing live shows typically provide
   useful info throughout."
4. **Pay off the tease by name.** "Call out the 'one move' from the cold
   open... the 'one move' promised in the open is never labeled."
5. **Reaction lines are Jeff's.** "You can leave these, but Jeff may trim. His
   reactions are often his own riffs."

## Story rules the data taught us

- **The extended threat.** Every scam needs the beat that puts the viewer in
  immediate personal danger. A wrong-number text became "one wrong reply puts
  you on the dark web forever." A hidden-camera story worked when the angle was
  the photos being sold, not just the spying.
- **Audience fit beats trend.** Ask whether a 55-and-over viewer is the
  *target* of this scam or only a spectator. **The Flock license-plate camera
  show (Aug 5) flopped because our viewers like those cameras.** A trending
  topic dies if our viewer isn't the one being targeted.
- **Everyday money wins.** The top shows are about things everyone uses daily:
  debit cards (2.5M), credit cards / tap to pay (1.2M), tipping (1.1M), the
  mailbox (1.0M). The Ghost Tapping show worked because it took something people
  do every day and made it the threat.

Audience: 62% of views from viewers 55+; 65+ is 37% of views and 52% of watch
time. Homeowners, retirees, people on Social Security. 70% of views are
returning viewers.

### How this squares with the sexy-beat rule in `ranking.md`

The sexy beat still governs: pitch what the story does to a person, not the
mechanism. The kit sharpens it three ways:

- "Everyday money" is not the wallet demotion. *Your debit card gets drained* is
  a threat to something you touch daily and it leads. *Prices are higher than
  the tag* is a wallet story and it's a B or C.
- "Being watched" only clicks when the viewer is the one watched and doesn't
  want it. Surveillance tech our viewers support (neighborhood and plate
  cameras) is a flop, however creepy the pitch sounds.
- The villain has to be a scammer or a company doing it to you. A story with no
  villain is B or C at best.

## The pitch workflow (the team's order)

1. **Find candidates.** Competitor search (this month, this year). News: FTC
   consumer alerts first, then FBI PSAs, state AGs, AARP, BBB Scam Tracker, local
   TV investigative units. The help line (`help-line-summary.md`, caller IDs
   only). Viewer emails Jeff forwards. Our own top Facebook posts.
2. **Check the heat.** At least one competitor video with real views, and its
   upload date confirmed.
3. **Check our coverage.** `scripts/coverage_check.py "pattern|synonym" --context`
   over `references/transcripts/` (62 shows, Mar 18 to Sep 29, 2026). No repeat
   inside three months. Ten-plus hits over several minutes is a segment; a few is
   a B/C or an aside. Check B and C stories, not just titles: our titles are
   vague on purpose. Then the story board snapshot and the upload list for
   anything newer. Check the calendar for collisions (a Social Security pitch was
   pulled because the Medicare show was a week away).
4. **Verify every link and date.** Open it. We nearly cited a 2025 lawsuit as
   new. Reports from advocacy groups or vendors are leads, not sources.
5. **Write the pitch** (`../pitch-email.md`).
6. **Send four or five options** with a recommended order.
7. After approval: the outline w/ videos (rossen-story-outline), then the bible.
8. When the bible comes in: notes and Jeff's brief (rossen-bible-final-reviewer).
9. After it airs: story board and transcripts get updated.

## Lessons learned the hard way

- **A keyword miss is not proof.** A deed fraud check said "zero coverage." Ryan
  remembered the April 8 B story. That show falls in the Mar 20 to Apr 15 gap
  with no transcripts. Ask someone who was there, especially for anything before
  mid-April 2026.
- **False matches happen.** "Tractor" matched "contractor." "Letter" matched
  "newsletter." "Medicare" hits are usually the Chapter sponsor read. Read the
  context.
- **Writers overstate.** "On purpose" when the evidence says "mistakes," "told
  us" when the source told someone else, a national claim built on one state's
  data, "never fixed" without dates. Jeff hedges by attribution: "employees say,"
  "and I'm quoting here."
- **The company's response.** Jeff always says "we reached out" or "they didn't
  get back to us."
- **Viewers think Jeff vets everything.** Ads, sponsors, anything recommended
  near the show reads as his personal endorsement. Flag anything that blurs it.
- **Protect callers.** Caller ID numbers only. Never promise a victim their money
  back. Respect the do-not-book list.
- **Keep business matters out.** Contract terms, compensation, revenue,
  commissions, hiring plans, and assessments of team members never go in a
  pitch, brief or document.
