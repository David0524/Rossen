# The viewer call-in show

Approved in the August 28, 2026 meeting with Jeff, Ryan, and Matt. First episode aired September 16, 2026.

## What it is

- One viewer's scam story, told by the person it happened to. An expert sits with Jeff. Jeff asks the questions.
- **Callers are audio only.** They get the same guest link, and we don't take the video. Jeff's reason: viewers "aren't always what you want to look at for five minutes." Matt's reason: putting viewers on camera means producing people for no editorial gain, and our audience grew up on talk radio.
- Taped on Friday after the live show, posted Wednesday. Live-to-tape, so it can be cut.
- The strongest caller is the A story and is packaged like any other video. Two packaging lanes: victim-forward or expert-forward.

## Why it exists

Wednesday scam shows were uneven because scam topics are lumpy week to week. A show built on people doesn't run out. It also sits on the channel's best-converting content.

## Status as of late September 2026

- Episode 1 aired September 16 with AARP's Amy Nofziger. It was a big booking lift for a modest result, and it played more like one person's story than advice viewers could use.
- Matt recommended moving it from weekly to monthly or every other month. Jeff and Ryan had not ruled on that as of September 23.
- F2 tapings since then have also been standard A, B, C story shows with an expert (car scams, taped September 25). So "F2" now means the taped Friday show, which is sometimes the call-in show.

## Run of show (Matt's proposal)

Cold open on the scam itself, then Caller A's full story, expert breakdown, Caller B, a shorter expert beat, practical close. Two callers plus an expert. Every story needs the beat that puts the threat on the viewer personally.

## The open that worked

Jeff asked for this idea: "our viewers just got ripped off for hundreds of thousands of dollars, they called us for help and today we're going to hear their stories." The version that landed:

```
-OUR VIEWERS JUST GOT RIPPED OFF
-HUNDREDS OF THOUSANDS OF DOLLARS, GONE
-NOT TO STRANGERS. TO PEOPLE WHO SOUNDED LIKE THE BANK. LIKE THE SHERIFF. LIKE HELP
-SO THEY CALLED US. 866-9-ROSSEN
-AND TODAY, YOU'RE GOING TO HEAR THEIR STORIES
-IN THEIR OWN WORDS, STEP BY STEP, THE EXACT MOMENT IT WENT WRONG
-SO WHEN THAT CALL COMES FOR YOU, YOU'LL KNOW
```

Rules behind it: stakes first, series announcement second. The first 45 seconds sell the two people, not the format. Get to the help line number fast.

## Taping out of order

Guests are often taped in a different order than they air. To keep the edit clean:

1. Record the cold open to match the edit order, not the tape order.
2. Kill every ordinal. No "up first," no "our second guest."
3. Tape the connective pieces as standalones: the series announcement and help line block, the expert's bridge between stories, the closing help line ask.
4. Tape the expert's intro once, with no reference to either guest.
5. Neither segment refers to the other.
6. If Jeff ad-libs a cross-reference, redo the line clean.
7. Leave a beat of silence between sections. The bible marks these "[LEAVE BUFFER FOR EDITING]."

## Booking callers

- Sourcing: Jeff forwards viewer emails, Matt and Ryan vet, Amanda calls and books. The help line voicemail database is the main pool.
- Book five or six to land two or three.
- Pre-interview every caller before booking. Confirm the story matches the voicemail.
- Collect the paper: checks, wire receipts, police reports, the letter, screenshots. It's verification and b-roll.
- **Never promise a victim we can get their money back.** The line to use: we can't get your money back and we won't promise to, and we're putting your story out so it doesn't happen to someone else.
- Dial the number from caller ID, not a number read aloud in the message. Transcripts mangle spoken numbers.
- Check the do-not-book flags first. Some callers are in distress and should be routed to help, not booked.
- Callers are private people. Use first names on air unless they've agreed otherwise. Keep their contact details out of bibles, emails, and this kit.

## Guests and disclosure

- If a guest is related to someone on staff, disclose it on air.
- If a guest works for a sponsor, Jeff discloses the relationship in the intro, even when no sponsor read runs in that show.
- Don't let an advocacy group be the expert.
- Keep political material as the guest's own quote, not Jeff's editorial.
