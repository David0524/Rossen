# The team's line-level rules

From Matt Raub's notes on every recent bible and the voice profile
(`references/producer-kit/rossen-voice.md`). Load for every draft and every voice
pass. These are written into the document.

## The open

- **The command hook is the first line of the document.** Jeff says the slate
  (`WELCOME TO ROSSEN REPORTS. I AM JEFF ROSSEN.`) on his own, so it is not
  written in (producer, 10/14). The document opens on a direct order to the
  viewer with `YOU` in it, tied to the A story: `DO NOT USE TAP-TO-PAY WHEREVER
  YOU SHOP UNTIL YOU WATCH THIS VIDEO.` (The team writes the open in; Jeff
  rewrites his lines an hour before the show and a page that already sounds like
  him saves him work.)
- **The open stacks stakes, pre-empts the objection** (`AND BEFORE YOU'RE LIKE,
  "BIG DEAL." OH, IT'S A BIG DEAL.`), **promises proof** (`IT'S CAUGHT ON
  CAMERA`), **teases a human victim with a dollar figure**, and promises the fix.
  Stakes are stacked as separate facts, one per line — not as a built triplet
  sentence, which fails the paraphrase test.
- **Open every tease with a concrete payoff promise**, not only a threat — and
  write down every promise you make. Each one (`THE ONE MOVE`, `THE 5-SECOND
  HABIT`) gets named in the body where it lands. See "Every promise in the tease
  is paid off by name" in SKILL.md.
- **Tease nouns must fit every case.** `A FREE SAMPLE OR A CRAZY DISCOUNT`, not
  `A FREE SAMPLE`, when one victim walked in for a $25 facial. `STORE`, not
  `MALL`, unless every case was in a mall.
- **Lead the story's tease lines with the victim who lost the money**, then the
  near-miss.
- **Like and subscribe: two bare lines**, `HIT LIKE AND SUBSCRIBE` and `JOIN
  THE CHAT`. Jeff ad-libs the why (the algorithm, independent, no corporate
  overlords), where-are-you-watching-from and the launch line; the producer cut
  them from the page (10/14). **On a live show, follow them with one specific
  on-screen chat question as a red cue** (Ryan, 10/9): `(((ON SCREEN: HAVE YOU
  GOTTEN ONE OF THESE CALLS? WHAT DID THEY SAY?)))`.

## Clips

- **Clip in: cue plus verdict**, as the runway's last line: `WATCH THIS. THIS IS
  CRAZY.` / `CHECK THIS OUT.` / `ROLL CLIP ONE. THIS IS NUTS.` Never `JUST WATCH`
  or `TAKE A LOOK`. The runway above it still carries the specifics and the
  question. (Older measurement notes say never write `WATCH THIS`; the team rule
  replaces that.)
- **Clip out: a one-line button** that reacts and restates what the clip proved:
  `JUST THAT FAST, AND NOBODY KNOWS.` Never clip-to-clip or clip-to-header without
  one (a `BUTT` between segments of one source is the exception).

## Tips and facts

- **Tips are numbered** (`NUMBER ONE.`), each a command plus a reason. URLs and
  phone numbers are called to screen and repeated: `REPORTFRAUD.FTC.GOV. THERE IT
  IS ON THE SCREEN.`
- **The company's response is in the bible**, or `THEY DIDN'T GET BACK TO US.`
- **Hedge by attribution, never by softening:** `POLICE SAY`, `EMPLOYEES SAY`,
  `CONSUMER REPORTS FOUND`, `AND I'M QUOTING HERE`. Attribute to the agency,
  the court, the testing body or the person, **never to the news outlet that
  reported it** (no outlet names in spoken lines; see SKILL.md, "What the
  producer cuts"). No "on purpose" without
  evidence, no "told us" when they told someone else, no national claim from one
  state's data, no "never fixed" without dates, no vendor or advocacy statistic
  stated as settled fact. Where a legal note requires it — someone arrested but
  not convicted, a source that says "alleged" — keep the legal word; the checker
  warns on it but does not block it.
- **Numbers as he says them:** `FIFTEEN THOUSAND`, `200 THOUSAND DOLLARS`, and
  the killer number repeated (`ONE IN THREE. ONE IN THREE KROGER STORES.`). Use
  the exact figure, not a rounded one.
- **Words he doesn't say** (the checker errors on them): outrageous, totally
  amazing, functionally, discrepancies, fluctuations, furthermore, additionally,
  consumers, individuals. Soft (warns): customers, shoppers, listeners, recently,
  currently. Say `viewers`, not `listeners`.
- **Every scam carries the extended threat** — the beat that puts the viewer in
  personal danger. **Get to the how-to faster**; viewers have asked for less
  preamble. **The ending closes**; it doesn't dissolve.
- **VOD language, not broadcast clock.** `RIGHT AFTER THIS VIDEO`, never
  `TONIGHT`. Don't date-stamp a story with a month reference that ages it.

## Ad-lib and first person

- **Ad-lib stays out** — the `by the way` asides and the chat shout-outs.
  **The close is the exception (Ryan, 10/9): write it**, three short beats
  before `-END OF SHOW` (share it with someone it protects, the next video, see
  you next time). See "Seed the chat and write the close" in SKILL.md.
- **First person: one or two lines per document, hard cap.** Lived, not
  editorial — `I HATE SCAMMERS, BUT I LOVE IT WHEN THEY GET BUSTED`.
- **Reaction lines are optional by design.** `I LOVE IT WHEN SOMEBODY STEPS IN`
  can stay, but Jeff trims them — his reactions are his own riffs (Ryan, Oct 8,
  2026). So a reaction line never carries a fact, a figure or a transition the
  next line depends on; if he skips it, nothing is lost. Spend the line budget on
  specifics and tells instead.
- **Never invent a fact about Jeff's life.** His habits, accounts, family,
  possessions, what he has tried — none of it goes in unless the user supplied it
  or a producer confirmed it. Otherwise write it as an open decision:
  `(((JEFF - DO YOU DO THIS ON YOUR OWN ACCOUNT? IF SO, SAY SO HERE)))`

## Common errors to catch before delivering

A guest carried over from last week; one guest named in copy and another in the
cues; caps typos Jeff will read cold (`SENIOR DIRECT`, `PRIVACY POLICE`); a
placeholder left in (`MARY FROM TK`); an image cue after the moment it
illustrates; inconsistent guest credentials; old footage sitting under a
"brand new" or "this week" claim.
