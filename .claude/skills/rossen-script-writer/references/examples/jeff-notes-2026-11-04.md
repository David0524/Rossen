# Worked example: Jeff's notes on the 11/04 F2 ("You're Owed Money")

Source: Jeff's feedback on the 11/04 Wednesday bible (A: unclaimed money, B: the
Dollar General overcharging lawsuit, C: Walmart+ and Sam's Club bundle), relayed
by David, Oct 30 2026. The bible as shot is in the 11/4 shoot folder in Drive
(`11-04 Wed -- You're Owed Money.docx`). Jeff's notes are about **story shape and
energy**, the layer above line voice. They apply to every bible from here on.

## Jeff's seven notes

1. **Build toward the payoff.** Establish the promise and the stakes early, but
   don't give away the full answer or the tutorial in the opening.
2. **Show real results early.** Use a compelling success story to show why
   viewers should care.
3. **Every clip advances the story.** Cut repetition. Each clip adds evidence,
   raises the stakes, or introduces a new development.
4. **BOLD headlines, "like you'd see in a newspaper."** Name the subject
   immediately and make the impact on the viewer clear.
5. **Rebuild energy at every transition.** Give Jeff a strong line coming out of
   clips and changing topics, a fresh reason to keep watching.
6. **Keep momentum after each payoff.** Once one question is answered,
   introduce the next complication, risk or useful takeaway.
7. **Keep introductions simple.** Don't name one-off interview subjects unless
   we're developing them as characters. `THIS WOMAN`, not `SHERRI`.

## Where the 11/04 bible missed, with the fix

### Note 4 — the headline Jeff rewrote himself

| Written | Jeff |
|---|---|
| `NOW THE FTC AND 20 STATES ARE SUING DOLLAR GENERAL` | `DOLLAR GENERAL JUST GOT BUSTED FOR OVERCHARGING YOU` |

What changed: the **subject comes first** (the company, not the agencies suing
it), the verb is a plain-English verdict (`BUSTED`, not `ARE SUING`), and the
line ends on **you**. The process (FTC, 20 states, a lawsuit) moves into the
first lines under the header.

The formula: **[WHO] + [WHAT THEY DID, in plain words] + [TO YOU / WHAT IT
MEANS FOR YOU].** Other 11/04 headers, rewritten the same way (direction, not
final copy):

| 11/04 header | Problem | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| `THE FEDERAL GOVERNMENT AND 20 STATES JUST FILED A MASSIVE LAWSUIT AGAINST DOLLAR GENERAL` (tease) | Agency-led, process verb | `DOLLAR GENERAL JUST GOT BUSTED FOR OVERCHARGING YOU` |
| `THEN INSPECTORS WENT STORE TO STORE… EVERY ONE IN THE COUNTY!!` | No subject named; no viewer impact | `EVERY DOLLAR GENERAL IN ONE COUNTY FAILED… IS YOURS NEXT?` |
| `WALMART JUST ANNOUNCED THEY'RE COMBINING THEIR TWO MEMBERSHIP PROGRAMS` | Press-release verb (`ANNOUNCED`); no impact | `WALMART JUST CUT THE PRICE OF TWO MEMBERSHIPS… AND YOU MAY BE OWED A REFUND` |
| `AND YOU DON'T EVEN HAVE TO LOSE IT… THE STATE CAN TAKE IT ANYWAY` | Subject buried | `YOUR STATE CAN TAKE YOUR MONEY… EVEN IF YOU NEVER LOST IT` |

Escalators (`BUT`, `NOW`, `EVEN WORSE`) are still welcome, but they sit
**in front of a named subject**, never in place of one.

### Note 2 — the A story opened on statistics, not a result

The A story's first section ran four stat lines (70 billion dollars, 50
million Americans, the list of property types) before any person, then a
small win (a 20-year-old paycheck), and only then the strongest result in the
show: a woman who nearly threw away **108 thousand dollars**. The fix: open the
story on the biggest real result, then let the scale numbers explain it
(`SHE'S NOT ALONE. STATES ARE HOLDING ABOUT 70 BILLION DOLLARS`).

This doesn't conflict with "lead with the case where the money is gone." In a
threat story the real result **is** the loss; in a money-back story it's the
win. Either way the first case is the most compelling real outcome on the page.

### Note 1 — the tutorial arrived before the story had earned it

The free-search walkthrough (clip 2, `JEFF NARRATES OVER`) ran right after the
second case, with two complications and the scam still to come. The opening
clip already showed the dot-gov search. The promise (`THE FREE SEARCH I PROMISED
YOU`) was right; the placement gave the answer away early. Keep the **promise**
up top and the **method** at the payoff point, after the success story, the
stakes and the complications. A single protective line in the intro (the 10/28
rule) is still fine; a step-by-step is not.

### Note 3 — the B story proved the same thing four times

Dollar General ran four outside clips plus the New York AG beat, and three of
them restated the same proof, a shelf price lower than the register price:
trail mix (ABC), kitty litter (the Oklahoma shopper), creamer (Local 12). Each
button re-ran a price comparison. Before keeping a clip, write what it adds in
one phrase:

| Clip | Adds |
|---|---|
| ABC / GMA | Evidence: the bust itself, national scale |
| Oklahoma shopper | Raises stakes: the company's response was a gift card |
| Local 12 | New evidence: every store in a county failed inspection |
| WHIO former employee | New development: why it doesn't get fixed |

Kept that way, the clips can stay; the problem was the copy around them
repeating the price math. Where two clips' "adds" phrases match, cut one.

### Note 5 — buttons that let the air out

Lines coming out of clips that stated a fact and stopped:

- `"A LOT OF NERVE." THIS WAS BACK IN 2021.` → the date is a JEFF note; the
  button reacts and points forward: `A LOT OF NERVE… AND IT GETS WORSE IF YOUR
  MONEY WAS IN STOCK.`
- `SHE SAYS THAT'S WHAT A MANAGER TOLD HER.` → attribution belongs in the
  setup, not as the re-entry line.
- Paige's refund clip (12) ran straight into the close with no button at all.

### Note 6 — where 11/04 got momentum right

Worth copying: after the free-search payoff, the next header opened a new
problem (`FINDING YOUR MONEY IS STEP ONE… GETTING IT CAN BE HARDER`), and after
the state-seizure case, a new risk (`AND THE MINUTE YOU START LOOKING… SO DO THE
CROOKS`). That is the shape: answer, then the next complication.

### Note 7 — seven one-off first names

Susan, Barbara, Eugene and Ann, Lori, Lynda, Paige, and "Alex's wife." None of
them recurs or is developed past one clip. Each should be `THIS WOMAN`, `THIS
COUPLE`, `THIS MAN`, `A FORMER EMPLOYEE`, `ONE SHOPPER`. Barbara is the only
borderline case: she is the show's success story and returns in the recap. If
a person carries the story across beats like that, the name can stay; that is
a producer call, so write it as an open decision rather than deciding alone.
