# Rossen Banners

## Step 1: Which show is this?

Decide this before anything else, from what the user hands over. Don't ask; the
source tells you.

| Handed a... | It's the... | Banners run... | Banners follow... |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bible** | **Friday live show** | live, as the show is recorded | what the bible plans |
| **Transcript** | **Wednesday taped show** | added after recording, before it airs | what was actually said, in the order it was said |

How to tell them apart:

- **A bible** is the shooting document written before the show: all-caps lines
  starting with dashes, bold tease headlines, producer cues in parentheses
  (`(((PLAY CLIP 1 HORIZONTAL)))`, `(((SPONSOR TEASE - KILL BANNERS)))`,
  `((BRING NADER IN))`), `OUT:` cues, `HIT LIKE AND SUBSCRIBE`, `-END OF SHOW-`.
  A show plan or outline counts as a bible.
- **A transcript** is what was said on the recording: ordinary mixed-case speech
  run together, filler ("um," "uh," "you know"), `>>` speaker changes, `[music]`
  or `[applause]`, often timestamps or a title/URL header, and none of the bible's
  cues.

If both arrive, it's the taped show: the transcript decides content and order, and
the bible only helps with spellings and facts. A draft banner list on its own
isn't a source; use the bible or transcript that comes with it to pick the show,
then revise it (see "Revising a draft").

Then follow the matching rules under "Live vs taped" below. Everything else in
this skill applies to both.

## What banners are

Banners are the headline strip at the bottom of the screen on Rossen Reports, like
a news crawl that changes as the show moves. On screen they alternate with viewer
comments, so a banner comes and goes and comes back. The operator works down the
list in order, one click per banner.

A viewer who scrolls in mid-show, watches on mute, or glances up from the kitchen
reads the banner and nothing else. The audience is 55+. So each banner has one
glance to say what is happening and give a reason to keep watching, and it has to
make sense on its own every time it reappears after a comment.

Jeff's own rule, in his words: **"Banners need to be as few words as possible, to
the point, and provocative to express our point quickly, and make people want to
watch."** Everything below is that sentence made specific.

`references/jeff-edits.md` has two real shows: the writer's draft, Jeff's revision,
and why each line changed. Read it before writing your first list in a session. It
is the best evidence of his taste.

## Live vs taped

**Live, from the bible.** Jeff improvises around the bible, so write banners that
stay true however he phrases the beat. Build from the bible's tease headlines, bold
beat headers, guest entrances and payoffs.

**Taped, from the transcript.** The banners must match what aired, not what was
planned. Leave out anything the bible has that Jeff didn't say. Quotes come word
for word from the transcript. Timestamps, if present, only help with order; don't
put times in the output. Also:

- Auto-transcripts garble names ("Rosen," "Deal Seek," "Stickly"). Fix names from
  the fixed labels below or the bible; if a name is still uncertain, flag it.
- The recording includes the sponsor read. Skip it entirely: no banners during
  sponsors.
- When Jeff says he's putting something on screen ("I'm going to put at the bottom
  of the screen the official site," "this number we're putting on the screen right
  now"), that's a banner. Write it with the exact site or number he gives, at that
  point.
- A QR code or membership pitch is its own graphic, not a banner.

## Structure: always in air order

The list is strictly chronological, top to bottom, so the operator can click down,
down, down.

1. `WELCOME TO ROSSEN REPORTS`
2. **Tease every story, quickly.** One banner per story in the order Jeff teases
   them. A big lead story can take two. On shows with Amazon deals, the deals tease
   is `🔥 AMAZON'S HOTTEST DEALS`, plus at most one more 🔥 line with that week's
   hook (`🔥 WE FOUND AMAZON'S SECRET PROMO CODES`).
3. `LIKE AND SUBSCRIBE` and `JOIN THE CHAT`, where the source puts them, usually
   right after the teases.
4. **Each story when it actually airs**, with its own new banners (not a repeat of
   its tease): the guest label when the guest comes in, one banner per real turn in
   the story, and an action banner at the protection payoff.
5. **DealSeek segment:** the DealSeek host's label, then `DEALSEEK.COM/ROSSEN`. That
   banner stays up for the whole segment, hacks, deals and live requests included,
   and across any sponsor break inside it. List it once. Nothing else goes in the
   DealSeek segment, and there are never banners for individual deals.
6. Sponsor breaks get nothing.

Count follows content, not a quota. Jeff's Friday phone-scam show aired 12
banners, five of them stock or labels. His Wednesday call-in show aired 17. If two
banners say the same thing, one of them is padding.

## Stock lines and fixed labels

Use these exactly as written, every time they apply:

```
WELCOME TO ROSSEN REPORTS
LIKE AND SUBSCRIBE
JOIN THE CHAT
🔥 AMAZON'S HOTTEST DEALS
DEALSEEK.COM/ROSSEN
NADER MARCOS, DEALSEEK CO-FOUNDER
TREY DONOVAN, DEALSEEK FOUNDER
JIM STICKLEY | ETHICAL HACKER
AMY NOFZIGER | AARP FRAUD VICTIM SUPPORT
SCAM HOTLINE: 866-9-ROSSEN
CALL AND SHARE YOUR SCAM STORY!
```

The hotline lines are for call-in shows. The DealSeek host varies (Nader or Trey);
the bible or transcript says who's on.

**A guest with no fixed label yet:** `FULL NAME | SHORT CREDENTIAL` a viewer
understands, taken from the bible or transcript. **A call-in or on-camera victim:**
`ON THE PHONE: FIRST NAME | STAKES`, first name only, never a city.
`ON THE PHONE: MARY | SCAMMED FOR $200,000`. With no dollar figure, say what they
are: `ON THE PHONE: ALAN | NEW SCAM VICTIM`.

**Emoji:** 🔥 on the Amazon deals tease at the top of the show, and nowhere else.
No other emoji, ever.

## The tests

Run every banner through these. Each comes from an edit Jeff actually made.

**One glance, one idea.** Aim for 4–7 words and about 38 characters. Jeff's
longest approved banner is 8 words and 45 characters; treat that as the ceiling.
A dash, comma, or semicolon joining two thoughts means it's two banners, and
usually only one survives: `ALAN SKIPS JURY DUTY—FAKE COPS SAY "JAIL TIME"` became
`FAKE COPS THREATEN JAIL`.

**A stranger gets it cold.** No jargon, industry terms, or bible shorthand, even
if the segment explains them. Jeff cut every `"PORT-OUT" FRAUD` banner, dropped
`KIOSK`, and flagged `SCAM "RECOVERY" SERVICES ROB VICTIMS, AGAIN` with "I don't
know what this one means." If Jeff can't parse it, a viewer who just tuned in
can't either.

**Say what it does to *you*.** Name the concrete harm to the viewer, and pick the
biggest harm the story supports. `NEW SCAM TARGETS YOUR NEW PHONE` became
`THIS NEW PHONE SCAM STEALS YOUR NUMBER`. `"NEW PHONE" SCAM LEAVES YOU WITH BILL`
became `THIS "NEW PHONE" SCAM CAN DRAIN YOUR ACCOUNT`. Use YOUR and YOU.

**Strong verb, real urgency.** `IMPOSTER SCAMS ON THE RISE` became `IMPOSTER SCAMS
EXPLODING RIGHT NOW`. Verbs that work: STEALS, HIJACK, DRAIN, THREATEN, EXPLODING,
SPREADING FAST. Newsroom filler that doesn't: ON THE RISE, BEWARE, TARGETS, AMID.

**"THIS" makes them stay.** `THIS NEW PHONE SCAM`, `CHANGE THIS SETTING`, `THEY SEND
THIS ARREST WARRANT`. "THIS" points at something Jeff is about to show and opens a
gap only watching closes. Two or three times a show at most, or it goes numb.

**Protection is an action.** The payoff banner tells the viewer what to do.
`PREVENT "PORT-OUT" FRAUD BEFORE IT HAPPENS!` became `CHANGE THIS SETTING ON YOUR
PHONE NOW`.

**Cut the adjective that tells them how to feel.** `THEY SEND TERRIFYING FAKE
ARREST WARRANT` became `THEY SEND THIS ARREST WARRANT`. Keep words that carry
information (FAKE, SECRET, STOLEN); drop the ones that editorialize (TERRIFYING,
INSANE, SHOCKING).

**Stakes beat geography.** Drop cities and states. `ON THE PHONE: MARY | OCALA,
FLORIDA` became `ON THE PHONE: MARY | SCAMMED FOR $200,000`, and `OCALA POLICE`
became `POLICE`. Keep a place only when the place *is* the story.

**Quotes when the words are the hook.** `SCAMMERS: "DEPOSIT $8,000" RIGHT NOW`,
`POLICE: MONEY "PROBABLY WENT OVERSEAS"`, `SCAMMERS WARN "DON'T TELL YOUR FAMILY"`.
Scare quotes mark the lie: `FAKE WELLS FARGO EXEC "INVESTED" MARY'S MONEY`. Every
quote must be real.

**Don't close the story.** Jeff cut `ALAN BARELY ESCAPES $8K SCAM`, which gave away
the ending, and folded `MARY LOST ALMOST $200K` into her name label.

**Every banner is new.** The 9/25 draft said "hijacks your phone and bank" three
ways. Jeff kept one.

**True and defensible.** Only facts and numbers from the source. Hedge with CAN
when the harm is possible rather than certain. Provocative never means invented.

**Mechanics.** All caps. Full dollar figures (`$8,000`), never `$8K`. `&` instead
of AND when it saves room. Past tense for what happened to a victim, present tense
for the threat to the viewer. Exclamation points only on stock lines.

**Finished copy, no placeholders.** Jeff once received `*amazon deal line with the
emoji*` and `AMAZON DEALS BANNERS + LABEL FOR NADER` and had to write them himself.
Every line you send is ready to air. If a fact is missing or the sources disagree,
write the best-supported version and flag the exact gap below the list.

## Workflow

1. Decide the show from the source (Step 1), then read the whole bible or
   transcript.
2. Map it in air order: the teases, stock-line spots, guest entrances, each story's
   turns, on-screen cues, payoffs, sponsor breaks, the DealSeek segment.
3. Write top to bottom following **Structure**.
4. Run the tests on every line.
5. Run the checker and fix every flag or have a reason not to:
   ```bash
   python scripts/check_banners.py banners.txt
   ```
   It catches mechanical misses (length, caps, placeholders, `$8K`, locations,
   emoji, weak phrases, joined ideas, near-duplicates, the DealSeek pair, fixed
   labels). It can't judge clarity or stakes; a clean run means the mechanics pass.

## Deliver

The finished list in chat inside a plain code block so it copies clean: one
banner per line, in air order, no dashes, bullets, numbers or times, nothing else
in the block. Save it as a .txt instead only if the user asks for a file.

Below the block, only if needed, a short **Check before air** note naming each
real gap (a disputed name, an unconfirmed number). No commentary on the banners, no
explanation of choices, no version history.

## Revising a draft

When someone hands over a draft list, do what Jeff does: keep every line that
already passes the tests word for word, rewrite the ones that don't, cut
duplicates, fill every placeholder, and fix the order. Deliver the clean list only,
with no trace of what the draft said or what changed.
