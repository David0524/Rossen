# Template: Jeff's 30-second brief

Sent the night before a shoot day, once the bibles are in. One email covering both shows. Jeff should be able to read each show in 30 seconds.

```
Subject: F1 and F2 bibles for tomorrow — the 30-second version

Jeff,

Both bibles are in. Here's each show in 30 seconds.

F1 — "[Show frame]" (live, with [guest])
- A: [Name of the story]. [Three or four sentences: what happens to the victim, in order. Name the clips and who's in them.]
- [Guest]'s beats: [what they explain], then the one-line close.
- B: [Name]. [Two or three sentences.]
- Close is [the fix], on screen.
- [Sponsor] spot sits between [x] and [y], then deals with [deals guest].
- Why it works: [one or two lines].

F2 — [Show frame] (tape, with [guest])
- A: ...
- B: ...
- C: ...
- Why it works: ...

Heads-ups
- [Anything that could trip him live: a guest name mismatch, a clip that isn't pulled, a fact that needs confirming.]

Matt
```

## Rules

- Read the whole bible before writing. The brief describes what's actually in it, not what was assigned.
- Name every clip by who's in it ("the Massachusetts woman"), not by number.
- State the viewer advice for each story.
- The heads-ups section is the most useful part. Look for: guests who aren't booked, names that don't match between pages, links that aren't video, stats without dates.
- If you polished someone else's bible, credit them. "Amanda's reporting and structure are all there."
