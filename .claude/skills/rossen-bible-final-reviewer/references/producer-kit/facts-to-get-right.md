# Facts to get right

Things we've already had to correct or need to be careful with. Check here before a number or a claim goes into a pitch, a bible, or a description. Add to it when you catch something new.

## Story facts

| Topic | Get it right |
|---|---|
| Email bombing | The fraud comes first, then the flood of email to bury the bank's alert. "Hundreds or thousands" of emails, not "a million." Proofpoint's figure is 1,500+ an hour. The emails are real newsletter sign-ups. A widely quoted "72%" figure is about IT-impersonation attacks in general and needs a better source. |
| Medicare open enrollment | October 15 to December 7. No marketing for next year's plans before October 1. "$60 billion a year in fraud" and "a Medicare number is worth more than a credit card number" both need primary sources. |
| Kroger pricing | "58 of 389" and "one in three stores" are an advocacy group's reading of Ohio inspection records. Nobody had independently re-run the math. Attribute on air to "Ohio state inspection records" and "Consumer Reports," never to the campaign. Consumer Reports' May 2025 numbers are the safe fallback. Kroger's banners include Ralphs, Fred Meyer, Fry's, Harris Teeter, King Soopers, Smith's, and QFC. |
| Dynamic pricing | Kroger has publicly denied surge pricing. Attribute concerns to critics. |
| Zelle | The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's suit was dismissed in March 2025. New York's attorney general sued in August 2025, and a court refused to dismiss it on July 20, 2026. Zelle's imposter reimbursement policy dates to 2023 and depends on the bank. |
| Bitcoin ATMs | The DC attorney general's suit against Athena Bitcoin is from September 2025, not 2026. |
| Retail shrink | The $112 billion figure is the National Retail Federation's number for 2022. Not "last year." |
| FTC alerts, September 2026 | Cloned dealership websites: September 1. Fake farm equipment dealers: September 15. |
| Fake Apple and Amazon fraud alerts | The underlying scam is old. The FTC warned about it in December 2020. Only the August 2026 "$149.99 pop-up" variant is new. |
| IRS letters | The IRS mailed about 1.4 million legitimate CP53E notices with QR codes, so "any letter with a QR code is fake" is wrong. |
| Navient settlement checks | Real. Not a scam. |
| Gold bar scam, New York | NYPD figure is "$100 million plus." |
| Deed fraud | We covered it as a B story on April 8, 2026. Say so in any pitch. |

## Show facts

- **F1** is the Friday live show. **F2** is the show taped the same Friday and posted the following Wednesday. Jeff, September 2026: "the F2 (taped show) is going to be our call-in show." Since then some F2 tapings have been standard story shows. Older documents use "F2" for the Wednesday show in general.
- **Target** is not off-limits, but never the title or a main story: Jeff says those didn't perform well (10/7/2026; replaces the old "off-limits" rule). A passing mention inside another story is fine.
- **Shorts** stopped in June 2026. Settled.
- The store is spelled **Rossen**, as in "the Rossen collection." Transcription tools write "Rosson."
- The Ghost Tapping bible is labeled "TAPE 08/28" but it's the live-show template.

## Deals segment

- The deal sheet's "Retail Price" column has been higher than Amazon's actual current price for most products on at least one show. **Quote the current price from the deal link, not the sheet.**
- If a price is the Subscribe and Save price, say so on air.
- Price history: CamelCamelCamel and Keepa work for name-brand products only, and both block automated lookups. Use the browser extension.

## Numbers that drift

- Subscriber count: about 645K on August 27, 2026. Older documents say 620K+ and 600K+.
- Audience gender split: two sources conflict. Check Studio.
- View counts in this kit are as of the date shown. Recheck before quoting.

## Tools that mislead

- Google Voice transcripts mangle phone numbers. Use caller ID.
- Machine transcripts of body cam audio are rough. Proofread names, dollar amounts, and legal terms.
- YouTube search results don't show upload dates. Confirm before citing.
- A keyword search that finds nothing is not proof we never covered something.
