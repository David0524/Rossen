## The numbers

- Median sentence: 10 words. Mean 12.9. Roughly 30% of sentences are 6 words or fewer. Fragments are normal ("By the hundreds." "Got it." "Nobody.").
- Second person is constant: "you/your" about 4.5 per 100 words. "we/our" about 1.2. A line written about "customers" or "shoppers" or "individuals" is not in his voice. Make it "you."
- Top sentence openers: And, So, I, You, This, It's, They, But. He starts sentences with "And" and "So" more than anything else.
- Pace: about 185 to 195 words per minute on the scripted parts. 850 words of VO is roughly 4.5 minutes of Jeff.
- Numbers are said as figures and then repeated: "by 90% since last year. 90%." "By the hundreds. By the hundreds." Dollar amounts always: "$40,000," "$199," "$1.6 million." He never leaves a story without a dollar figure attached to a person.

## The show template (every episode, same order)

1. **Slate:** "Welcome to Rossen Reports. I am Jeff Rossen."
2. **The command hook, in the first 15 seconds.** A direct order to the viewer tied to the story: "Do not go fill up your car. Do not use your ATM card until you see this." / "Do not use tap-to-pay wherever you shop until you watch this video." / "If there is a specific sticker on it, I don't want you to open it."
3. **Stakes stack, three fast blows:** "They will wipe out your life savings. They will wipe out your accounts. They will charge their credit cards. And yes, the police will even come to your door."
4. **Pre-empt the objection.** He argues with the chat before the chat argues: "And before you're like, 'Big deal.' Oh, it's a big deal." / "It's not just happening to idiots, stupid people. I see the comments. No, it's happening to everyday people, people like you and me."
5. **Proof promise:** "It's caught on tape." "We're able to actually show you." "We just did some research."
6. **Victim tease with a dollar figure:** "There's a woman you're going to meet who just lost $40,000 to this."
7. **Fix promise:** "I'm going to tell you exactly what to look for." "And what you can do about it. You know, here we always give options on that too."
8. **Rundown of the rest of the show:** "Plus..." "There's also..." "We also have this crazy story I just read about..." Each one gets a hook and a "wait until you see."
9. **Housekeeping with the why attached:** "The YouTube algorithm loves when you hit like. I don't know why. They send it out to more people and it really helps to support our consumer journalism. We are independent." "We have no corporate overlords." "Join the chat. Let us know where you're watching from. Uncensored. Good, bad, or indifferent."
10. **Launch line:** "All right, let's get right to it." / "Let's begin and get right to it."

## How he builds a story segment

- **Claim, then scale, then recency:** "This scam has just exploded according to a new survey by 90% since last year. 90%." "This has become an epidemic here in 2026." "It just crossed my desk this morning."
- **Mechanics in plain words, with a running gag:** "Now there's a new version called shimming. I guess they've run out of clever words."
- **Clip intro is two beats: a cue and a verdict.** "Watch this." "Check this out." "Roll clip one. This is crazy." "Watch this. This is sick." "Here's how it happened. Watch clip three." "This is nuts." He never says "just watch" or "take a look."
- **Post-clip button, always.** One-line reaction, then restate what the clip proved: "Just that fast, and nobody knows." "Yeah, homegirl, let's do better. Amazon, come on." "Okay, so if you're keeping track at home that's used underwear, used bras, and a used neck brace for a disabled woman. Got it." He never goes clip-to-clip or clip-to-header without a button.
- **Escalation ladder between beats:** "But that's not even the worst way. It gets worse." "But this next tactic is even scarier." "And here's the kicker."
- **Pre-empt disbelief:** "And if you think that I'm being far-fetched, this is happening every day." "I'm not joking around here."
- **Quotes are flagged out loud:** "and I'm quoting here, 'high-quality standards.'" "I want to get this right."
- **The company is a character and he holds it accountable:** "Amazon knows exactly what's going on." "Here's what makes me angry. You expect your bank to have your back." He usually includes what the company said or that they didn't respond.
- **Personal anchors:** "It actually happened to one of our producers." "My wife is obsessed with this app." "My kids are older, but when we were shopping for pacifiers..." "I get very stressed about money. Shocker, it's why I started this whole channel."
- **Lists of three, concrete and a little funny:** "Worn out slippers, blood-stained jeans, torn up books." "Cheerios to boneless beef to dog food" is exactly his shape.

## How he does the tips

- Setup line: "So, how are you supposed to protect yourself? There are ways." / "Here's how to spot it." / "So what can you do?"
- **Numbered.** "Number one." "Number two." He uses numbered tips constantly (29 times across five shows).
- **Each tip is a command plus a reason:** "Turn on all the alerts for your bank debit card, so you know if there's a transaction the minute you walk away." "Use credit cards, not debit cards, because credit cards are usually insured." "Go paperless. There's really no need anymore in 2026."
- **Absolutes for the big one:** "Never, ever, ever give your one-time verification code to anybody. Nobody."
- **Puts the URL or phone number on screen and says so:** "reportfraud.ftc.gov. There it is on the screen. Reportfraud.ftc.gov." Then the why: "The reason it's so important to report this stuff is so it gets spread out. We read about it."
- **Ties back to the chat:** "Uh true31386 says best thing to do is go to the bank teller. I see a bunch of comments coming in like that."

## How he goes to a sponsor

He never enters a sponsor read cold. Two moves, every time:

1. **Tease what's next first, then break:** "There's another home scam that may be one of the worst I've ever seen. It's exploding right now in 2026. But first a quick word from our sponsor."
2. **Bridge from the content into the product with something personal:** "As you know, I've dedicated my life to protecting you from terrible scams, and that's why I'm thrilled about today's sponsor, OmniWatch." "Now, I'm just going to say it. I love food. I wish I had time to cook." "I get very stressed about money. Shocker."

First sponsor read usually lands 13 to 20 minutes in, after the lead story is fully told.

## Review checklist (run a script against this)

1. Does the first line after the slate give the viewer an order with "you" in it?
2. Are the stakes stacked in threes, and does he argue with the "big deal" objection?
3. Is there a human victim with a dollar amount teased in the open?
4. Every clip: cue plus verdict going in, one-line button coming out?
5. Escalators between beats ("It gets worse")?
6. Every number said as a figure, and the killer number repeated?
7. Tips numbered, each a command plus a reason, URL/phone called to screen?
8. Sponsor entered with a tease of what's next plus a personal bridge? Not before the lead story is done?
9. Any word from the "doesn't say" column?
10. Any sentence over 25 words that isn't a list?
11. Did we say what the company said, or that they didn't respond?
12. Any claim stated flat that he'd get called out on in the chat? He hedges by attribution, not by softening: "employees say," "Consumer Reports found," "and I'm quoting here."
