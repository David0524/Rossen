# Sponsor script rules

These are the house rules for sponsor reads, inherited from Jacob Salamon, who ran operations until August 31, 2026. Olivia Flynn drafts sponsor scripts. Matt reviews them. Apply these to every sponsor script.

**The override that beats every rule below:** always pull the sponsor's talking-points document first. Anything the sponsor marks "read verbatim" is untouchable, even if it breaks a house rule.

## Structure and disclosure

1. Always disclose the sponsor in the script: "the sponsor of this episode." Not negotiable.
2. Jeff doesn't say taglines. Strip them.
3. Use the sponsor's talking points for the current month.
4. Reads are 60 seconds. Cut anything already said earlier in the script.
5. Remove bracketed directions like "[Host: Spell out name]." Keep the spelled-out URL and code.
6. Attribute claims to the brand: "from Ollie," "from real vets."
7. Use the sponsor's exact offer language and the QR code they supplied.

## Language

8. Natural contractions. "Couldn't," not "could not."
9. Anchor to time. "This week," not "right now."
10. Don't name the product in two sentences back to back.
11. Frame around convenience: "tastes fresh from the bakery, without leaving the house."
12. No "limited time" or seasonal framing unless the sponsor confirms it.
13. Relatable beats specific. "Afternoon wall," not "2 PM wall." Cut hedges like "sometimes."
14. Use the product name rather than "it," but not back to back.
15. Numbers the way people say them. "More than 60 trace minerals," not "60-plus."
16. Cut hype tails. "Daily routine," not "no matter WHAT you're doing."
17. Spoken URLs drop "https://."

## The intro line

18. "And that's why I love [Brand], the sponsor of today's episode." No slogan on the intro.

## The deal block

Use this order only when the online offer is the main call to action. Some campaigns are retail-first, and then the link is the backup.

19. "And I love finding you a great deal!" Then the percent or dollars off, first. Then the URL and code. Then restate: "That's [URL] with code ROSSEN for X off." Then one call-to-action line: "Or scan the QR code or click the link in the description."
20. One call to action. Don't stack them.
21. Flag any read over 60 seconds. Cut history lessons down to one trust credential.

## The lower third

22. Leave margin around the sponsor's logo.
23. Offer text is larger than the link and code.
24. Feature text is bright yellow, #FFF000.
25. Dark bar. Sponsor's QR code present.

## Workflow

26. Pull the talking-points document before finalizing.
27. Verbatim-marked lines, including pinned-comment copy, don't get edited.
28. Sponsor documents may also require description copy, a pinned comment, a show-notes link, the product on screen, or a flavor focus. Treat those as a production checklist.
29. The deal-block order applies only when the online offer is primary.
30. Test the vanity link before air. If it's dead, Olivia coordinates the fix.

## The model to copy

The Monarch script from September 2026 was approved with zero notes. Its shape: an emotional hook tied to the channel's mission, disclosure in the second paragraph, one hero feature explained simply, a short analogy, the deal in exact offer language, and a closing restatement of the URL and code.

## Placement inside a show

Disclosure up top. Placed at a natural break. Never in the middle of a victim's story or on a cliffhanger. In the livestreams, Jeff teases what's coming next and then bridges into the sponsor with something personal.

## Audience trust

Our viewers take Jeff's sponsor reads and any nearby recommendation as his personal endorsement. Anything that blurs reporting and advertising is a priority fix. If a sponsor's category sits awkwardly with a consumer-protection show, raise it with Matt directly rather than in a document comment.

## How Matt wants notes on sponsor scripts

Straight find-and-replace edits. No explanation.
