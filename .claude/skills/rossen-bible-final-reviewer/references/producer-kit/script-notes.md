# Template: script notes for the writer

These get pasted as comments on the Google Doc. The writer wants "replace this with this," not an essay.

```
Page N
- "ANCHOR TEXT FROM THE SCRIPT" → "Replacement line in Jeff's voice."
- "ANCHOR TEXT" → typo.
- "ANCHOR TEXT" → Cut. Reason in five words.
- After [clip] → add a button line: "One-line reaction."
```

## Rules

- One line per note. Anchor first so she can find it.
- Give the replacement in Jeff's words, not a description of what's wrong.
- Accuracy notes are questions or confirmations, kept short: "Confirm 'layoffs' vs. 'staffing cuts.'"
- Check the script against the voice profile's 12-point checklist.

## What to look for every time

1. First line after the slate: is it an order to the viewer with "you" in it?
2. "Customers," "shoppers," "individuals" → "you."
3. Words Jeff doesn't say: outrageous, totally amazing, functionally, discrepancies, fluctuations.
4. Clip intros: a cue plus a verdict. "Watch this. This is crazy." Not "just watch" or "take a look."
5. A one-line button after every clip.
6. Tips numbered, each a command plus a reason.
7. Sponsor breaks: tease what's next first, then break. Never cold.
8. The deals handoff: live-request pitch before the guest intro. "Trey, what's going on? Let's get right to it."
9. Claims stronger than the evidence.
10. The company's response, or "they didn't get back to us."
11. Typos and doubled words.

## When asked to rewrite a whole bible

Keep the formatting, the order, every cue, every guest line, and the timing of the guests. Rewrite Jeff's lines only. Truncate interviews so Jeff narrates the setup and the guest speaks at the three or four moments that need their voice.
