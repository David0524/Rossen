# How a bible is built

Amanda writes the bibles. Matt sends the structure, beats, and sources. A first pass is due the day before the show. Jeff riffs off the page, so treat guest questions as a map, not a script.

The reference is the Ghost Tapping live bible, taped August 28, 2026. It aired September 2 as "CREDIT CARD ALERT — This NEW 'Tap to Pay' Scam Drains EVERYTHING On Your Card" and did 1.2M views. Every bible since has been asked to follow its skeleton.

## Page conventions

- Every line starts with a dash and is in ALL CAPS. One thought per line.
- The first line of a page is bold and larger. It's the section opener. Each section starts a new page.
- Second person throughout. "YOU WON'T BELIEVE," "HERE'S EXACTLY HOW."
- Numbers spoken plainly: "FRAUD UP 150% SINCE JUST LAST YEAR," "200 THOUSAND DOLLARS."
- Say "viewers," not "listeners."
- Stage directions and cues are in red, in parentheses.

## Cue notation

| Cue | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `(((CLIP 3 HORIZONTAL)))` then the link, then `[00:44 - 01:05] (OUT: FOUR STAR REVIEWS)` | Outside clip, its timecodes, and the last words spoken before we cut back |
| `(POP UP: IMAGE OF ...)` or `(IMAGE #4 – DESCRIPTION)` | Full-screen or over-the-shoulder graphic |
| `(VIDEO SCREENGRAB)` or `(VIDEO OF AT&T)` | Screen recording or live demo |
| `(JIM JOINS THE ROOM)` / `(BRING TREY IN)` / `(MARY EXITS)` | Guest in or out |
| `(JIM ANSWERS)` / `(LAUREN EXPLAINS MECHANICS)` | Guest speaks. The note says what they should cover. |
| `(CLEAR BANNERS FOR AD)` / `(KILL BANNERS FOR AD)` | Lower thirds off before a sponsor read |
| `(OMNIWATCH SPOT)` | Sponsor read plays |
| `((QR CODE UP))` | Deals QR code on screen |
| `[LEAVE BUFFER FOR EDITING]` | Pause so the editor has a clean cut point |
| `(OPTIONAL TIP)` | Jeff can use or skip |
| `(KYLE: zoom in and circle ...)` | Note to the graphics operator |

## The skeleton for a live scam show

1. **Cold open.** Headlines for the A story, then the B story, then the C story, then the expert tease. Short lines that ratchet.
2. **Like, subscribe, join the chat.**
3. **A story.**
   - Mechanics beat: how the scam works, in order.
   - Victim clip.
   - Bring in the expert.
   - Escalation: "BUT IT GETS WORSE."
   - Tease what's next, then the sponsor read.
   - Back to the story. Scale beat: "THIS IS NOT A ONE-OFF."
   - "Your bank might not have your back."
   - Practical close: "HERE'S EXACTLY HOW" to protect yourself.
4. **B story.** Same shape, shorter, often with a live screen demo.
5. **C story.**
6. **Expert questions.** Four, numbered.
7. **Deals block** on Friday shows.

## Rules

- **The sponsor break goes after the victim's story, not in the middle of it.** Never cut a cliffhanger for an ad. Tease what's coming, then break.
- **Every scam needs the beat that puts the viewer in personal danger.** A wrong-number text scam became "one wrong reply puts you on the dark web forever." A hidden-camera story worked when the angle was the photos being sold, not just the spying.
- **Get to the how-to faster.** Viewers have asked for less preamble.
- **Outside clips are cleared** and shouldn't crowd out Jeff on camera. Matt's standard is about two per segment.
- **Every number has a source,** and the wording matches the source.
- **Guest credentials are consistent** across the bible and match the segment's topic.
- **The ending has to close.** It shouldn't dissolve.
- **The company's response is in the bible,** or the line that they didn't respond.
- **Links in the bible should be video.** If a clip lives on a news site rather than YouTube, flag it so it gets pulled ahead of time.

## Common bible errors to catch

- A guest carried over from last week's bible.
- One guest named in the copy and a different one in the cues.
- Typos in caps that Jeff will read cold: "SENIOR DIRECT" for "DIRECTOR," "PRIVACY POLICE."
- A placeholder left in: "Mary from TK."
- An image cue placed after the moment it illustrates.
- A statistic from a vendor or advocacy group presented as settled fact.
