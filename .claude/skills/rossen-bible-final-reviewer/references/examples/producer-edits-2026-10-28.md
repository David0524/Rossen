# Worked example: producer edits to the F2 10/28 bible

Claude's draft ("\"YOUR CARD DECLINED\" GOT HER CHARGED $53,000", A + B + C, guest Tom Hyslip
in B) compared against the team's preliminarily final version (`F2_Wed_10_28_--.docx`).
Story C was cut for time, so it isn't covered here.

Internal team material. Never paste it into a deliverable.

How to use it: read this alongside `ryan-notes-2026-10-09.md`. Ryan's notes are about
structure and retention. These edits are mostly about **what earns air time**, **Jeff's
own voice and archive**, and **headers that talk to the viewer**. The before and after
pairs below show each rule in practice.

---

## The short version

The bones held. The team kept the story order, the Mary Ellen and New Mexico cases, the
"one move" promise and its payoff, both protection lists word for word, and the guest
block. They made four kinds of change:

1. **They cut the investigative chain down to what the viewer needs.** A went from five
   clips to three, plus Jeff's own video. The company's door-knock, its lawyers'
   response, the Hawaii settlement and the anonymous FTC complaint in B all came out.
2. **They put Jeff in it.** A first-person line about his past warnings opens the story,
   and his own 2025 video replaced the CBS4 Indy explainer.
3. **They rewrote the headers as viewer-facing warnings and scope escalations**, where
   the draft had place-led plot summaries.
4. **They removed hedges and labels.** Dating guards, the "asked not to use her name"
   line, repeated "HERE'S YOUR RED FLAG:" labels, and more than half the JEFF notes and
   red cues all went.

## Lessons for next time

### 1. Use Jeff's own coverage: in the intro, and as a clip

The draft had Jeff's December 2025 short on this scam, but tucked it into a JEFF note as
optional, to keep the first-person count under the cap. The team led with it and rolled
it as the online segment's clip.

- **Before:** `{{JEFF PLEASE NOTE: You warned about this in your Dec 15, 2025 short; say
  so here if you like.}}`
- **After (first line of A):** `-I'VE BEEN WARNING YOU ABOUT ONLINE CARD DECLINE SCAMS,
  BUT THEY STILL HAPPEN IN BRICK AND MORTAR STORES. THOSE OLD SCAMS CAN STILL WORK.`
- **After (online segment):** `I REPORTED ON A SIMILAR ONLINE VERSION OF THIS SCAM LAST
  YEAR` / `-TAKE A LOOK. WE CAN'T GET OFF THIS ROLLER COASTER` / `((ROLL JEFF VIDEO
  FROM 2025))`

**Rule:** When the outline or research shows Jeff already covered the scam, (a) open
with a first-person line that frames today's story against what he warned about, and
(b) propose his own video as the clip for that beat, ahead of any third-party
explainer. The CBS4 Indy clip already graded as A's weakest (72). Jeff's own footage
was the obvious upgrade, and the grade should have said so. Jeff's own reporting
history doesn't count against the first-person cap.

### 2. Headers are warnings and escalations, not place-led plot

| Draft | Final |
|---|---|
| `YOU AGREE TO THE PRICE… THEN THE CLERK SAYS ONE SENTENCE` | `THE CLERK IS ASKING YOU FOR ANOTHER CREDIT CARD, BE SUSPICIOUS` |
| `IN CALIFORNIA… EVEN HER BANK TRIED TO STOP IT!!` | `BANKS ARE TRYING TO STOP YOU FROM DOING THIS BUT ARE FAILING` |
| `AND IN NEW MEXICO… SHE AGREED TO 600 DOLLARS. THEN SHE OPENED HER BANK APP!!` | `THIS IS HAPPENING NOT ONLY IN STORES BUT ALSO SPA[S]` |
| *(none: "SO REMEMBER THIS…" lines)* | `IN STORES AND ONLINE, YOU NEED TO FOLLOW THE SAME STEPS` |
| `IT LOOKS LIKE YOUR DOCTOR'S OFFICE… AND IT WANTS YOUR CARD` | `THAT EMAIL LOOKS LIKE IT'S FROM YOUR DOCTOR… BUT SCAMMERS WANT YOUR MONEY!!` |

**Rule:** A header tells the viewer what to do or how big this is. Write it as a
warning ("BE SUSPICIOUS") or as an escalation by scope or venue (stores → spas →
online). Don't lead with a state (`IN CALIFORNIA…`, `AND IN NEW MEXICO…`). Don't give
away the case's plot (`SHE AGREED TO 600… THEN SHE OPENED HER BANK APP`). Before a
protection list that covers several segments, add a header that ties them together.

### 3. Give the safety step in the intro, even when it's promised later

- **Before:** `-BECAUSE WHAT COST THESE WOMEN THOUSANDS WAS ONE SENTENCE.`
- **After:** `-BEFORE YOU HAND OVER ANOTHER CARD… CHECK WHETHER THE FIRST ONE WAS ALREADY
  CHARGED.`

The "one move" payoff still landed later, by name. Ryan's notes say this too (put the
safety-critical step early). Here the draft held the step back to protect the
promise. **Rule:** A promised payoff can repeat a step the intro already gave. Never
withhold the step to keep the promise fresh.

### 4. Cut the investigation down to what the viewer needs

Cut from A:
- The CBS button `MAZAL GROUP. CBS REPORTS THAT'S THE COMPANY BEHIND THAT BEE & CO.`,
  plus the Hawaii settlement lines
- Clip 4 (NPR at headquarters, "contact our legal department") and its runway
- The Mazal lawyers' response
- The "no price tag" red flag, the dispute line, the state AG line
- Clip 5 (CBS4 Indy) and the online setup, which Jeff's video replaced

Cut from B: the whole Minnesota FTC complaint case (`…A CHARGE POPPED UP IN THEIR CARD
APP. FOR A PET SERVICE!!`).

**Rule:** Matt's standard is about two outside clips a segment. The draft ran five in
A. Each case or clip has to change what the viewer does or feels. Corporate structure
(who owns which brand, which company settled where) is reporting the viewer doesn't
need. Keep it in JEFF notes. An anonymous, second-hand case (an FTC complaint
obtained through a records request) loses its slot once there's a named viewer and a
guest. When a story has a guest, plan one case plus the viewer, not two cases.

### 5. The tease stays generic about the person and broad about the scale

| Draft | Final |
|---|---|
| `-THE NEXT TIME A CLERK SAYS YOUR CARD DECLINED…` | `-THE NEXT TIME AN EMPLOYEE SAYS YOUR CREDIT CARD DECLINED…` |
| `-DON'T HAND OVER ANOTHER ONE UNTIL…` | `-DON'T HAND OVER ANOTHER CARD UNTIL…` |
| `-AN 82-YEAR-OLD RETIRED TEACHER HANDS OVER HER CARD…` | `-A WOMAN HANDS OVER HER CARD…` |
| `-FROM CALIFORNIA TO NEW MEXICO… AND NOW ON FAKE SHOPPING SITES TOO!!` | `-IN STORES ACROSS AMERICA… AND NOW ON FAKE SHOPPING SITES TOO!!` |
| `10/28 WEDNESDAY — "YOUR CARD DECLINED" GOT HER CHARGED $53,000` | `10/28 WED` |

**Rule:** In the tease, use a word that fits every venue in the story. "Employee"
covers store and spa staff; "clerk" doesn't. Name the object ("credit card", not
"card", not "one"). Keep the person generic ("a woman"). Her age and job are body
details, so every viewer can see themselves in the open. Give scale as national
("across America"), not as a list of states. The top line is just the date and day.

### 6. Put the viewer in the scene over B-roll

- **Before:** `-LATER THAT DAY SHE CHECKED HER BANK APP. BOTH CARDS WERE CHARGED.`
- **After:** `-LATER YOU CHECK YOUR BANK APP AND BOTH CARDS ARE CHARGED.`

**Rule:** While Jeff talks over B-roll that shows no victim, switch to second person,
present tense. The pictures are of the place, so make the viewer the person standing
in it.

### 7. Drop hedges in black copy; keep them in notes

Cut: `SHE ASKED REPORTERS NOT TO USE HER NAME.` and `A COUPLE OF YEARS AGO,` (the
New Mexico woman), plus `ON STORES LIKE THESE` on the investigation line.

**Rule:** A line that exists only to protect the writer doesn't belong in black copy.
That includes anonymity explanations, date hedges and scope qualifiers, as long as
attribution (`SHE SAYS`) is already carrying the legal weight. Put the hedge in a JEFF
note. Exception: keep a date in black when leaving it out makes an old case sound
current *and* would change what a viewer does. Flag that as an open decision instead
of writing it in.

### 8. Don't label every takeaway

- **Before:** `-HERE'S YOUR RED FLAG: YOUR BANK FLAGS A CHARGE AS FRAUD?`
- **After:** `-WHEN YOUR BANK FLAGS A CHARGE AS FRAUD`
- **Before:** `-HERE'S YOUR RED FLAG: A "FREE" GIFT THAT WANTS A SHIPPING FEE ISN'T FREE.`
- **After:** `-A "FREE" GIFT THAT WANTS A SHIPPING FEE ISN'T FREE.`

The one that survived is the "one move" payoff: `"YOUR CARD DECLINED. GOT ANOTHER
ONE?" THAT'S YOUR RED FLAG.` **Rule:** Use "red flag" about once a story, on the
line that matters most. Everywhere else, just state the rule.

### 9. Name the scam in plain words, and let Jeff react

- Added: `-IT'S A CLASSIC PHISHING SCAM` (B, right before the clip). This sets up the
  guest's topic.
- Added: `-AND ITS ALL OVER THE MEDIA` on the open-enrollment line.
- `THE KIT IS "FREE"… YOU JUST PAY SHIPPING!!` went from a header to Jeff's line:
  `-THEY SAY THE KIT IS "FREE"… YOU JUST PAY SHIPPING!! HAH! OKAY`
- Added: `UNREAL!` after the 40-times markup.

**Rule:** Name the category the viewer already knows (phishing, imposter scam) in one
plain line, especially when a guest is booked to cover it. Write one or two reaction
beats per story (`UNREAL!`, `HAH! OKAY`), where Jeff's personality carries the line.

### 10. Viewer segments: name, recency, thanks, tip line

- **Before:** `-SHE'S 77. SHE SENT US THIS.`
- **After:** `-77 YEAR OLD DANNY SENT US THIS THIS WEEK.` … `- THANK YOU DANNY FOR SHARING
  YOUR STORY, VIEWERS CAN SHARE TIPS WITH US AT INFO@ROSSENMEDIA.COM`

**Rule:** A viewer segment uses the viewer's first name and says when they sent it
("this week"). It closes with Jeff thanking them by name, then the tip line:
**INFO@ROSSENMEDIA.COM**. Write the thank-you line with an `XXX` placeholder for the
name if the name isn't in yet.

### 11. The pre-sponsor tease talks to the viewer

- **Before:** `-NEXT… THE EMAIL THAT LOOKS LIKE YOUR DOCTOR'S PATIENT PORTAL. OFFERING A
  FREE MEDICARE KIT.` / `-A FORMER SECRET SERVICE AGENT ON HOW TO SPOT THESE EMAILS.`
- **After:** `-NEXT… YOUR DOCTOR'S [OFFICE] IS OFFERING A FREE MEDICARE KIT? THINK AGAIN` /
  `-A FORMER SECRET SERVICE AGENT IS HERE TO TELL YOU HOW TO SPOT THIS COMMON SCAM.`

**Rule:** Write the "next" lines as a question to the viewer, then a verb-led guest
line ("is here to tell you"). Use full sentences, not noun phrases.

### 12. Check outside B-roll for names, not just sound

Kyle's comment on the KOB 4 clip: *"Crop out her name since this is technically a
different case with the same store."* The draft marked the clip `PLAY MUTE` because
the sound was a different customer, but missed the lower-third name.

**Rule:** When B-roll comes from another outlet's story about a *different* victim,
mute it **and** add a red crop cue for any name, lower third or face on screen:
`(((EDIT - CROP LOWER THIRD: DIFFERENT CUSTOMER)))`. The grader's visual notes should
check for this.

### 13. Fewer notes, fewer cues

The draft had 27 JEFF notes; the final has 11. Every kept note guards a line that
still airs. The team also removed:
- the on-screen chat-question cue (this is a taped F2)
- the producer pre-interview cue above the guest questions
- the viewer-name producer cue, once it was answered

**Rule:** Aim for one note per fact that could get Jeff in trouble if he ad-libbed
past it, and no more. On a taped F2, skip the chat-question cue. Producer cues
should be questions only the producer can answer. Delete each one once it's
answered.
