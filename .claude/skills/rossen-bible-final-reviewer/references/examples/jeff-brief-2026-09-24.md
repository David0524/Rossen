Subject: F1 and F2 bibles for tomorrow — the 30-second version

Jeff,

Both bibles are in. Here's each show in 30 seconds.

**F1 — "Your phone is the target"** (live, with Jim Stickley)
- **A: The new-phone callback.** New iPhone lands, you activate it, twenty minutes later "your carrier" calls. They know your name, address, make and model. Shipping error, send it back, here's a prepaid label or a QR code, and a gift card for your trouble. The phone goes to the scammers. Clip: the Massachusetts woman (Xfinity), who got lucky because the carrier killed the phone in transit. Escalator: the Utah man who never ordered a phone, one showed up anyway, and the bill haunted him for months.
- **Jim's beats:** how they spoof the carrier's caller ID, where the order info comes from (dark web), then the one-line close: no carrier calls you to send a phone back. Hang up, dial the number on your bill.
- **B: Port-out fraud.** Scammers call your carrier pretending to be you, move your number to their phone, and every bank text and security code goes to them. Clip is a victim's account of the takeover. Jim explains why it beats bank safeguards, and the FCC line about scraping social media for the security-question answers.
- **Close is the 5-minute fix, on screen for all three carriers:** AT&T "Wireless Account Lock," Verizon "Number Lock," T-Mobile "Port Out Protection." Screen-record videos are built for each.
- OmniWatch spot sits between the A and the tips, then deals with DealSeek.
- **Why it works:** it's new-iPhone week, so the box is literally on the porch. And B is the version nobody's heard of that costs the most.

**F2 — Car scams** (tape, with Lauren Fix, The Car Coach)
- **A: Selling your car? The buyer pours oil in your engine.** Two or three "buyers" show up, one distracts you with questions or a wad of cash, the other pours oil where it doesn't belong. Test drive, smoke, "your engine's shot," lowball. Clip 1 is a Victorville seller's own dashcam catching it. Clip 2 is the California father-son arrest. Lauren explains why it smokes so fast, the biggest red flag, and how to tell a real problem from a planted one. Tips: check IDs when they arrive, nobody near the car unsupervised, bring a friend, walk away if it feels off.
- OmniWatch spot, then:
- **B: The dealership doesn't exist.** AI-cloned dealer sites, real reviews, real inventory, and sometimes the car in the ad is real, the scammers just don't own it. Two FTC alerts this month, classics and muscle cars hit hardest. Clip 3 is a victim story, clip 4 is the Car Dealer Magazine reporter walking a clone site. Lauren gives the red flags. Tips: never wire a deposit, never pay for a car you haven't seen, hire an inspector if it's out of state.
- **C: A ticket in the mail for a car you've never seen.** Plate cloning. They find a car that looks like theirs, copy your plate, run red lights on camera, and the ticket comes to you. Clip 5 is the San Antonio Purple Heart veteran whose cloned plates were tied to crimes. Close: don't post your plate on social media, and if a ticket arrives from somewhere you've never been, call police immediately.
- **Why it works:** we did the buyer-side used-car scams in July (clone VINs, dashboard tape). This is the flip, and C is the one nobody sees coming.
