# Notes: F1 09/16 Kroger livestream script

Each note is anchored to the script line to comment on. Paste the text under **Comment**.

## Cold open (page 1)

**Anchor:** "-THE SHELF SAID 3 DOLLARS AND NINETY-NINE CENTS"
**Comment:** Great concrete open, keep it. But Jeff always follows the slate with a command aimed at "you" in the first 15 seconds ("Do not use tap-to-pay until you watch this"). Add one line before the price: "Before you go grocery shopping today, before you swipe your card at Kroger, I need you to see this." Also name Kroger here. Right now the tease says "the shelf" and "the register" and Kroger doesn't land until line 4.

**Anchor:** "-IT'S NOT A FLUKE. IT HAPPENS ALL THE TIME."
**Comment:** This is his rhythm exactly. Keep. Consider his "big deal" pre-empt right after it, because $2.08 on one item sounds small: "And before you're like, 'Two bucks, big deal.' Oh, it's a big deal. Do that three times a week, every week. That's hundreds of dollars a year coming out of your pocket." He does this move in almost every open (address-theft show: "And before you're like, 'Big deal.' Oh, it's a big deal.").

**Anchor:** "-OVER ONE IN THREE KROGER GROCERY STORES CAUGHT MAKING PRICING MISTAKES / -USUALLY BY OVERCHARGING CUSTOMERS"
**Comment:** Two things. "Customers" should be "you" (he runs 4.5 "you/your" per 100 words, almost never "customers"). And he repeats the killer number: "One in three. One in three Kroger stores." Suggest: "More than one in three Kroger stores caught charging the wrong price. One in three. And usually the mistake is in their favor, not yours."

**Anchor:** "-WE CAN PROVE IT / -THANKS TO NEW GOVERNMENT RECORDS JUST MADE PUBLIC"
**Comment:** Fine, but the open is missing two things every Jeff open has: the "caught on tape" promise (we have three clips, say so: "and it's caught on camera") and a human victim tease. We don't have a dollar-figure victim in this script, so use the beef clip: "You're going to see what happened when one shopper found two different prices on the same package of beef." He never opens without a person.

**Anchor:** "-AND THE SECRET TIP TO GETTING YOUR MONEY BACK AT KROGERS"
**Comment:** Spelling consistency: script uses both "KROGER" and "KROGERS." Pick one (he'd say "Kroger"). Bigger issue: this tease promises a secret, and the tip on page 11 is "go to customer service." That's not a secret. Confirm whether Kroger's scan-accuracy guarantee still applies in Ohio divisions (historically some Kroger divisions give you the item free, or the first one free, when it rings up higher than the shelf). If that's real and current, THAT is the secret tip and it should be teased here and paid off on page 11. If it isn't, soften the tease to "and exactly how to get your money back."

You want a Dyson vacuum? Tell us. You want a pet bed? Tell us. Even give us the brands. We have deal hunters standing by and they'll find it live." Give him the examples on the page.

**Anchor:** "-SO JOIN THE CHAT. / -HIT LIKE AND SUBSCRIBE"
**Comment:** He always attaches the why and the community line: "Really helps the algorithm, pushes our message out to more people. We're independent, no corporate overlords. Let us know where you're watching from." And the launch line is missing: "All right, let's get right to it." He says it every show before the lead story.

## Story open (page 3)

**Anchor:** "-YOUR GROCERY STORE IS OVERCHRAGING YOU / -ON PURPOSE"
**Comment:** Typo OVERCHRAGING. Bigger: "on purpose" is the strongest claim in the script and the evidence on this page is inspection failures the script itself calls "pricing mistakes," plus employee complaints about understaffing. The chat will call this out and Kroger would too. Jeff is blunt, but he hedges by attribution, not by softening: "Your grocery store is overcharging you. All the time. And Kroger's own employees say the company has known about it for years." Save "on purpose" for page 8, where dynamic pricing actually is on purpose.

**Anchor:** "-THIS IS NOT SPECULATION / -WE KNOW IT'S TRUE"
**Comment:** His version of this beat: "And if you think I'm being far-fetched, this is in the government's own records." Or "I'm not joking around here."

**Anchor:** "-OHIO STATE GOVERNMENT RECORDS THAT WERE JUST MADE PUBLIC / -REVEAL THE RESULTS OF NEARLY 400 GROCERY STORE INSPECTIONS BY THE GOVERNMENT"
**Comment:** Scope problem. The cold open says "one in three Kroger stores" with no qualifier, then the body reveals it's Ohio only. He needs to own that up top or here: "We got the records for one state, Ohio, nearly 400 inspections. If it's happening there, it's happening where you shop." Also "government" twice in one sentence. Name the actual inspector (county auditors' weights and measures divisions?) because he pays it off on page 11 when he tells people where to complain.

**Anchor:** "-15 PERCENT OF KROGER STORES FAILED THEIR INSPECTIONS / -MORE THAN ONE IN THREE STORES SHOWED DISCREPANCIES IN PRICING / -IN ONE OHIO COUNTY, THAT NUMBER WAS OVER HALF"
**Comment:** Three stats in a row with no button and no explanation of why 15% failed but 33% had errors. He'd want one line: "You're allowed a few mistakes and still pass. These stores had so many they failed." "Discrepancies in pricing" is not his word, use "charged the wrong price." Name the county and make it a "you" line: "If you shop in ___ County, more than half the Kroger stores had it wrong." And the open promised "usually by overcharging." The body never says what share of errors were overcharges. If the records have that number, it's the most important one in the show. Put it here.

## Page 4

**Anchor:** "-THIS IS OUTRAGEOUS. AND IT'S NOT A NEW PROBLEM"
**Comment:** "Outrageous" is zero-for-five shows. He says "This is crazy" or "Here's what makes me angry." And his escalator is "And it gets worse. Kroger's done this before."

**Anchor:** "-KROGER ACTUALLY JUST SETTLED A LAWSUIT OVER ITS PHARMACY PRICES"
**Comment:** He never states a settlement without the dollar amount and the when. Add both. Also flag that pharmacy pricing is a different issue than shelf tags, so keep it to one line as "pattern," which is how it's written.

**Anchor:** "-AND COMPARE THEM TO ACTUAL RECEIPTS"
**Comment:** Tense: COMPARED.

**Anchor:** "-THEY CAUGHT THE STORE OVERCHARGING ON EVERYTHING FROM CHEERIOS TO BONELESS BEEF TO DOG FOOD / -JUST WATCH"
**Comment:** The list of three is perfect, that's his shape. But he doesn't say "just watch." His clip cue is two beats, a cue and a verdict: "Watch this. This is crazy." Or "Roll clip one."

## Page 5

**Anchor:** "-WE KNOW WHY THIS IS HAPPENING. KROGER EMPLOYEES TOLD US."
**Comment:** Accuracy: "told us" means Rossen Reports interviewed Kroger employees. The next line says the employees complained to Consumer Reports. If we didn't talk to them, this is a misattribution he'll have to walk back live. His version: "Kroger's own employees blew the whistle."

**Anchor:** "-CLAIMING KROGERS KNEW ABOUT "WIDESPREAD ERRORS" ON PRICE TAGS FOR YEARS"
**Comment:** He flags quotes out loud so the audience knows it isn't his word: "and I'm quoting here, 'widespread errors.'" Write the flag in.

**Anchor:** "-THEY SAID THE CHAIN'S RECENT LAYOFFS WERE RESPONSIBLE FOR THE MISTAKES"
**Comment:** Who is "they," the employees or Consumer Reports? Ambiguous on the page. Also confirm "layoffs" vs. "staffing cuts" / "reduced hours," which is how the CR reporting described it. Different word, different legal exposure.

**Anchor:** "-BECAUSE SHRINKING STAFF SIZES MEANT FEWER EMPLOYEES / -WITH LESS TIME TO MONITOR PRICES IN EVERY AISLE / -AND REMOVE OUT-OF-DATE SALES STICKERS"
**Comment:** "Shrinking staff sizes meant fewer employees" says the same thing twice. He'd do this in fragments: "Fewer people on the floor. Nobody pulling the old sale tags. So the shelf says sale. The register says full price." That last pair is the whole story in nine words.

**Anchor:** "-LOOK WHAT HAPPENED WHEN THIS PERSON FOUND BEEF WITH CONTRADICTORY PRICES DUE TO A SLOPPY STICKER JOB:"
**Comment:** "Contradictory prices due to a sloppy sticker job" is not his register. "Watch what happened when this shopper found two different prices on the same package of beef. This is crazy."

## Page 6 to 7

**Anchor:** "[01:17-01:37] (END: THIS IS CRAZY]"
**Comment:** The script goes straight from this clip into the next bold header. He never does that. Every clip gets a one-line button before he moves on ("Yeah, homegirl, let's do better. Amazon, come on."). Give him a beat here: "Two pounds on the sticker. One pound on the scale. Same price. That's not a computer glitch, that's somebody not doing their job."

**Anchor:** "-EVEN THOUGH IT HIRED 15K NEW EMPLOYEES AFTER THE BAD PR IN 2025"
**Comment:** Write out "fifteen thousand." "After the bad PR" is inside-baseball; he'd say "after they got caught." Bigger: the header says "Kroger never fixed the problem," but the Ohio records run 2022 to 2026. If most of the failures are from before the 2025 hiring push, "never fixed" isn't supported. Confirm the records show failures after the hires. If they do, say it: "These inspections are from THIS year."

**Anchor:** "-NOW, A QUICK WORD FROM OUR SPONSOR / (OMNIWATCH)"
**Comment:** Two notes. (1) He never enters a sponsor cold. He teases what's next, then breaks: "Coming up, the reason Kroger's prices are changing on you in the first place, and the secret tip to get your money back. But first, a quick word from our sponsor." (2) This lands about 5 minutes into the show. In the last five livestreams the first sponsor read came 13 to 20 minutes in, after the lead story was done. And OmniWatch is a data/identity product. The natural bridge is the loyalty-data section on pages 9 and 10 ("Kroger is building a secret profile on you"), not here. Suggest moving this break to after page 10, and putting Chapter here if we need two breaks.

## Page 8

**Anchor:** "-SO WHY ARE PRICES CHANGING SO MUCH AT THESE STORES IN THE FIRST PLACE?"
**Comment:** The connective tissue is missing. The first half was about tags not matching the register. This question assumes we've established that prices are changing a lot, and we haven't. One bridge line fixes it and it's where "on purpose" belongs: "And here's the part nobody's talking about. Part of the reason the tags can't keep up? Kroger is changing prices more than ever. On purpose. It's called dynamic pricing."

**Anchor:** "-THESE ARE STRATEGIC PRICE FLUCTUATIONS THAT THE STORE MAKES / -TO MAXIMIZE PROFITS BY ADJUSTING PRICES / -BASED ON THINGS LIKE AMOUNT OF FOOT TRAFFIC IN THE STORE"
**Comment:** "Strategic price fluctuations" is press-release language. He explains mechanics with a scenario the viewer can picture: "Store's packed on Saturday? Price goes up. Store's dead at 9 pm on a Tuesday? Price goes down. Same gallon of milk. Different price. Depending on when you walk in. And those digital shelf tags let them do it in seconds."

**Anchor:** "-HERE'S HOW IT WORKS:" (Local 12 clip)
**Comment:** Accuracy flag. The clip is about a group launching an ad campaign over dynamic pricing "concerns," and Kroger has publicly said it does not do surge pricing. As written, the script asserts dynamic pricing as a thing Kroger is doing. The chat will bring up the denial. His way through this is attribution plus the receipts: "Kroger says it doesn't do surge pricing. But they've put the digital tags in, and here's what critics say those tags are for." Producers should confirm Kroger's current statement and whether we asked them for comment on this show. He always says "we reached out to Kroger" or "Kroger didn't get back to us." Neither is in the script.

**Anchor:** (end of page 10, before "SO WHAT CAN YOU DO?")

## Tips (page 11 to 12)

**Anchor:** "-HERE'S EXACTLY HOW YOU AVOID BEING OVERCHARGED:"
**Comment:** Good setup line. Number the tips. He said "number one / number two" 29 times across five shows and never gives an unnumbered list.

**Anchor:** "-TAKE A PHOTO OF ANYTHING YOU BUY ON SALE / -COMPARE THE PHOTOGRAPH TO THE PRICE THAT SHOWS UP AT THE REGISTER"
**Comment:** "Photograph" should be "photo." Each tip is a command plus a reason for him: "Number one. Phone out. Take a photo of the shelf tag on anything you buy on sale. Because once you've walked out that door, it's your word against theirs."

**Anchor:** "-UNDER KROGER'S "MAKE IT RIGHT" POLICY", EMPLOYEES CAN FIX MISTAKES ON THE SPOT"
**Comment:** Stray quotation mark after POLICY. See the page 1 note: confirm whether the scan guarantee (item free if it rings up wrong) applies here. That's the "secret tip" the open promised, and it's the line the chat will screenshot.

**Anchor:** "-BUT IF YOU'VE ALREADY LEFT THE STORE, CALL 1-800-KROGERS TO REPORT IT"
**Comment:** He calls the number to screen and repeats it: "1-800-KROGERS. There it is on the screen. 1-800-KROGERS." Make sure graphics has it.

**Anchor:** "-IF KROGER DOES NOT MAKE IT RIGHT, SUBMIT A COMPLAINT TO YOUR STATE OR COUNTY'S WEIGHTS AND MEASURES CONSUMER PROTECTION OFFICE,"
**Comment:** This is a mouthful and it's the one nobody knows, so he'd frame it that way: "Number three, and this is the one nobody knows about. Google your county plus 'weights and measures.' That's the office that did these inspections. They will go into that store." Then his standard why: "And the reason it matters to report it is that's how these records get made, and that's how we find out about it." Needs a lower-third.

**Anchor:** "-DATE AND AMOUNT OF OVERCHARGE IN YOUR COMPPLAINT"
**Comment:** Typo COMPPLAINT. And a callback he'd want: "That photo you took in tip number one? That's your evidence."

**Anchor:** "-AND NOW A WORD FROM OUR SPONSOR / -(CHAPTER)"
**Comment:** Same as before, never cold. Tease first: "Coming up, the best Amazon deals of the week, secret promo codes, and your live requests. But first, a quick word from our sponsor."
