# Templates: recap, guest prep, deals talking points, producer brief

Less frequent than the pitch email, but each has a shape the team is used to.

## Meeting recap to Jeff and Ryan

```
Subject: Recap: [meeting] [date]

[Decisions, in prose, one short paragraph per topic.]

STILL OPEN
- [Item, framed as your recommendation, with who decides.]

DELIVERABLES
Matt: ...
Ryan: ...
Jeff: ...

ALREADY IN MOTION
- ...

Let me know if any of this doesn't match your notes, happy to adjust!
```

Before sending, check anything that came from an automated transcript: names, the spelling of "Rossen," episode titles.

## Prep agenda for an outside guest's team

```
Subject: Prep for [guest] on the [date] [topic] show

1. The episode. What it is, when it tapes or goes live, how long their part runs.
2. Questions [guest] should be ready for. About ten. Flag the one that may be uncomfortable.
3. Facts to align on. Every number they plan to say, with its source.
4. What we need from you by [date]. Bio and exact title for the lower third, facts with sources, a real client story if they have one, availability for a 15-minute tech check the day before.
5. Logistics. Link, call time, what to wear, where to look.
```

Guidance for guests: answers of 20 to 30 seconds, plain language, no unexplained acronyms. If the guest works for a sponsor, this agenda covers the editorial segment only, and Jeff discloses the relationship on air.

## Deals talking points for Jeff

For when the deals guest can't make it and Jeff runs the segment alone. Three short bullets per product, written to be spoken.

```
6. Logitech C270 Webcam — Logitech charges $21.99 → $16.89 (Price Drop, no code)
- Cheaper than Logitech sells it on their own website.
- Been on Amazon since 2011. $16.89 is the lowest it ever goes. Don't wait for better.
- The camera for Zoom with the grandkids or telehealth with the doctor.
```

Rules: the header carries the math. Verify the "was" price against the live listing, not the deal sheet. Say when a price is Subscribe and Save. Put backups last. Remind Jeff to say the collection link twice.

## Producer brief for a call-in episode

```
[Warm open: what's decided, and that they own it.]

What the show is. [Audio-only callers, taped Friday, two stories plus an expert, one spare.]

The database. [Which tab is hers. Dial the caller ID number. Check the flags first.]

This episode. [Caller IDs and a two-line summary of each story.]

What I need from you this week.
1. Pre-interview both callers. Confirm the story matches the voicemail.
2. Set expectations: we can't get their money back and won't promise to.
3. Collect documents: checks, wires, police reports, letters, screenshots.
4. Pick a backup story scored 7 or higher.
5. Send availability for the Friday taping.

What's off your plate. [Name it.]

[Offer a call.]
```

## Bible assignment: the seven beats

When assigning a live scam show, give the beats in this order. It's the Ghost Tapping skeleton. Details are in `voice/bible-format.md`.

1. Cold open headlines, with a suggested first line.
2. Mechanics beat, with any correction to how the scam is usually described.
3. Victim clip, then our guest.
4. Sponsor read placement: after the victim's story, not inside it.
5. Escalation beat.
6. Expert, demo, and four questions.
7. Practical close.

Then: sources, all linked. Title directions for Ryan. A thumbnail idea. The deadline.
