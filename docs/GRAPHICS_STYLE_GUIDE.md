# Rossen Reports — Broadcast Graphics Style Guide 2.0

Supplied by the producer, 10/29/2026. Use for every ChatGPT image prompt (CLAUDE.md, standing notes).

**Creative direction: modern consumer journalism.** Clean, sophisticated broadcast graphics: authoritative,
trustworthy, contemporary, approachable. Less decoration. More information. Stronger impact. Graphics often
run in a smaller part of the screen, so they must read when scaled down. Premium network news, not sports,
not a corporate deck, not an elaborate infographic.

## 1. Color palette
- Deep navy #061A42 — primary background (about 75–85% of the composition)
- Cobalt blue #0755C8 — secondary background, restrained accents
- Electric yellow #FFF000 — the most important information only; no large yellow blocks unless a major finding or warning
- White #FFFFFF — primary typography, carries most information
- Bright blue #23A8FF — subtle dividers and secondary details
- Gradients sparing and smooth. No excessive glow, bright outlines or competing blues.

## 2. Background and composition
Clean, rich navy with subtle cobalt depth; texture nearly invisible. A restrained diagonal or angled yellow accent
may reference the Rossen identity. Prioritize alignment, generous negative space, consistent margins, simple
hierarchy, balance, minimal decoration. Avoid: stacked trapezoid panels, heavy outlines around rows, multiple
diagonal stripes, oversized number badges, excessive dividers, competing geometry, decoration that doesn't inform.

## 3. Typography
Modern, bold, condensed sans-serif (DIN Condensed, Trade Gothic, Archivo Narrow). Typography is the primary visual.
Headlines: large, bold, white, all caps, short, consistently placed, never crowding the edges. Supporting text:
bold/semibold white, slightly smaller, consistent size, generous line spacing. Yellow for one critical word,
figure or takeaway; never several unrelated highlights. No extreme compression, tiny labels or type variety.

## 4. Iconography
Simple, modern, flat; consistent line weight; geometric; white or yellow; uniform size and alignment; readable
small. One icon per item when it helps; none when type alone works. No illustrations, detailed pictograms,
decorative symbols or mixed styles.

## 5. Formats
- **Explainer** (lists, benefits, tips, instructions): headline at top; 3–5 concise rows; small consistent icons
  if useful; left-aligned; generous spacing; minimal or no dividers; no numbering unless the sequence matters.
- **Reveal** (comparisons, hidden costs, savings, findings): one headline; one dominant figure or comparison;
  minimal support; yellow on the key number; strong negative space.
- **Warning** (scams, traps, urgent alerts): one commanding headline; one prominent warning or action; limited
  support; yellow for urgency; red only for genuine danger. No sensationalism.

## 6. Signature identity
One short, angled electric-yellow accent integrated into the headline area, on every graphic; not repeated per row.
Identity comes mainly from consistent type, color, spacing and composition.

## 7. Smaller on-screen display
Legible at 50–65% size: recognizable headlines, no small text, one idea per row, high contrast, generous space,
no thin details. Don't fill space just because it's there.

## 8. Production
16:9, 1920×1080, TV-safe margins, crisp edges. No unnecessary logos, photos, people or watermarks. No invented
facts, stats or prices. Preserve supplied wording; exact spelling and punctuation. Consistent across the package.

## 9. Standard
CLEAN. MODERN. CREDIBLE. DISTINCTLY ROSSEN. The viewer gets the takeaway in about two seconds. If an element
doesn't improve clarity, hierarchy or brand recognition, remove it.
