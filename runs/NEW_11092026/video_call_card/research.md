# Research: "Don't Let Your Grandkids Show THIS on a Video Call"

Run: NEW_11092026 / video_call_card. Researched 2026-11-10. This is a research file, not the outline.

## Bottom line: the story is thin
- **There is only one documented case of this exact scam, and it was an attempt.** A stranger on a video call asked children to fetch and show a parent's card. The only source is the Hyde County (NC) Sheriff's Office release of Sept. 29, 2026, reprinted by WCTI 12 and The Outer Banks Voice. **No loss is reported. Nobody is named or quoted, and there is no video.** The release does not say whether a card was actually shown.
- No second US agency has issued a warning about the video-call version, and the FTC and FBI have no advisory on it.
- **The closest documented case is from 2018, but it was not a video call.** In Lakeville, MN, an 8-year-old sent photos of his mom's credit card and driver's license to a stranger. The stranger reached him through Fortnite chat, calls and texts and promised V-Bucks. His mother told her story on camera to KARE 11. No dollar loss was reported.
- **Supporting cases:**
  - Claire Sweeney, UK actor, Dec. 2022: someone in Roblox chat asked her 7-year-old to "go to my purse, take out my credit card and read the numbers." No loss.
  - Sharjah Police, UAE, Nov. 2025: "some children have shared front-and-back images of bank cards."
  - Action Fraud, UK, 2018: Fortnite scams that ended in fraudulent charges.
- **What would make it stand up:**
  1. Reach the Hyde County Sheriff's Office for an on-camera interview or a phone quote. Ask whether a card was shown and whether any money was lost, and ask for the sheriff's Facebook post or graphic.
  2. Find one US family who lost money after a child showed or read out a card. A producer callout on social media is the realistic route; I found none in 2024–2026 coverage.
  3. Get a bank or issuer fraud-team quote on how a card front and back (number, expiration date, CVV) is enough for online fraud.
- **Recommended framing:** "Sheriff's warning + it has happened before (Lakeville) + what to do." Do not say that "families are losing money" to this video-call scam; no source supports that.

## 2. Other cases and warnings

### US: same shape (a child gets or shows a parent's card for a stranger)

**1. Lakeville, MN, 2018 (Fortnite).**
- **What happened:** 8-year-old Charlie sent photos of his mother Krista Kneeland-Pearson's driver's license and credit cards to a stranger. The stranger contacted him through Fortnite in-game messages, then phone calls and texts, and offered V-Bucks. Newsweek says the stranger "specifically wanted her Visa." She found her purses on his bedroom floor.

- **Quotes** (as relayed by Newsweek from KHOU): "It makes me really nervous" and "What is going to happen with all of my information?"

| 5 | Require purchase approval on the child's device (Google Play: all content, paid content or in-app purchases). | https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/7039872 |

### Do-nothing sanity check (what happens if a card was exposed and you do nothing)
Source: FTC lost/stolen cards page.

**Credit card: your liability is low either way.**
- If "your account number is used but your card isn't lost or stolen," then "You aren't responsible for any charges you didn't authorize."
- Even if you do nothing up front, you are protected as long as you dispute the charges when they show up. The real cost is hassle, a replacement card and missed automatic payments.

**Debit card: doing nothing is genuinely risky, because it's your checking account.** Your liability depends on how fast you report:
- $0 before any unauthorized use.
- $50 within 2 business days of learning of it.
- $500 after that, but within 60 days of the statement.
- After 60 days, "All the money taken from your ATM/debit card account, and possibly more."
- **The FTC page does not clearly tie the number-only scenario to a specific debit rule.** Have the reviewer confirm the Reg E treatment of number-only debit fraud before stating it on air.

**On-air takeaway:** "Credit card? Call the bank, you're covered. Debit card? Call today, because the clock is on your own money."
- Also say: the exposed number can be sold or reused for months, so a reissued card ends the risk, while watching statements only catches it after the fact.
- **This is a reasoned inference, not a sourced claim.**
