## HERE'S HOW TO PROTECT YOURSELF
`A5 · the fix`

1. **LOOK AT THE AMOUNT BEFORE YOU TAP** (the one thing from the tease)
   - Every single time, on any reader or phone. If they cover the screen, don't tap.
   - Keep your card or your phone in your own hand. Never hand it over.
   - **CLIP 3 (HORIZONTAL):** [Good Morning America — Tap-to-pay scam: How to protect yourself](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dwgCZVD7U00) · 2:09
     - **0:29–0:42 ("SO, CHECK THE TOTAL")**
     - *Soundbite: "When someone tries to sell you an item, they may charge you way more than the actual item costs. And because you're not signing a receipt and getting your card back, you may not check the total. So, check the total."*
2. **"WE DON'T TAKE CASH"? WALK AWAY**
   - That's the red flag. A real collection on the street will take your $10 bill.
   - Want to give? Go to the charity's own website, or look it up at Give.org.
3. **USE A CREDIT CARD, NOT YOUR DEBIT CARD**
   - With credit, it's the bank's money while you fight. With debit, it's your cash out of your checking account.
4. **TURN ON ALERTS FOR EVERY CHARGE**
   - Set your bank or card app to alert you on every transaction, no minimum, so you see it in seconds.
5. **IF IT HAPPENS, DISPUTE IT RIGHT AWAY AND DON'T TAKE NO**
   - Call your bank the same day, then put the dispute in writing. You have about two months from your statement, but don't wait.
   - The law covers a charge in the wrong amount. If they say no, push back, and file a complaint with the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau.

## The fix: sources

- Step 1: check the amount, don't let them cover the screen, keep it in your hand ([BBB "ghost tapping" alert, via FOX 26 Houston](https://www.fox26houston.com/news/bbb-warning-consumers-tap-to-pay-scams-ghost-tapping); [NBC 5 Chicago, Jul 22, 2026](https://www.nbcchicago.com/consumer/caught-on-camera-woman-records-possible-donation-scam-that-left-chicago-residents-out-thousands/3964946/); [GMA clip](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dwgCZVD7U00); Jim Stickley on [Rossen Reports 9/2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jmUfTCWWDVM), 11:04–11:44).
- Step 2: "no cash" is the red flag; give through the charity's own site or [Give.org](https://give.org) ([CBS Chicago](https://www.cbsnews.com/chicago/news/tap-to-pay-scam-victim-lost-thousands/); [Seattle PD via FOX 13, May 22, 2026](https://www.fox13seattle.com/news/tourists-digital-payment-scam); Shaler Twp, PA police via Moneywise, Mar 2026; [FTC: how to donate wisely](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-donate-wisely-and-avoid-charity-scams)).
- Step 3: credit over debit (FTC charity guidance; [Fair Credit Billing Act, 15 U.S.C. 1666](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1666), a charge "not in the amount reflected"; [FTC: disputing credit card charges, $50 cap](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/using-credit-cards-and-disputing-charges)).
- Step 4: alerts on every transaction ([BBB alert](https://www.fox26houston.com/news/bbb-warning-consumers-tap-to-pay-scams-ghost-tapping); [GMA clip](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dwgCZVD7U00) 1:24–1:35).
- Step 5: dispute within 60 days of the statement ([Reg Z 12 CFR 1026.13](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/13/) for credit; [Reg E 12 CFR 1005.11](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1005/11/), "incorrect electronic fund transfer," for debit; [CFPB EFT FAQ](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/compliance-resources/deposit-accounts-resources/electronic-fund-transfers/electronic-fund-transfers-faqs/) on "fraudulently induced"); [CFPB complaint](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/complaint/). Legal or TJ still has to sign off on the dispute wording (Open calls).
- Do-nothing test: a tap on the 10th, statement sent on the 1st of next month, gives a deadline around the end of the month after (about 80 days); someone who never opens statements can miss it. Hence "call the day you see it."
