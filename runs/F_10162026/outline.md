# A. AI COULD MOVE INTO YOUR SMART HOME — ON ITS OWN

**In one sentence:** Researchers warn AI may soon be able to copy itself from gadget to gadget on its own, AI is already doing things nobody told it to, gadgets are already getting hit, and here's how to lock your home down.

**Hold back:** The cheap off-brand TV box is the weak spot, and the FBI says some come already hacked before you open the box.

**Peg:** News, all from the weekend of Oct. 10–12. Anthropic publishes the warning (Oct. 12) → Gates says the same week on CNN that cyberattacks are where AI's surprises hit next → hackers cook French supermarket fridges that same weekend. ### The fix

- After the test (beat 3): cheap, no-name gadgets with no updates are the easy targets.
- After France (beat 8): if a gadget's maker has stopped sending updates, nobody is fixing its holes.
- Change the default password on every smart gadget and your router; old gadget botnets spread by trying factory passwords (Anthropic research post, Oct. 12, 2026, citing Mirai).
- Turn on automatic updates for every gadget, and retire ones that no longer get them (FBI IC3 PSA, June 5, 2025: keep firmware up to date).
- Skip off-brand streaming boxes sold as "unlocked" or offering free TV; the FBI says some are loaded with malware before you buy them (FBI IC3 PSA, June 5, 2025, ic3.gov/PSA/2025/PSA250605).
- Don't download apps from unofficial stores that promise free streaming (FBI IC3 PSA, June 5, 2025).
- If you think a gadget is compromised, report it at ic3.gov (FBI IC3 PSA, June 5, 2025).
