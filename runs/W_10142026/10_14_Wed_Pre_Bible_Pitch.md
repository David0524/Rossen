# 1. THIS FREE SAMPLE COST HER $26,000

**Slot:** F2 A (approved) · **Confidence: HIGH** · *Fresh peg is the CBS LA closure; the $26K widow is a 2025 case*

**SLOT FIT:** F2 A. It carries it: a scam with a villain, a widow on Social Security with a dollar figure, cases in five states, and three CBS LA packages over 130K each. The store closure on Sept 24 is the news.

> 🔥 **VIRAL CLIP** — YouTube, CBS LA: 444,022 views, uploaded Sept 23 · 338,485, uploaded Aug 27 · 131,585, uploaded Oct 1 (all observed Oct 6)
> https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I_AnpuMDlls · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6OGQNCnZTfE · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g9e4ElDgYG8
> Appears to show: Bee & Co. customers and families describing pressure sales, then the boarded-up Topanga store (from titles and descriptions).
> *Not watched — no timecode, no outcue, no confirmation of what's on screen.*

### WORKING TEASE

> THEY HAND YOU A FREE SAMPLE AT THE MALL.
> "COME IN, LET ME SHOW YOU." TEN MINUTES, TOPS.
> THIS 84-YEAR-OLD WIDOW WENT IN FOR A 25-DOLLAR FACIAL… AND WALKED OUT 26 THOUSAND DOLLARS POORER.
> SHE LIVES ON SOCIAL SECURITY. THE STORE SAID: NO REFUNDS.
> IT'S HAPPENING IN MALLS COAST TO COAST. AND ONE STORE JUST GOT SHUT DOWN.
> HOW TO WALK PAST THAT KIOSK… AND WHAT TO DO IF YOU ALREADY SIGNED.

### THE HOOK

Kathryn Taylor, 84, a widow in Fernandina Beach, Florida, went into a Bee and Co skincare store for a $25 facial. Her receipt shows about $26,000: $9,000 for "Genesis Z," $5,500 for "Sapphire X," $10,000 for a "Phyto Thermal Collection." The store refunded about $4,500 and refused the rest (First Coast News, June 2025). This fall, CBS California Investigates reported more cases at Bee & Co. stores in Southern California: a man with an intellectual disability who spent more than $50,000, and an 80-year-old woman who says she lost nearly $3,000. On Sept 24 the Westfield Topanga store went dark. The same playbook has turned up in Arizona ($25,000, age 79) and Washington (a 75-year-old pushed to withdraw $50,000).

### HOW IT WORKS

- **The lure:** a free sample or a cheap facial. Then the chair: "I probably said no 50 to 60 times" (Arizona victim, ABC15).
- **The paper:** prices you can't see and a "no refunds" form signed in the chair.
- **Extended threat:** the bill posts after you're home, and the store says no refunds.

### FOOTAGE WE NEED

- **The get: CBS LA, three packages** (444K, 338K, 132K), plus the closure.
- First Coast News 47K (Taylor's receipt); ABC15 Phoenix; KOIN 6 Vancouver.

### THE PAYOFF

- **Never sit down for a "free" demo.** Take the sample and keep walking.
- Ask for the total in writing **before** you hand over a card. Never sign a "final sale" form you can't read.
- Already charged? Dispute it with your card company that day, and report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. *(verify)*

### THE HOOK

Robert Rowland, 75, of Conway, South Carolina, had a caregiver from a home-care agency. In April 2024, records show, Sarah Smalls became his power of attorney, and a quitclaim deed moved his home to her for $5. When his daughter Nora couldn't reach him, she drove over: the locks were changed, and the caregiver answered the Ring doorbell to say he had died three days earlier. - **File one page:** a $5 quitclaim deed. The deeds office checks the form, not whether it's fair (Horry County, as reported).
- **Extended threat:** the family finds out after the death, and police side with the paperwork.

# 3. IF YOU SEE THIS VIDEO ABOUT YOUR PROPERTY TAXES, IT'S FAKE

**Slot:** F2 C (recommended) · **Confidence: MEDIUM** · *Real warnings in four states; the "Oct 15 deadline" isn't sourced*

**SLOT FIT:** F2 C. A scam aimed straight at 55+ homeowners, with official warnings from Wisconsin, Ohio, California and Colorado. Thin footage. ⚠️ The Medicare show (live Oct 9) is a benefits-season government-impostor neighbor; this one is local tax, so it's a mild overlap only.

### WORKING TEASE

> IT LOOKS LIKE THE NEWS. IT SOUNDS LIKE THE GOVERNMENT.
> "CONGRESS JUST FROZE PROPERTY TAXES FOR SENIORS. FILE THIS FORM."
> THE FORM DOESN'T EXIST. THE LAW DOESN'T EXIST. THE VIDEO WAS MADE BY A-I.
> AND THE PHONE NUMBER GOES STRAIGHT TO A SCAMMER WHO WANTS YOUR SOCIAL SECURITY NUMBER.

### THE HOOK

An AI-generated video tells older homeowners that Congress passed a law letting seniors freeze or cut their property taxes, and to request a "Senior Homeowner Tax Review Request" or "IRS Form 1098-SR." Neither exists. The contact information doesn't reach a county assessor; it reaches scammers who ask for Social Security numbers, bank details, or a filing fee. Wisconsin's Department of Revenue, Ohio's Franklin and Hamilton county auditors, three California assessors and Colorado's Douglas County assessor have all warned about it.

### HOW IT WORKS

- **A fake official video** with real seals and government language.
- **A form name that sounds real.**
- **The ask:** your SSN, your bank account, or a fee to "file for you."

### FOOTAGE — BE HONEST ABOUT THIS

Local warnings exist (Denver7, WSYX, WXOW, KERO) but each has under 1,000 views. The scam video itself may be down. This is Jeff, a screenshot of the fake, and an assessor's warning letter.

### THE PAYOFF

- **Property taxes are set by your county and state, never by Congress.** No federal form exists.
- Call your assessor at the number on your tax bill, not the one in a video.
- Never give your SSN to file for a "freeze." Real senior exemptions are filed with your county, for free. *(verify)*

# 4. GOT MONEY FROM AMAZON? IT'S REAL. CASH IT BEFORE IT EXPIRES

**Slot:** F2 C, or the 10/16 F1 Amazon show · **Confidence: HIGH** · *TIME-SENSITIVE: payments expire 60 days after they're issued*

**SLOT FIT:** Good-news C. It's not a scam, so it can't lead; it fits the 10/16 Amazon show better. The payoff is "this one is real, and here's the scam version."

### WORKING TEASE

> CHECK YOUR PAYPAL. CHECK YOUR VENMO. CHECK YOUR MAILBOX.
> AMAZON MAY HAVE SENT YOU UP TO 200 DOLLARS. AND IT'S REAL.
> BUT IT EXPIRES. AND THE SCAMMERS KNOW YOU'RE WAITING FOR IT.

### THE HOOK

Under the FTC's $2.5 billion Prime settlement, Amazon began a new round of automatic payments on Oct 1, 2026, to people who used 11 to 20 Prime benefits in a year. The cap rose from $51 to $200. Payments arrive by PayPal, Venmo or paper check, with no claim needed, and they expire after 60 days. The FTC says it is not contacting anyone about these refunds; anyone who does is likely a scammer.

### FOOTAGE — BE HONEST ABOUT THIS

ABC News 1.6M (Nov 2025, an earlier payment round) and Cleveland 19 (Oct 1). Mostly Jeff and a screen-share of the FTC refund page.

### THE PAYOFF

- Check the PayPal, Venmo and mailing address on your Amazon account. **Cash it within 60 days.**
- Questions go to ftc.gov/amazon-refunds only. *(verify exact URL)*
- Nobody legit asks you to pay a fee or "verify" your bank to get it.
