## 1. Cases

### A. SUCCESS: Braintree, Massachusetts (June–July 2024). Neighbor finds camera, police set a trap, 3 arrested
**Confirmed facts**
- On June 20, 2024, burglars broke into a family's home in the Messina Woods neighborhood. The family's own security video shows a masked burglar crawling across the living-room floor before alarms went off. CBS Boston says police believe the burglars "used it to case that home and broke in while the family was on vacation."
- About two weeks later a neighbor spotted a wireless camera in the bushes across the street, aimed at the house. WCVB says it had leaves glued on. CBS Boston says it was wrapped in duct tape: neighbor Kailynn Needle: it was "standing up facing up so it would face the house and it had duct tape around it so that it would be camouflaged into the bushes." A neighbor called police.
- Police treated it as "more like a crime in progress" (Chief Tim Cohoon). They had the family fake a second vacation (the family drove to Logan Airport) and put the house under surveillance.
- On July 11, 2024, undercover officers watched a man dressed as a landscaper / in black with a backpack come back to the house and the camera. They arrested him after a foot chase and a struggle, and arrested two women in a getaway car.
- Lt. Det. Brian Cohoon: **"We set a trap, they took the bait, and we were able to take them into custody."**
- Chief Tim Cohoon: "Everybody dropped everything to make sure we were putting our best foot forward on this one."
- Charges (per WCVB): the man was charged with assault and battery on a police officer, resisting arrest, carrying a dangerous weapon, conspiracy, B&E and possession of burglarious tools. The women were charged with conspiracy and possession of burglarious tools, and one also with giving false information. Police say all three are Colombian citizens and appear linked to "South American Theft Groups," "an organized international theft group known to use extensive surveillance."
- Victim: "It's really, really terrifying honestly ... you wouldn't think that it would happen to you."
- What was taken in the June 20 break-in: **could not find** (no outlet lists items or a value).
- Outcome of the charges (plea or conviction): **could not find**.

## 3. Mechanics (sourced)
- **What they look like:** "a cellphone or small camera taped to a battery pack ... wrapped in camouflage tape, dark fabric, or artificial leaves" (Rancho Cucamonga PD via KTLA 10/8/26). Fake grass (West Covina, ABC7 4/27/25). Box wrapped in artificial turf holding "a phone and a camera with extra batteries" (Sheriff Luna, ABC7 5/20/26). Black electrical tape (Temecula). Duct tape (Braintree). Pouch in a bush (PVE). Phone buried in a lawn (Queens).
- **Power and feed:** external power bank / battery pack plus portable Wi-Fi hotspot or MiFi (San Jose, San Dimas, Temecula, Whittier: "a large battery that was connected the camera with its own hotspot"). The feed is watched remotely: Temecula's device was likely "feeding live images" (KTLA 9/5/24); Rancho Cucamonga PD says crews "monitor the footage remotely before a burglary."
- **Where they go:** bushes, planters, trees, fences, near driveways, on poles (Rancho Cucamonga, Brea, Cerritos). Usually **across the street or on a neighbor's property, aimed at the target home** (Braintree, West Hills, Alhambra, Whittier, San Dimas, Michigan).
- **What crews watch for:** when residents leave and return, driveways and entry doors, routines, valuables (Rancho Cucamonga PD, Brea PD, Patch/LASD 5/15/26). They also check social media for travel, leave a DoorDash bag and ring the bell to see if anyone answers, and target homes near golf courses, parks and trails with easy escape routes (ABC7 5/20/26). San Jose: suspects pose as delivery drivers.
- **How long left:** varies. In West Hills it was planted at about 2 a.m. and the burglary came about 9 hours later. In Braintree, police believe it was there before the 6/20 break-in and a suspect visited it afterward; it was found about 2 weeks later. Kent Co.: "stalking them for weeks, anywhere from one week to three weeks." Exact durations otherwise: **could not find**.
- **Retrieval:** crews come back for it. West Hills burglars grabbed the camera on the way out. The Braintree suspect came back to the camera, which is how police caught him.
- **Related tools:** WiFi jammers to knock out wireless home cameras (LA 5/20/26; Abington 2025; NBC News 12/5/25). GPS trackers on victims' cars (Michigan, Abington). Lower Merion: "in and out of a house within five minutes."
- **Bought where:** "They purchased devices at different locations and different stores under aliases and paid cash" (Riverside Co. investigator, KTLA 9/5/24).

## 4. What police say to do (exact source per step)
1. **Walk your yard regularly.** Check bushes, planters, trees, fences and landscaping. Sources: Whittier PD (KTLA 9/16/26), Brea PD (KTLA 6/23/26), LASD (Patch 5/15/26).
2. **Trim trees and shrubs** to remove hiding spots (Whittier PD, Brea PD, LASD San Dimas).
3. **If you find one: don't touch, move or remove it. Leave it in place and call police** so officers can collect evidence. Brea PD: leave it, call 714-990-7911. Rancho Cucamonga PD: "Don't touch or remove them." LASD: "not to touch or move suspicious cameras or devices." SJPD: don't touch, call police. (West Covina's homeowner kicked his out. Don't model that. A Western Journal headline says "uproot it." That contradicts police; don't use it.)
4. **Tell your gardener / landscaper to look** (LASD Lost Hills: "While out gardening or if you have a someone that maintains your yard, be on the lookout..."; San Dimas's device was found by a landscaper).
5. **Use hard-wired security over wireless**, which can be jammed (Sheriff Luna, ABC7/NBC LA 5/20/26).
6. **Don't post travel on social media. Tell neighbors or police when you'll be away** (ABC7 5/20/26).
7. **Lights:** keep outdoor areas well lit, and use timers so the home looks occupied (LASD San Dimas; Lower Merion PD via FOX 29 7/10/26).
8. **Look out for neighbors' homes too.** Most cameras are aimed across the street (Rancho Cucamonga PD: "look out for your neighbors"; Luna: "If you see anything that looks like this in your neighbor's home, in your home, report it to us immediately.")
9. Flashlight trick for spotting lens reflections at night: a private bug-sweep investigator (Thomas Ham, FOX 11 6/19/24) suggested it, **not police**.
