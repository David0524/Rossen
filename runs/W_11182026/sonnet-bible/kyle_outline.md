## The Show, In Order

Four stops on your trip. Each story builds with IT GETS WORSE, ends on its own payoff, then hands off to the next stop.

### THEY GOOGLED THEIR AIRLINE... AND IT COST THEM \$7,000!

An Omaha family was finishing a week in Cancun when the app wouldn't let them check in. Anitha Googled Delta's number. The man who answered said all four tickets were "on standby" and they had to pay \$7,000 to fix it, refunded later. They paid. At the airport, the tickets were never on standby. It was a scammer, not Delta.

**Clip 2:** [<u>WOWT video</u>](https://www.wowt.com/video/2026/06/04/alleged-scam-uses-phony-number-airline/)

**SOUND 0:21–1:04:** "A relaxing week in Cancun ended with shaken nerves for Anitha when she tried to check in the family for the return flight." ANITHA: "He said like, hey, your all four tickets are on standby. So the moment I heard that word standby, you know, I was totally panicking." ... "She Googled the number, thinking it was Delta, but that turned out to be scammers. And they wanted \$7,000 to rebook the tickets, which the couple paid, but had been promised a refund."

**IT GETS WORSE: it's not just airlines.**

A man near Atlanta needed a rental car fast for a family emergency. He Googled Budget at the airport and hit call. The man who answered said his truck was gassed up and ready. He paid \$800 and even got a confirmation email with the Budget logo. It wasn't Budget.

**IT GETS WORSE: now Google's own AI hands you the scammer's number.**

A Las Vegas man asked Google for Royal Caribbean's number to book a shuttle for his cruise. Google's AI answer at the top of the page gave him a scammer, who knew the real shuttle prices and took his card. The Points Guy then tested it: Google's AI told travelers to call a scam line it labeled the airline's "Primary US escalation line." Scammers planted those numbers on hacked university and government websites, on The Villages' own website in Florida, even on a page hosted by Google itself.

**Clip 3:** [<u>Alex Rivlin's own Facebook video</u>](https://www.facebook.com/1140995993/videos/604442056076662/)

**SOUND 1:20–1:36:** ALEX RIVLIN: "So what did I do? I went to Google and I wrote in Royal Caribbean customer service phone number, USA, right? As you can see in the search bar right here. And then the AI result came up."

**SOUND 2:23–2:40:** "You put that phone number into the search bar in Google and say, who does this phone number belong to? It will actually say Royal Caribbean. It's pretty wild, but it is not Royal Caribbean's phone number."

**SOUND 4:36–4:47:** "I go immediately to my credit card and I see the charge in my credit card for some foreign company that I had never dealt with, done business with or authorized to do anything with."

**Clip 4:** Jeff's demo. Jeff asks Google and its AI for an airline's customer service number on camera, then shows the real one in the airline's app.

**THE PAYOFF: Never Google a customer service number, and never trust the number in an AI answer. Get it from the company's app, your confirmation email or the back of your credit card. Save it in your phone before you leave.**

### THE SCAMMER LOGGED INTO HER ACCOUNT... AND CANCELED HER FLIGHT!

Remember Ethan? A woman outside Chicago wanted to change a nonrefundable Icelandair ticket and ended up on the phone with him. He said he could override it: log into her account, upgrade her to a "flexible" ticket for about \$4,000 more, then cancel. He logged in and canceled her real reservation himself.

**Clip 5:** [<u>ABC7 Chicago video</u>](https://abc7chicago.com/videoClip/19560432/)

**SOUND 0:59–1:23:** JOANNA MOROZ DOMINO: "Very apologetic, saying he'll be able to help me." REPORTER: "He told her he could override the non-refundable tickets by logging into her account and upgrading her to a more expensive, flexible ticket, then canceling everything. It was all a scam to get her to pay the crooks about \$4,000 more." MOROZ DOMINO: "The scammer logged into my account. He was the one who canceled it."

**IT GETS WORSE: catch them, and they'll cancel your flight anyway.** In Maryland, a woman realized she'd been scammed on an "upgrade" and called the scammer out. So he used her confirmation code and last name to cancel her flight, three days before takeoff. That code and your last name are all a scammer needs.

**IT GETS WORSE: some scammers already have your confirmation code before you ever call.**

Frontier is warning about fake emails with customers' real names, real confirmation codes and real trips. One promised a \$387.90 refund, then asked for a credit card number. Frontier says it never emails asking for payment or reservation details, and it hasn't said how scammers got the information.

**Clip 6:** [<u>Jay Ayers TikTok</u>](https://www.tiktok.com/@jayayers3/video/7703296931115683103), 155K views. Backup: [<u>NBC10 story with video</u>](https://www.nbcphiladelphia.com/news/local/frontier-airlines-warns-passengers-to-active-email-phishing-scam/4489866/)

**SOUND 0:00–0:27:** JAY AYERS: "It's a scam going on right now from Frontier. If you took a flight on Frontier in the past, you're gonna get an email from somebody saying, hey, here's a refund that's arrived, and we did an audit of our system... Right here at the bottom it says, confirm card details to receive refund. Come on!"

**THE WIN: she got her \$4,000 back.** She'd paid with her American Express card. After ABC7 stepped in, AmEx refunded her.

**Clip 7:** [<u>ABC7 Chicago video</u>](https://abc7chicago.com/videoClip/19560432/)

**SOUND 2:01–2:10:** REPORTER, over her AmEx letter crediting \$3,899.88: "After we contacted Moroz Domino's credit card, American Express, she says they refunded her the nearly \$4,000 from the fraudulent transaction."

**THE PAYOFF: Your confirmation code is the key to your trip. Never give it to anyone who calls or emails you, and never let anyone log into your airline account. If you already did, call the airline from its app and ask them to lock your reservation. Pay with a credit card, and if something feels wrong, call your card company right away. And no real airline makes you pay to get a refund.**

### THIS FAKE FLIGHT WEBSITE SOLD THEM TICKETS... AND THE AIRLINE SAID THEY WERE FRAUD!

A Dallas family bought tickets to a cousin's wedding in Las Vegas through a website called Fly-n-Fare. At the Frontier counter, they were told the tickets were flagged as fraud. They missed the wedding. Fly-n-Fare never answered, and the BBB gives it an F.

**Clip 8:** [<u>FOX 4 Dallas video</u>](https://www.fox4news.com/video/1714045)

**SOUND 0:42–1:10:** "Shamira James jumped on the opportunity to get tickets for five family members to fly to Las Vegas for her cousin's wedding, but once at the check-in desk for Frontier Airlines, a fun trip turned into a costly 24-hour nightmare." SHAMIRA JAMES: "She was like, no, these tickets were flagged as fraud. We can't let you guys get on the flight. So I was like, wait a minute. Hold on. So I start calling Fly-n-Fare and they wouldn't answer. They kept forwarding my call to voicemail."

**IT GETS WORSE: it happens with hotels too.**

A Wisconsin family booked a hotel online. When they showed up, they found out they'd booked through a fake copycat website. No room, and the money was gone. They tried booking on another site, and a box had already been checked for a \$200 "refund protection" charge.

**Clip 9:** [<u>FOX6 Milwaukee story with video</u>](https://www.fox6now.com/news/wisconsin-fake-hotel-booking-scam)

**SOUND 0:13–0:53:** "You book a hotel room for a family reunion, then find out the room was never reserved. It happened to one Wisconsin family." MICHELLE REINEN, WISCONSIN CONSUMER PROTECTION: "They found out that when they got there, they hadn't really booked through the hotel. They had been directed through a fake imposter website." ... "A box had been pre-checked for them for a refund protection program that they had to pay \$200 for."

**THE WIN: the Dallas family got their money back.** After FOX 4 stepped in, Frontier refunded them.

**Clip 10:** [<u>FOX 4 Dallas video</u>](https://www.fox4news.com/video/1714045)

**SOUND 3:02–3:17:** "While they still missed their cousin's wedding, Shamira and Tamara are thankful to at least be getting some of their money back." THE FAMILY: "Thanks to you, Frontier came in... and it's given us our money back."

**THE PAYOFF: Book on the airline's or hotel's own website or app. If you used another site, look up your confirmation code in the airline's or hotel's app. If your trip doesn't show up, you don't have a reservation.**

### HIS SUITCASE WAS STOLEN... AND HE FOUND THE THIEF WEARING HIS CLOTHES!

You made it. Now go get your bag. Jameel Reid landed at the Atlanta airport and his suitcase was gone from baggage claim. He had an AirTag inside. It showed his bag at a hospital, then back at the airport, then at a train station. He followed it there and found a man sitting next to his suitcase, wearing his shirt, his jeans and his socks. He filmed the whole thing. Police arrested the man, and Jameel got everything back except the clothes the man had on.

**Clip 11:** [<u>FOX 5 Atlanta video</u>](https://www.fox5atlanta.com/news/man-hunts-down-stolen-suitcase-using-apple-airtags) · Backup: [<u>GMA video</u>](https://video.goodmorningamerica.com/video/98200229)

**SOUND 1:21–1:51:** "The odyssey finally led Reid right here to this SkyTrain station at the Georgia International Convention Center." JAMEEL REID: "As I come out, I smell my cologne." ... "He was wearing Reid's clothes." REID: "He had on my shirt, my jeans and my socks. I'm like, wow, this guy's really got on my entire stuff." Includes Reid's own video of the arrest.

**THE PAYOFF: Put an AirTag in every checked bag. The FAA allows them, and Delta, United, American and JetBlue can see your bag's location when you share it from the Find My app. And get to the carousel fast.**

## The Payoff

**Jeff's rule: only call the number you already have.**

**Before your next trip, do these three things:**

1.  Save your airline, hotel and rental car numbers in your phone, straight from their apps.

2.  Put your confirmation code in the airline's app and make sure your trip shows up.

3.  Drop an AirTag in your checked bag.

**And never:**

- Google a customer service number or trust one in an AI answer.

- Give your confirmation code to someone who calls or emails you.

- Pay money to get a refund.

**If it happens to you: pay with a credit card and call your card company fast. She almost lost her \$4,000 because she waited too long.**
