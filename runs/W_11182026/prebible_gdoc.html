THE PITCH

Your flight goes wrong, you Google the airline's number or ask Google's AI, and the "agent" who answers charges you to fix it, or logs in and cancels your real flight. Frontier just warned that fake emails carry real names and confirmation codes. Eight days before Thanksgiving, every viewer can check where the airline number in their phone came from.

STORY 1 — THE AIRLINE SCAMS THAT CAN STRAND YOU

Confirmed:

  • Omaha: Anitha (surname withheld) couldn't check in after a week in Cancun, searched for the airline's number, was told "all your four tickets are on standby," paid $7,000 to be refunded later, never was. At the airport, the tickets had never been on standby. Airline not named. BBB's Josh Planos and Celebrate Travel's Donna Dutcher quoted (IBTimes UK, June 5, 2026, citing WOWT).
  • Phoenix: "Cher" reached an American Airlines impostor after a cancellation; paid $1,010 to rebook, then was told to pay $1,010 more to get a $2,020 refund. ABC15: "You should never have to pay money to receive a refund" (ABC15, undated page, mid-2026; Simple Flying, July 9, 2026).
  • River Forest, IL: an email about an Icelandair change led to a "live chat" with a phone number; the man logged in, charged about $4,000 for "flexible" tickets and canceled the real reservation. AmEx refunded the $4,000 after the I-Team stepped in; about $2,000 for the original tickets was not recovered (ABC7 Chicago, July 23, 2026).
  • Boston: Marcia Hannon, booking for six travelers in their 70s and 80s, called a number from a Google search; AmEx stopped a $4,500 charge. The Points Guy found Google AI Mode and AI Overviews giving scam numbers, planted on hacked pages including The Villages, FL and the City of Freeport, TX. Google: "Our anti-spam protections are highly effective at keeping scams out of our features"; flagged pages removed (The Points Guy, Michelle Couch-Friedman, Oct 27, 2026; Travel Noire, Oct 30).
  • Frontier: "We are aware of an active email phishing campaign targeting our customers"; "Frontier Airlines does not email customers requesting flight, reservation, payment or contact detail information." Emails carry real names, six-character confirmation codes and itineraries; one offered a $387.90 refund (NBC10, Nov 2; TravelersToday, Nov 4; ABC7 KGO, Nov 11, 2026; flyfrontier.com/phishing-awareness). Frontier: 801-401-9000.
  • The Villages, FL: George and Diana Franco paid $11,975.63 for 14 Punta Cana tickets; 3 were issued. Delta: booked by "a fraudulent third party who had no ticketing authority." Paid March 2024; story updated June 28, 2026 (WESH 2).
  • Dallas: two cousins lost $2,500 through "Fly-n-Fare"; Frontier flagged the tickets; BBB rates it F (FOX 4, Sept 25, 2025).
  • BBB warnings on third-party booking sites: Local 3 News, Nov 9, 2026 (0:40 video); KPRC, Nov 10, 2026 (Leah Napoliello, BBB Greater Houston).
  • AARP: the FTC got more than 64,000 reports of travel, vacation and timeshare fraud in 2025 (AARP, updated Apr 24, 2026).
