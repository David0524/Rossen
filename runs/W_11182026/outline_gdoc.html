SUMMARY

When a flight goes wrong, people search for the airline's number, and the "agent" who answers is a scammer who charges them to fix it, or logs in and cancels the real trip. It's spreading: Google's own AI answers have handed out scam numbers, fake emails carry passengers' real confirmation codes, fake booking sites sell tickets that don't exist, and thieves work the baggage carousel. Then the one place to get your airline's number every time, and five more ways to keep your trip and your money.

HERE'S HOW TO PROTECT YOURSELF

A9 · the fix

  1. GET YOUR AIRLINE'S NUMBER FROM THE APP… NEVER FROM A SEARCH (the one place from the tease) - Open the airline's app, your confirmation email, or your boarding pass. The real number is right there. - Never call a number from a search result, an ad, or an AI answer. - Save the real number in your phone today, before you need it. So what do the experts say? Listen. - CLIP 8 (HORIZONTAL): ABC 7 Chicago — Suburban woman recovers nearly $4K after fake airline support scam · Jul 23, 2026 · 4:14
    • 3:27–3:50 ("YOUR HOTEL CREDIT CARD")
    • Soundbite: "…can also send you phishing messages claiming that something is wrong with your current travel. They can trick you into sending payment information, so always contact companies directly… get real numbers through your app or by calling the number on the back of your airline credit card or your hotel credit card."
  2. NO REAL AIRLINE CHARGES YOU TO FIX ITS OWN PROBLEM - If the airline canceled your flight, you're owed a refund. You don't pay to get it. - Anyone asking for money to rebook a canceled flight, or to send you a refund, hang up.
  3. NEVER CLICK A LINK ABOUT YOUR BOOKING… OPEN THE APP YOURSELF - Even if the email has your name and your confirmation code. - Frontier says it will never ask you to re-enter your card, confirm a flight, or pay to get a refund by email.
  4. MAKE SURE YOUR TICKET IS REAL - Within a day of booking, put your confirmation code into the airline's own app or website. If it isn't there, call the airline at the number from step one. - Book with the airline directly when you can, and pay with a credit card, not a debit card.
  5. ALREADY PAID A SCAMMER? CALL YOUR CARD COMPANY FIRST - Then call the airline at the real number, and ask them to lock down your reservation. - Change your airline account password.
  6. AT BAGGAGE CLAIM, GET TO THE CAROUSEL FIRST - Use a bright, easy-to-spot bag tag, and keep your medications and valuables in your carry-on.

The fix: sources

  • Step 1: get the number from the app, confirmation email or boarding pass; never from a search or AI answer (ABC 7 Chicago, Jul 23, 2026, clip 8; ABC15: "go directly to a business website to find their contact information"; The Points Guy, Oct 27, 2026). Save it before you leave (BBB and Delta via WOWT, Jun 4, 2026).
  • Step 2: you're owed a full refund when the airline cancels; never pay to get one (ABC15, citing federal rules; ⚠️ load the DOT page, transportation.gov/airconsumer, before air).
  • Step 3: Frontier won't email asking you to revalidate a flight, re-enter a card, pay for a refund or confirm billing (flyfrontier.com/phishing-awareness; ABC7 KGO, Nov 11, 2026).
  • Step 4: check the confirmation code in the airline's app or site within a day (Delta via ANF, Aug 11, 2026 ⚠️ secondary); book direct and pay by credit card (BBB via FOX 4, Sep 25, 2025; BBB via Local 3, Nov 9, 2026).
  • Step 5: call the card company first, then the airline, change the password (The Points Guy, Oct 27, 2026; ABC 7 Chicago: AmEx reversed the charge, but first said she'd missed the 60-day window).
  • Step 6: get to the carousel first, distinctive tag, valuables and medication in the carry-on (⚠️ no official source loaded; check TSA or the airport's guidance).
  • Do-nothing test: a viewer who only saves the number (step 1) is still exposed to the email (step 3) and the fake booking site (step 4). A viewer who already paid needs step 5 the same day, because card disputes run on deadlines (River Forest nearly lost hers past 60 days).
