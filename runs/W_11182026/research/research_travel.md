### 2. The new twist: Google's AI answer gives you the scammer's number
**Boston, MA, Marcia Hannon, organizing a trip for six travelers in their 70s and 80s; $4,500 charge attempted (July 2026; published Oct 27, 2026)**
- Swiss changed their Boston to Zurich to Geneva connection. The number on the tickets was disconnected, and the airline chat had 47 people ahead of her. She Googled, called the number shown, and signed a "credit card authorization form" bearing an airline logo. Minutes later **AmEx flagged a $4,500 charge** from WHOP, a digital marketplace, and stopped it.
- The Points Guy's ombudsman found Google **AI Mode / AI Overviews** telling travelers to call a scam call center and "ask for a tier-two claims agent," and labeling a scam line the "Primary US escalation line." The fake numbers were planted on hacked university, government and clinic pages, and on the websites of **The Villages, Florida** and the City of Freeport, TX. A fake Royal Caribbean number was sourced from a hacked U.S. government page, and an Expedia one from spam hosted on Google.com.
- Google: "Our anti-spam protections are highly effective at keeping scams out of our features"; it removed the pages that were flagged.
- **Source:** The Points Guy, Michelle Couch-Friedman, Oct 27, 2026 (loaded): https://thepointsguy.com/news/travel-scam-google-ai-search-results/. Also Travel Noire, Oct 30, 2026 (loaded): https://travelnoire.com/google-ai-search-scam-travelers

## PAYOFF (tell + move per scam)
1. **Fake airline number.** *Tell:* "your tickets are on standby" or "pay now, we'll refund you later." *Move:* get the number from the airline's app, your confirmation email or the back of your boarding pass. Never Google it. Real airlines don't charge you to fix their cancellation. ABC15: "You should never have to pay money to receive a refund." Federal rule: airlines must refund a canceled flight within 7 days on a credit card (ABC15). Ask hotel staff to help find the real number (Donna Dutcher, via IBTimes).
2. **AI search number.** *Tell:* the answer cites a university, government, clinic or church page, or tells you to ask for a "tier-two claims agent" or "escalation line." *Move:* don't call a number from an AI answer; open the airline's own app. If you paid: (1) call your card company, (2) tell the airline to lock the reservation, (3) set up identity monitoring (Couch-Friedman, TPG, Oct 27, 2026).
3. **Frontier-style email/text.** *Tell:* real trip details plus a request to "confirm" your card or claim a refund; a sender address that isn't the airline's (noreply@reviewpro.com). *Move:* Frontier says it will never ask you to revalidate a flight, re-enter a card, pay to get a refund, or click a link to confirm billing. Close the email and open the app yourself. Questions: Frontier, 801-401-9000 (flyfrontier.com/phishing-awareness). Forward scam texts to 7726 (SPAM) (Fox/CyberGuy, 2025; verify).
4. **Fake booking site.** *Tell:* a price far below everyone else; a confirmation or card charge in another company's name (e.g. "AirTravelPad" / "AirlineFare"). *Move:* book on the airline's own site; within a day, put the confirmation code into the airline's app (Delta advises adding your record locator to your SkyMiles account; ANF, Aug 11, 2026) to see whether a real ticket exists. Pay by credit card (BBB, Nov 9, 2026). Search the site name plus "scam" before paying (BBB Houston, Nov 10, 2026).
5. **Carousel thieves.** *Tell:* someone lingering at the carousel without luggage tags (in the MIA case deputies say the suspect had no boarding pass or claim tags; CBS Miami, Dec 2025). *Move:* go straight to the carousel, use a bright, distinctive bag tag, keep valuables and medications in your carry-on, and drop an AirTag/tracker in the bag (verify).
6. **Booking app "re-verify your card."** *Tell:* urgency ("pay or lose your room") plus a link to a payment page. *Move:* never enter a card through a message link; call the hotel at the number on its own website; keep payment inside the app's checkout (verify with Booking.com's own guidance).
7. **Fake social media help accounts.** *Tell:* a fast reply to your complaint, a "support/care/helpdesk" name, a request to move to DMs or WhatsApp. *Move:* never post your confirmation number publicly; use only the accounts linked from the airline's website (FTC's Alvaro Puig, 2024 guidance via WJLA).
8. **Too-good travel agent.** *Tell:* "great deals" paid by Venmo/Zelle. *Move:* pay by credit card; check that the agent is registered (some states require it) and look them up at BBB.org (AARP 2019 guidance; verify state rules).
- **Toll texts:** toll agencies don't text you a payment link (Ohio Turnpike, BBB); check your account by typing the agency's site yourself.
- **Hotel room call:** hang up and call the front desk from the room phone's button, or walk down to the lobby.
