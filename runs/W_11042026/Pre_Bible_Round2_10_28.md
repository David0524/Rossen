# 1. THEY FOLLOW YOU HOME FROM THE BANK

**Slot:** F2 A · **Confidence: HIGH** · *The scariest beat on the sheet. The losses in the last six weeks are small.*

**SLOT FIT:** F2 A. It carries it on the beat. It's a crew with a villain, the viewer is picked because they're older (an elderly couple in San Antonio), there were arrests Oct 19, and holiday-cash warnings are out. The weak spot: no clip over 200K, and the biggest named cash loss in the window is just over $1,000.

### WORKING TEASE

> YOU JUST TOOK OUT CASH FOR THE HOLIDAYS.
> YOU WALK OUT TO YOUR CAR. YOU DRIVE HOME.
> AND THE CAR BEHIND YOU… PULLS INTO YOUR STREET.
> POLICE CALL IT "JUGGING." THEY WATCH THE BANK. THEY PICK YOU. THEY FOLLOW YOU.
> ONE WOMAN SAYS THEY TRAILED HER FROM THE BANK, TO HER HOUSE, TO LUNCH.
> THE ONE THING TO DO IF A CAR IS BEHIND YOU WHEN YOU LEAVE THE BANK.

### THE HOOK

On Oct 15, an elderly couple (police believe the husband is 88 or 89) walked out of a Frost Bank in San Antonio carrying a bag of cash. A gray sedan pulled up behind them, a masked passenger grabbed the bag, and the car took off with more than $1,000. Police: "More than likely, the car was parked here, watched them come out with the bag from the bank." A day earlier in the Houston area, a woman noticed someone walking in and out of her bank without ever going to a window. That crew followed her home, then to a Pearland restaurant, and smashed her car window in under a minute. "And they followed me home." Two suspects were arrested; police tie them to a larger group.

### HOW IT WORKS

- **A spotter inside the bank** watches who takes out cash.
- **A chase car** follows you home or to your next stop. They swap license plates as they go.
- **The grab:** your bag, your car window, or you in your driveway.
- **Extended threat:** now they know where you live.

### WHAT'S NEW

The ATM cash-trapping show (Sep 9) was about the machine. This one is about you after you leave the bank, and it ends at your house.

### FOOTAGE WE NEED

- **The get: FOX 26 Houston**, with the victim and the arrests (122K, Oct 20).
- KENS 5 on the San Antonio couple (Oct 15). ABC10's $13K Manteca loss (62K, June). FOX 5 San Diego, a father followed home (89K, March).
- Jeff in a bank parking lot: what to watch for in the mirror. *(demo)*

### THE PAYOFF

- **The one thing: if a car follows you, never drive home.** Drive to a police station or a busy lot and call 911. *(verify: Independence, MO police advice)*
- Put the cash away before you leave the teller. Ask for an envelope, never walk out with a bank bag.
- Check your mirror for two or three turns after you leave.
- Never leave a purse or cash in the car, even for "a minute."
- After the San Antonio couple: a car parked near the door with someone sitting in it is your cue to go back inside.

> "YOUR BANK ACCOUNT HAS BEEN COMPROMISED. YOUR SAVINGS ARE NOT SAFE."

### THE HOOK

A caller posing as a U.S. Treasury agent told Joanne Tarczewski of Paulding County, Georgia, that her bank account was compromised. Over three months she sent wires, close to $10,000 in gift cards, and a $40,000 cash withdrawal: more than $50,000 in all. Her bank flagged the transactions and called her daughter. When the scammers asked for $35,000 more, deputies coached her through the handoff and arrested the courier at the meeting spot. "You live under strain constantly because you're saying, are they real?" **Why now** 🚨: a bank closed Sept 25 and the FDIC took over, so "is my bank safe?" is in the news. The scammer's script starts exactly there. *The link between that news and these calls is my inference; see the back.*

### HOW IT WORKS

- **The fear:** "your bank has been compromised" (Sheriff Ashley Henson). **The fix:** move your money "to protect it."
- **The handoff:** a courier meets you in person. **Extended threat:** a stranger who knows you have cash, and keeps coming back.

### FOOTAGE WE NEED

- **The get: FOX 5's sting package** and the sheriff's video. WatchGuard body cam of a cash handoff (a different scam, the same handoff).

### THE PAYOFF

- **The one sentence: "Banks will never call or text and ask you to move your money to protect it."** (FCC, July 2026)
- Hang up and call the number on the back of your card or on your statement, never one the caller gives you.
- Never hand cash or gift cards to anyone who comes to collect them.
- Set up a family check-in on any big withdrawal. That's what caught this one.
- Report it: the National Elder Fraud Hotline, 1-833-372-8311. *(verify)*

# 4. THE CELEBRITY IN YOUR MESSAGES IS A.I. — EVEN ON A VIDEO CALL

**Slot:** F2 A · **Confidence: MEDIUM** · *The AI video is new. Clip heat is thin and the best footage belongs to Nat Geo.*

**SLOT FIT:** F2 A, if the room accepts a smaller loss up top. It's a villain, a 55+ target, and a home on the line. The $384,000 Pennsylvania case gives it size. Romance touched Mar 25 and Aug 26 (wrong number into pig butchering). It was never the lead, and it was never about AI video.

### WORKING TEASE

> HE SAYS YOUR NAME. ON VIDEO.
> YOU ASK HIM TO WAVE. A FEW MINUTES LATER… HE WAVES.
> HE'S A ROCK STAR. AND HE SAYS HE'S IN LOVE WITH YOU.
> EXCEPT IT WAS NEVER HIM. EVERY VIDEO WAS MADE BY A-I.
> SHE SENT MORE THAN 10 THOUSAND DOLLARS… AND ENDED UP FACING FORECLOSURE.
> POLICE NOW SAY IT PLAINLY: A VIDEO CALL DOESN'T PROVE ANYTHING.

### THE HOOK

Leslie Pickett of Louisiana spent ten months with a man she believed was Jon Bon Jovi. He sent AI-made videos that said her name, did the gestures she asked for within minutes, and eventually got on a live video call. She sent more than $10,000 by Venmo, PayPal, Cash App and crypto, fell deep into debt and faced foreclosure (Fox News, Oct 24). Journalist Mariana van Zeller's Nat Geo team traced the account to a house in Lagos. In September, a 63-year-old Pennsylvania woman lost about $384,000 plus hundreds of Apple gift cards in an online romance scam. State police: "Photos, videos and even video calls do not prove someone is who they claim to be."

### HOW IT WORKS

- **A famous face** in your messages, or a game app's chat.
- **The proof:** personalized AI videos, then a live call.
- **The need:** a frozen account, a lawsuit, a fee, paid in gift cards, apps or crypto. **Extended threat:** your house (foreclosure; in a 2025 Florida case, a home-equity loan).

### FOOTAGE — BE HONEST ABOUT THIS

The best footage is the Nat Geo documentary and van Zeller's YouTube upload (70,688 views, Oct 21), and it may not clear. Local coverage is mostly print. Realistic segment: Jeff, a screen-share of what an AI "celebrity" video looks like *(demo)*, and the Pennsylvania state police warning.

### THE PAYOFF

- **A video call is not proof anymore.** (Pennsylvania State Police)
- Never send money, gift cards or crypto to someone you've only met online.
- No real celebrity asks a fan for money, and none needs your help with a frozen account.
- Before you send anything, tell one family member. If you can't, that's the tell.
- Report it to the FBI at ic3.gov.

# 5. "YOU WON!" — THE PRIZE CALL THAT COST A 75-YEAR-OLD $25,000

**Slot:** F2 A or B · **Confidence: MEDIUM** · *Ryan called prize scams "very clickable"; the board said it needed a fresh peg. This is it.*

### WORKING TEASE

> "CONGRATULATIONS. YOU'VE WON."
> ALL YOU HAVE TO DO… IS PAY A SMALL FEE TO COLLECT.
> A 75-YEAR-OLD IN FLORIDA PAID. THEN PAID AGAIN. GIFT CARDS. CASH. ZELLE.
> 25 THOUSAND DOLLARS GONE.
> AND POLICE SAY ONE OF THE PEOPLE BEHIND IT… WAS A FLIGHT ATTENDANT. ARRESTED WHEN SHE LANDED IN MIAMI.

### THE HOOK

A caller posing as a Publishers Clearing House representative told a 75-year-old Palm Coast, Florida resident she had won a prize. To collect, she paid with gift cards, cash withdrawals, Zelle and credit card charges, about $25,310 in all. On Oct 23, federal officers arrested Destiny Blake, 24, an American Airlines flight attendant from Brooklyn, on a Flagler County warrant as she arrived at Miami International. Surveillance video showed her using the victim's card at a Queens supermarket, per the sheriff's release. The same month, other counties warned about calls and letters saying seniors had won, with a fee to claim the prize *(verify)*.

### HOW IT WORKS

- **The win** you never entered, by phone or a letter with a realistic check.
- **The fee** to collect, then taxes, then "processing."
- **The access:** your card, your accounts. **Extended threat:** once you pay, your name goes on the list for the next caller.

### FOOTAGE — BE HONEST ABOUT THIS

There's no clip heat. YouTube is full of scam-baiter PCH videos, which don't count. Ask Flagler County for the supermarket surveillance and the booking video. Otherwise it's Jeff, the sheriff's release, and a screen-share of a fake prize letter.

### THE PAYOFF

- **A real prize never costs money to collect.** Any fee means it's a scam.
- Never pay with gift cards, Zelle or cash to "release" a prize.
- Hang up and call a family member, not the number they give you.
- Already paid? Call your bank and the card company that day, then police.
