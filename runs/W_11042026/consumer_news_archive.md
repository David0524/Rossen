# Consumer news on Rossen Reports: what the archive shows, and a draft way to grade it

Prepared Oct 28, 2026. Data: all 149 livestreams on the channel's Live tab, with views and YouTube's approximate upload month, pulled Oct 28. Lift = views ÷ the median of shows from the same approximate month. I classified each show by its lead topic from the title, then corrected the cases where the title misleads by reading the opening of 62 transcripts. **Draft for the producer, not yet in the skill.** CLAUDE.md: no skill changes until approved.

Caveats:
- YouTube's upload months are approximate, and the oldest bucket (Oct 2025 and earlier) runs high.
- This is the whole-show number, not the segment: a consumer B inside a scam show can't be measured until the retention data arrives (docs/PLATFORM_DATA_PLAN.md).
- Titles were classified by me. Treat the lane medians as directional.

## What separates them

1. **The company is the villain, and it's doing something to you.** Banning you, arresting you, watching you, charging you twice. "What they're hiding" works; "here's a deal" doesn't.
2. **There's a consequence, stated.** Banned, arrested, photographed, charged twice. The ban and arrest titles run 2.3x to 22x.
3. **It's something the viewer uses every day.** Phone, TV, card, the grocery store, the electric bill, Amazon returns. Travel, theme parks and health ran 0.1x to 0.7x.
4. **The viewer can check right now.** Shut off the apps, change the TV setting, check your returns history. The same stop-and-check test the lead test already uses.
5. **Payouts, lawsuits and "secrets" without a threat miss.** Uber sued, red-light fines erased, "secret file," hidden charges plus a refund.

## Draft: a consumer-news track inside the quality gate

Keep the scam track as is. Add a second track for stories with no scammer, chosen at the start of grading. Proposed changes only; everything not listed stays the same.

**Hard gates (consumer track):**

| Gate | Scam track (today) | Consumer track (draft) |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | A scammer, thief or company does it to a 55+ viewer, not niche | **A company, store, platform or rule does something to the viewer's money, privacy or rights** that they don't know about. The viewer is the one affected, through something they use every day. Fails: perks, curiosity, deals, travel-only, or a lawsuit about someone else. |
| H3 | A documented victim or loss | **A documented person affected, or an official action** (a regulator, a ruling, a company's own policy page or announcement) from the last 12 months |
| H2, H4, H5, H6 | unchanged | unchanged. A clip is still required: "Clips are super important." |

**Score (consumer track):**
- **Fear becomes Stakes.**
  - 2 = the company can ban, arrest, track or double-charge you, and the consequence is stated.
  - 1 = you're overpaying or missing money.
  - 0 = curiosity or perk.
- **Track record uses these consumer lanes:**
  - watched 2.0x
  - ban or arrest 2.3x+
  - service 0.8x
  - overcharging 0.8x
  - travel and health 0.7x or lower
  - deals 0.5x
- Heat, Breadth, Peg, Newness and Producibility are unchanged.
- The pitch threshold is unchanged (9+, Heat at least 1).

**Slotting:**
- A consumer story is B or C by default.
- It can be pitched as an A only with Stakes 2 and the lead test.
- LEGAL: Jeff's Sep 28 rule says an A "has to be a scam." The archive says consumer-villain leads have done well: the apps, self-checkout, Keep-it and returns-ban shows. **That's Jeff's call, not the skill's.**

**Packaging rule:** frame it as the company doing it to you (TRAP, BAN, ARREST, SPYING, "What They're Hiding"), never as a deal, a fee explainer or a lawsuit headline. Don't call a non-scam a "scam" in the title (tipping did, and it worked, but it's a credibility risk).
