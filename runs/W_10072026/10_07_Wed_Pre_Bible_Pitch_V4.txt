WORKING TEASE
THAT BLACK BOX ON YOUR TARGET BASKET? IT'S A TRACKER… AND A TARGET WORKER JUST PROVED IT TO 3.4 MILLION PEOPLE. THE FACT-CHECKERS LOOKED INTO IT… HE WAS RIGHT. THE LIGHTS OVER YOUR HEAD ARE IN ON IT. THE HOOK
Target is tracking your basket. Target worker Elijah films the black box screwed onto a red basket and calls it a tracker. Snopes checks and says Target does track shoppers with it: a Bluetooth beacon that talks to the LED lights over your head, which pass the location to Target's app. Snopes' caveat is the payoff: the beacon only knows where the basket is; the app on your phone is what ties it to you. Lowe's cameras know which aisles you visit. Its privacy statement says the cameras track which aisles you walk and how long you stay, and some lots read your plate. And new AI-powered grocery carts are coming to a store near you. Instacart's Caper Cart has cameras pointed into your basket, cameras pointed out at the aisle, and a location tracker, and it serves you ads based on where you're standing.
THE NEW AI CARTS: HOW FAST THEY'RE SPREADING
Instacart says it tripled Caper Cart deployments in a year. Now in 100+ cities across 15 states.
Already rolling at Kroger, ShopRite, Schnucks, Sprouts, Wegmans, Weis and Geissler's.

Elijah's TikTok — the get. In hand; needs clearance.
Lot plate cameras and a Caper Cart in an aisle — not pulled yet.
THE PAYOFF
iPhone: Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services → each store app → Never. Then Settings → the app → Bluetooth off.
Android: Settings → Apps → store app → Permissions → Location → Don't allow. (verify on Jeff's device)
Turn off the app's location and Bluetooth, and the basket tracks a basket, not you. The cameras and plate readers don't need your phone; those you can't switch off.

WORKING TEASE
YOU'RE WATCHING YOUR TV… AND YOUR TV IS WATCHING YOU BACK. IT'S GRABBING WHAT'S ON YOUR SCREEN… CABLE, NETFLIX, EVEN YOUR GAME CONSOLE. AND NOW RESEARCHERS SAY SOME LG TVs KEEP LISTENING… EVEN WHEN THEY LOOK TURNED OFF. LG SAYS THAT'S NOT TRUE. BUYING A NEW TV ON PRIME DAY? DO THIS BEFORE YOU WATCH A SINGLE SHOW.
THE HOOK
Most smart TVs run Automatic Content Recognition, sampling what's on your screen, even cable and consoles, to build an ad profile. Texas sued Samsung, LG, Sony, Hisense and TCL in December; Samsung settled Feb 26 and LG May 11, both agreeing to ask Texans first. Then on Sept 6, Gamers Nexus, working with Level1Techs and security researchers, said LG TVs capture microphone audio in standby, keep voice transcripts in plain text, and scan your home network for other devices. LG says the claims "are not true" and that voice only runs when you press the button or say "Hi LG." Tom's Hardware could not verify either side.
PAST PERFORMANCE

FOOTAGE WE NEED

THE PAYOFF
Samsung: Settings → All Settings → General & Privacy → Terms & Privacy → Viewing Information Services → off. (verify by model year)
LG: Settings → All Settings → General → System → Additional Settings → Live Plus → off. Turn off voice recognition too. (verify path)
Vizio: Settings → System → Reset & Admin → Viewing Data → off. Sony: Samba Interactive TV → off. (verify Sony menu)
Roku TVs (TCL, Hisense and others): Settings → Privacy → Smart TV Experience → uncheck "Use info from TV inputs."
Most private: keep the TV off Wi-Fi and stream through a separate stick. Recheck after updates.
