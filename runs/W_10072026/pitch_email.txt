1. THEY STOLE HER HOUSE WITH A PIECE OF PAPER

Deed fraud. Scammers forge a deed, record it at the county, and sell or borrow against a home the owner still lives in, and paid-off homes are the target because there's no lender to catch it. New Jersey's State Commission of Investigation released its deed-fraud report Sept 9 (it opens with the woman charged with trying to steal the title to Graceland), and Harris County sued nine people the same day over a ring that took at least 10 homes. The insider version: a South Carolina man's caregiver allegedly got the deed to his house for $5, and his daughter found out he'd been dead three days. That clip has 5.7M views. We touched deed fraud as a B story in April; nothing since.

3. STRANGERS MOVED INTO HIS HOUSE… AND HE CAN'T GET THEM OUT

Serial squatters and fake tenants. A Texas woman rented a home using her father's identity and a bad check, never paid a dime, and cost the owner her house; she just got 20 years after nearly three dozen evictions. In San Francisco, the city attorney sued over a vacant $4.4 million Sea Cliff home whose deed was allegedly forged at least seven times so strangers could move in. Florida's rental-fraud law (HB 1293) makes fake pay stubs and IDs on a rental application a felony starting this Thursday, Oct 1.
