## Things that change the outline (read first)
- **Item 5 date is wrong.** The Target basket TikTok went up **Aug 29, 2026**, not 2025. Snopes rated the claim **TRUE** (Sept 5, 2026). It is not a light that pings. It is a Bluetooth beacon on the basket that talks to the smart LED ceiling lights.

It is being tested in about 10 to a dozen U.S. restaurants, and the U.S. rollout is expected in 2027. We **cannot** say McDonald's is moving away from AI. The accurate line is that McDonald's pulled back in 2024 and is now trying again, while also retraining workers on hospitality.
- **Macy's:** the only hard beacon evidence is from 2013-2015. I could not open Macy's current privacy notice (the site returned 403). Do not say Macy's app tracks you by Bluetooth today.
- **"Sell":** Target's own policy says it "sells" data under California's legal definition, which covers ad-related sharing. Neither Target nor Walmart lists **location** as data shared for targeted ads. Do not say "they sell your location."

**2. Do they "sell" the data?** Verdict: PARTLY. It depends on the legal definition.
- **Target:** the policy says it "sells" personal information under California law, meaning it shares it for interest-based ads. The categories are identifiers, internet activity, demographics and commercial (purchase) information. **Geolocation is not listed** among the sold/shared categories. Opt-out options include "Do Not Share for Cross-Context Behavioral Advertising." Target also runs the Roundel ad business on first-party shopper data.
- **Walmart:** the policy says: "We may disclose personal information to third parties or process your personal information for various purposes, including targeted advertising. These kinds of disclosures may be deemed selling under certain state privacy laws." In Appendix 1, Geolocation is marked **"Used for Targeted Advertising? No"**, but it is still listed as disclosed to vendors (it names "advertising networks") and to business partners. Opt out under "Your Privacy Choices," found in the app settings or the site footer.
- **Macy's:** it has a "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" page. I could not read the detail.
- **Safe line:** "These stores use what they learn about you to sell ads. Their own policies admit some of that sharing counts as a 'sale' under state privacy laws, and you can opt out. Look for 'Do Not Sell or Share' or 'Your Privacy Choices.'"
- **Avoid:** "Target/Walmart sell your location to the highest bidder."
- Sources: the Target and Walmart policies above; Macy's DNS page: https://www.macys.com/customer-service/articles/do-not-sell-or-share-my-personal-information

**3. Why it hurts you**
- **FTC surveillance-pricing study (Jan 17, 2025):** CONFIRMED. The FTC studied pricing middlemen. Staff found that details like "a person's precise location or browser history" can be used to target people with different prices for the same product, and that even "mouse movements" and items left in an online cart can feed pricing. These were *preliminary staff findings*. The Ferguson-led FTC later closed the public comment docket and never finished the study.
- **Newer angle:** on **Aug 19, 2026** the FTC proposed an enforcement policy statement. It says that failing to disclose the use of personal data to set individual prices can be deceptive or unfair under Section 5. It is **proposed, not final**. The FTC itself says it can't ban personalized pricing outright.
- **New York's Algorithmic Pricing Disclosure Act:** CONFIRMED. **In effect since Nov 10, 2025.** Companies that set prices with an algorithm using your personal data must show: **"THIS PRICE WAS SET BY AN ALGORITHM USING YOUR PERSONAL DATA."** Penalties go up to $1,000 per violation. Judge Rakoff dismissed the NRF's First Amendment challenge on Oct 8, 2025. The NRF's appeal is **pending at the 2nd Circuit**, with the AG's brief filed Feb 2026. I found no ruling yet.
- **Kroger:** CONFIRMED, but it's from **Aug 5, 2024**, so it's old. Sens. Warren and Casey wrote to Kroger warning that electronic shelf labels could enable "surge pricing" and that facial recognition on shelves could tailor offers. Kroger denied surge pricing and said it isn't using facial recognition with its shelf labels or pricing.
- **Safe line:** "The FTC found companies can use things like your location and browsing to charge you a different price than your neighbor for the same item. New York now makes stores tell you when an algorithm used your personal data to set your price."
- Sources:
  - FTC press release, Jan 17, 2025: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-surveillance-pricing-study-indicates-wide-range-personal-data-used-set-individualized-consumer
  - FTC proposed policy statement, Aug 2026: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/08/ftc-seeks-comment-enforcement-policy-statement-regarding-personalized-pricing
  - Jones Day, Nov 2025 (NY law in effect): https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2025/11/new-yorks-novel-algorithmic-pricing-disclosure-law-takes-effect
  - MediaPost, Feb 16, 2026 (2nd Circuit appeal): https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/412793/
  - Sen. Casey release, Aug 2024 (Kroger): https://www.casey.senate.gov/news/releases/casey-warren-raise-alarms-about-krogers-use-of-digital-price-tags-warn-of-grocery-giants-surge-pricing-causing-price-gouging-and-hurting-consumers

**4. Phone settings:** CONFIRMED
- **Android 12 and up (including 14-16), per Google:** Settings > Location > **Location services** > turn off **Bluetooth scanning** (and Wi-Fi scanning).
- **Samsung One UI:** Settings > Location > **Location services** > Bluetooth scanning off. Samsung also has a separate toggle at Settings > Connections > More connection settings > **Nearby device scanning**. That one is for Samsung's device-pairing pop-ups, not location, but it's fine to mention.
- **Android per-app:** Settings > Apps > [app] > Permissions > **Nearby devices** > Don't allow. To see all apps with it: Settings > Security & privacy > Privacy > **Permission manager** > Nearby devices.
- **iPhone:** Settings > **Privacy & Security > Bluetooth** > toggle each app off. **Caveat:** iPhone apps detect store beacons (iBeacon) through **Location Services**, not the Bluetooth permission. The stronger tip is Settings > Privacy & Security > **Location Services** > [store app] > **Never** or **While Using**, with **Precise Location** off.
- **Safe line:** "On Android, go to Settings, Location, Location services and turn off Bluetooth scanning. On iPhone, go to Settings, Privacy & Security, and check both Bluetooth and Location Services for your store apps."
- Sources:
  - Google: https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/3467281
  - Google app permissions: https://support.google.com/android/answer/9431959
  - Apple: https://support.apple.com/en-us/102267
  - How-To Geek (Samsung): https://www.howtogeek.com/i-forced-my-galaxy-phone-to-stop-checking-location-networks-and-added-hours-to-my-battery-life/

**5. Target basket "black box":** CONFIRMED (fix the date to 2026)
- The TikTok was posted **Aug 29, 2026**, about 3.4 million views. A worker points to a device on a basket. **Snopes (Sept 5, 2026) rated it TRUE.** The device is an Acuity Brands Bluetooth Low Energy beacon whose manual says it "communicates its position to Atrius Assets." Target has used that indoor-location software since at least 2020. The beacon sends signals to Target's smart LED ceiling lights, and those can pass location to the Target app.
- The beacon itself doesn't know who you are. The link to *you* comes through the Target app, if you've allowed location. Target had not replied to Snopes at publication.
- **Safe line:** "Yes, that little box is real. It's a Bluetooth beacon that works with the store's ceiling lights. If you have the Target app with location turned on, that's how Target can tell where you are in the store."
- Sources:
  - Snopes, Sept 5, 2026: https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/target-basket-trackers/
  - Yahoo syndication: https://tech.yahoo.com/cybersecurity/articles/fact-check-target-baskets-track-230000060.html

**6. AI smart carts:** CONFIRMED
- **Instacart Caper Cart:** in 100+ cities across 15 states with more than a dozen banners: Kroger, Schnucks, ShopRite/Wakefern (about 20% of Wakefern stores), Wegmans, Sprouts and others. The store count **tripled** in 2025. Weis Markets launched **June 4, 2026**. Instacart gives no total store count.
- What the carts collect, per Instacart: basket-facing cameras, a scale, **in-store location tracking**, loyalty-account link, and "real-time, location-aware ads" from brands. Caper's own privacy policy (updated June 18, 2026) **explicitly does not cover shopper data from the carts**, so we can't say what is kept or for how long.
- **Amazon Dash Cart:** a redesigned cart went into Whole Foods in Jan 2026, with a plan for **25+ Whole Foods stores by end of 2026**. Amazon Fresh physical stores closed in early 2026. I didn't verify Dash Cart data details beyond cameras and sensors.
- **Safe line:** "Instacart's AI carts are now in grocery stores in more than 100 cities. They have cameras and location tracking, and they show you ads based on where you are in the aisle."
- Sources:
  - Instacart/Weis release, June 4, 2026: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/instacart-and-weis-markets-launch-ai-powered-caper-carts-to-transform-in-store-shopping-302790778.html

## STORY C: Companies moving away from AI

**7. McDonald's/IBM:** CONFIRMED, but there's a big update
- In **June 2024** McDonald's ended its IBM "Automated Order Taker" test in 100+ restaurants. It was shut off by July 26, 2024, after viral error videos.
- **NEW, Sept 23-25, 2026:** McDonald's unveiled **"Archy,"** an AI drive-thru voice that takes orders in English and Spanish and is part of ArchIQ, running on Google edge hardware. McDonald's claims accuracy above 90%. It is being tested in about 10 to a dozen U.S. restaurants plus Australia, with the U.S. rollout expected in 2027.
- **Safe line:** "McDonald's pulled its AI drive-thru in 2024 after the mistakes went viral. Just last week it announced it's trying again with a new AI voice."
