### 1. "The stranger's sunglasses were filming you." Smart glasses secretly recording women, and Meta's fix from last week (Sept 23)
- **Beat:** A woman found out she'd been secretly filmed by a stranger's Meta smart glasses only after the video went viral. Last week Meta admitted there was a loophole and patched it.
- **What happened:**
  - CBS News (Aug 21, 2026): Toluwa Omitowoju was filmed without consent by a man in Meta AI Ray-Bans for a "pickup line" video. It went viral: "probably over 200,000 views". She said "I had no say," and learned about it when friends sent her the video. CBS reporter Anna Schecter tested the recording light: when she covered the LED *after* recording had started, the glasses kept filming. Meta said the LED blocks recording only if it's covered *before* you press record. https://www.cbsnews.com/news/meta-ai-smart-glasses-covert-filming-privacy/
  - Fortune (Jul 28, 2026): Instagram head Adam Mosseri said Instagram will take down pickup-line harassment videos. Meta held 69.2% of the smart-glasses market in Q1 2026, and shipments were up 167% year over year to about 2.25M units. Fortune also cites a lawsuit alleging contractors in Kenya reviewed footage of users in the bathroom and undressing. https://fortune.com/2026/07/28/ray-ban-meta-pervert-glasses-secret-videos-women/
  - **NEW PEG, Meta Connect, Sept 23, 2026:** Meta launched camera-free "Ray-Ban Meta Audio Glasses" ($349) and a mandatory update. The glasses now check the LED continuously, recording stops if the light is blocked at any point, and tampering with the LED disables the camera. BGR, Sept 24: https://www.bgr.com/2267868/meta-smart-glasses-audio-only-no-camera/
- **Named people on camera:** Toluwa Omitowoju (victim, CBS interview); Anna Schecter (CBS test).

### 2. "A cop tracked his wife 717 times on the cameras watching your street." Florida pulls Flock cameras (deadline Sept 30) plus a new public map of 335,701 devices
- **Beat:** There may be a camera on your street logging every trip your car makes. A police officer allegedly used them to follow his estranged wife 717 times, and now anyone can look up where the cameras are.
- **What happened:**
  - Haines City, FL officer **Christopher Anthony Goodson, 31**, was arrested Aug 11, 2026 (unauthorized computer access, official misconduct). The affidavit says he searched his estranged wife's plate in Flock 717 times between Sept 2024 and June 30, 2026. She wasn't tied to any crime. He told a supervisor he was checking on his kids while they were with their mother. https://local12.com/news/nation-world/florida-police-officer-flock-cameras-717-searches-estranged-wife-christopher-anthony-goodson-haines-city ; https://www.wfla.com/news/polk-county/haines-city-officer-used-flock-cameras-to-track-estranged-wife-on-717-occasions-affidavit/
  - FDOT memo (Aug 31) revoked permits for plate readers on state right-of-way and gave agencies 30 days to remove them, so the **deadline is Sept 30, 2026**. After that, FDOT removes them itself. DeSantis said "I think it's out of control." A former Sumter County detective was also charged over tracking someone's ex. Flock cut its default data retention from 30 to 7 days. https://www.fox13news.com/news/flock-cameras-must-be-pulled-from-florida-state-roads-within-30-days-desantis-orders ; WUSF, Sept 2 (Tampa has 17 cameras to remove; Sheriff Grady Judd objects): https://www.wusf.org/transportation/2026-09-02/law-enforcement-agencies-remove-flock-cameras-florida-roads
