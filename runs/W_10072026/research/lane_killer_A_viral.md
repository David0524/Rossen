## RECOMMENDATION — A: "You hired her to care for Dad. She took his house for $5."

This is the Robert and Nora Rowland caregiver-deed case in Conway, SC.

**Why it's the killer:**
- It is the biggest victim-register consumer clip I found in the window. The WSMV package has **5,680,248 views** in about a month.
- It hits three 55+ fears at once:
  - **the home**: the deed is signed away for $5
  - **the body**: a Parkinson's and brain-injury patient in the care of a stranger
  - **trust**: the aide you hired and let into the house
- The on-camera victim is named and emotional. Nora Rowland appears in the WMBF/WSMV, Inside Edition and WJZ/CBS Baltimore pieces.
- There's a jaw-drop twist: the daughter was the one put in handcuffs.
- It is not the same as the "home title theft" pitch that was ruled out for having no victim video. This one has a victim, bodycam and 6M+ views across the story.

**Beat:** She drove down for a wellness check. The locks were changed, a Ring camera was up, and the walls were painted orange. Her dad had been dead three days, and the house was in his caregiver's name. Then the cops cuffed *her*.

**What happened (verified):**
- **Robert Rowland, 75, of Conway, SC.** He had Parkinson's and a traumatic brain injury and was classified as a vulnerable adult.
  - In March 2024 he started with a caregiver, Sarah (Weaver) Smalls, placed through Griswold Home Care, later identified as Griswold Care Pairing of Myrtle Beach.
  - In April 2024 Smalls was named his power of attorney.
  - **On Apr 17, 2024, a quitclaim deed moved the house to her for $5.**
  - Bank statements show transfers to her before and after his death.
  - Sources: WMBF, Aug 27 https://www.wmbfnews.com/2026/08/27/this-is-my-dads-house-daughter-claims-nurse-took-deed-fathers-conway-home-before-his-death/ and WSMV republish, Aug 29 (updated Sep 3), byline Makayla Evans / Jordan Gartner: https://www.wsmv.com/2026/08/29/this-is-my-dads-house-daughter-says-caregiver-took-deed-her-fathers-home-weeks-before-his-death/
- **Dollar figures.**
  - CBS Baltimore (Sep 3): a **$300,000 home** transferred for $5, plus **about $27,000 taken, including $14,000 in cash**.
  - It also reports the caregiver moved him into a senior-living facility without telling his daughter, then moved into his home.
  - https://www.cbsnews.com/baltimore/news/baltimore-woman-caregiver-stole-fathers-home-thousands-hid-death-south-carolina/
- **The discovery.**
  - Around Father's Day 2024, Nora drove from Baltimore after weeks of silence. Her key didn't work, and the door had been changed and a Ring doorbell installed.
  - Smalls showed police the deed, and **Nora and her partner were handcuffed**. Moneywise/Yahoo and Inside Edition say she spent **3 days in custody**.
  - The trespassing charges were dismissed.
  - Sources: WMBF (above); Moneywise/Yahoo, Sep 2: https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/she-went-check-her-elderly-151500824.html
- **Where the case stands.**
  - **No criminal charge against Smalls** had been reported as of early September.
  - Conway PD is investigating. CBS Baltimore says Horry County investigators "recently began" working the case after Nora filed a civil complaint.
  - She has to sue civilly to recover the assets.
- **Griswold's statement:** Smalls was an independent contractor, passed a background check, worked briefly in 2024, and the company doesn't condone abuse (WMBF).

**Verbatim quotes, each from a page I loaded:**
- Nora Rowland: "I'm walking out of the house expecting them to come and help, and they put me and my partner in handcuffs immediately. I just kept saying, 'I don't know what's going on; this is my dad's house. My dad is dead.'" (WMBF)
- Nora, describing the house: "The walls are painted this god-awful highlighter orange." (WMBF)
- Nora: "It's so easy for them to get taken advantage of. He was a nice man; he would never think that someone could do this to him." (WMBF)
- Nora: "I hired her to care for him, not to steal from him." (Moneywise/Yahoo)
- Nora: "I keep wracking my brain like, how could I have prevented this?" (CBS Baltimore)
- **Horry County Register of Deeds Marion Foxworth III:** "If it contains all the correct information to meet the legal standard, then we record it. We don't check whether what's in that deed is fair or right." (WMBF)
  - This is the scariest line for the audience: the county records any deed that is filled out correctly and never checks whether it's legitimate.

**Protective payoff (for the Jeff walk-and-talk):**
- Use a licensed, **bonded** agency. Ask directly whether the aide is an employee or an "independent contractor". This case turned on that answer.
- Sign a durable financial **power of attorney with a trusted family member now**, while Mom or Dad is still competent, so an aide can't be named POA later.
- **Keep the aide away from money.** Use a prepaid debit card for household spending, and give the family view-only access to the accounts.
- **Sign up for your county's free property/deed fraud alert.** Many counties email you when a document is recorded on your name. This is the direct answer to the registrar quote. (It is not verified for Horry County specifically, so check before saying it on air.)
- If contact suddenly drops off, request a wellness check, and call Adult Protective Services.

Tips 1–3 and the APS tip come from Moneywise/Yahoo (Sep 2).

**Weaknesses / legal:**
- **Smalls is accused, not charged.** Always say "allegedly." Consider not naming her or showing her face.
- Griswold says she was an independent contractor, so don't imply the company stole.
- **Some details conflict across outlets.**
  - "3 days in custody" (Moneywise, Inside Edition) vs. "handcuffed" (WMBF).
  - Whether Robert died at home or in the facility is unclear. WSMV says Nora found he'd "been dead three days", while CBS says he was moved to senior living first.
  - Use the narrow common version: he died, nobody told her, and she learned days later.
- The events are from 2024. The story broke in Aug 2026.
- Bodycam comes only from the WMBF text. **Unverified whether the WSMV YouTube upload includes it.** Screen it.
- I could not load the upload date or description for 3EzgKCWb83Q (bot wall). "This month" comes from the search filter.
- Nora's age is not reported.

## RUNNER-UP 1 — "They paid $100K+ to move in for life. The place went bankrupt, and the money's gone."

This is the senior-community (CCRC) entrance-fee bankruptcy story.

**Scorecard:** it has life savings, home and a very fresh NBC investigation, but it is smaller on views.
- NBC News, **Sep 27, 2026**, Gretchen Morgenson, "Promises unkept: Senior living home bankruptcies rob families of their life savings": https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/promises-unkept-senior-living-home-bankruptcies-rob-families-life-savi-rcna590148
  - Houston's **The Buckingham** used bankruptcy to avoid repaying **nearly $150M in entrance fees**, even after it was bought by a private equity firm.
  - Named people: **Roger Scales** (moved his mother in), **Emily Ryan, 85**, and **Tom Ryan, 93**. The full article text didn't load, so I have no quotes.
- The liquidation plan took effect **Aug 31, 2026** and projects **about 4.8% recovery** for residents' refund claims, vs. about 56% for bondholders.
  - Source: WebSearch summary citing elevenflo.com (https://elevenflo.com/blog/buckingham-senior-living). The page was not loaded; verify before air.
  - The second Chapter 11 filing was Nov 2025, per a McKnight's search result.
- Clip: **NBC News, "Residents warn of losing life savings after senior community goes bankrupt"**, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c-Z93a2cKwM, **108,551 views**, uploaded this month (the NBC video page says late Sep; anchor Yasmin Vossoughian, per search summary).
- Background: CBS NY Harborside (Port Washington) story, **Oct 24, 2024** (old). More than 180 seniors and about $130M were at risk. Named residents include John Haney, 93, and Fran Schmidt, 91: "They're supposed to protect the elderly." https://www.cbsnews.com/newyork/news/the-harborside-bankruptcy-port-washington/
- **Why #2:** It's the freshest peg (Sep 27) and the purest 55+ "life savings plus home" fear, and it's new to Rossen. But there's only one clip at 108K, and it's a slower, systemic story, not a villain story.
  - **Payoff:** Ask whether the entrance fee is refundable and **who guarantees it**. Check the community's audited financials and occupancy, the state regulator, and escrow rules.
  - **Best use:** a B, or a second act of the A ("even the place you move into can take your house money").

## RUNNER-UP 2 — "Your investment firm calls. Two minutes later, every dollar is gone, and they say it's your fault."

This is the Charles Schwab impostor case in Oakland.

- ABC7 SF, Dan Noyes, **Sep 3, 2026**: https://abc7news.com/post/was-panic-scammers-use-frantic-phone-call-drain-oakland-womans-investment-account/19781742/
  - Victim **Jazz Powell-Loggins** is on camera.
  - A caller posing as Schwab security phoned on Jul 13. Within 2 minutes, **$12,145**, her whole account, was moved to an Ohio bank.
  - Schwab denied her claim, saying its "investigation found that you provided the verification code to a third party." She is going to arbitration.
  - Her quote: "And it was panic, panic immediately. 'Hey, someone's trying to sell your money out.'"
- Clip: ABC7 News Bay Area, "Scammers use frantic phone call to drain Oakland woman's life savings", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dCi0vihleiI, **120,985 views**, this month.
- **Why not A:** Her age isn't given and the loss is small. It's also adjacent to the spoofed-bank-caller pitch already judged "adjacent" and to the aired fake-Zelle story. It works as a fold-in line only.
