## COSTCO-SPECIFIC

**Costco's own official warnings say nothing about the parking lot.**
- Costco Customer Service, "Preventing Fraud," Document CU1432 v12.0, published 03/16/2026. https://customerservice.costco.com/app/answers/answer_view/a_id/1174 (Confirmed.) It names six scams, all online or phone: phishing/spoofing, fake job offers, pop-up ads, check scam, fraudulent phone calls (a fake "$500 Costco Travel credit or Shop Card" survey reward, sometimes spoofing local warehouse numbers), and PayPal phishing. It says nothing about in-person, parking-lot, gas-station or jewelry scams.
  - Key lines: "Unsolicited electronic communications from Costco do not ask for your personal information." Real Costco email comes from a Costco.com domain. Digital Shop Cards come from costco@digitalshopcard.costco.com.
  - **Angle for Jeff as Costco's biggest fan:** "Costco warns you about fake emails. Nobody's warning you about the parking lot. So I will." (Opinion line. The fact under it, that the page has no lot warnings, is confirmed.)
- The old answer page (a_id 1180) now says "This answer is no longer available."

**Costco lot cases found (detail in CASES):** San Jose (Almaden Plaza) heirloom swap Aug 2026; Mettawa, Ill. fake-gold con $15,800 (Mar 2026 crime, reported Aug 2026); Lehi/American Fork, Utah car tampering Jun 2026; Vallejo, Calif. purse robbery Aug 2025; Portland 93-year-old pickpocketed May 2024; Salinas necklace swap (KION, date not confirmed); Cumming, Ga. 75-year-old, $10K+ charges, Dec 2022; Arvada, Colo. about $10K in charges (2023 case number); Long Island "deception thefts" at 10+ Costco and Bed Bath lots, Aug 2022; Fife, Wash. $10,000 "attorney" con, Feb 2021.

**Costco-branded bonus (weaker): "scan your membership card for me" at the Costco pump.**
- A stranger says they forgot their card or their phone died and asks you to scan your membership so they can fuel. The Motley Fool (on AOL, Sep 27, 2024) says the payment card may be stolen and your account gets tied to the purchase. https://www.aol.com/finance/buy-gas-costco-watch-scam-091511077.html (Confirmed as Motley Fool's claim. The "no ZIP code at Costco pumps" mechanism is called "online lore" in that piece, so it is **unverified**. Most supporting material is a Reddit thread and a TikTok, @bestwallethacks https://www.tiktok.com/@bestwallethacks/video/7397796976571452702, views not confirmed, manual pull.)
- The safe advice is Costco's own policy: membership cards aren't shareable. Send them to the attendant. **(verify Costco's attendant policy)**
- Panhandling risk: low.

**Costco gas pump skimmers:** I found no 2026 report. The closest is a skimmer on a **register** at the Warminster Township, Pa. Costco, found Jul 19, 2025 (6abc, search snippet only, https://6abc.com/post/skimming-device-discovered-register-costco-warminster-township-bucks-county-police/17275508/). ### 1. The "free gift" necklace swap: they put fake gold on you and take your real gold. LEAD CANDIDATE.
**San Jose, Calif.: Costco, Almaden Plaza lot. Sunday before Aug 23, 2026.**
- What happened: Two women approached an elderly couple **loading groceries** and won their trust with religious talk. One followed the husband to his car and put a "gift" necklace around his neck. His daughter says that's when they removed his chain. They took his **18-karat gold medallion**, an heirloom from the priest who married the couple, and left a fake chain. A **second car with at least two men blocked the couple's car** from leaving. San Jose police confirmed arrests on the Monday after.
- Victims: husband 86, wife 75. Loss: the heirloom medallion (no dollar value given).
- Outlet: NBC Bay Area, published Aug 23, 2026, updated Aug 24. https://www.nbcbayarea.com/news/local/elderly-san-jose-couple-robbed-in-parking-lot/4131895/ (Confirmed.) Shopper quote: "I think people need to be more aware of their surroundings and be less trusting, especially if they flatter you."

### 2. "Real gold" for gas money: the stranded-family seller and the $15,800 loss at a Costco

### 7. Myth buster: the paper on your back windshield
- The viral warning (stop to remove a flyer and a carjacker jumps in) has circulated since at least 2004. Fact-checkers rate it false or unproven, and police in Toms River, N.J. disavowed one version. https://mybeachradio.com/carjacking-internet-rumor-not-true-toms-river-police-say/ and https://hoaxes.org/weblog/comments/carjacker_strategy_place_paper_on_your_rear_window (search snippets, not loaded).
- Useful as "you've seen this on Facebook. Here's what's actually happening." It should **not** be pitched as a real scam.

## PAYOFF (tell + move per scam)

1. **Necklace swap / "blessing."**
   - Tell: a friendly stranger wants to put something on you (a necklace, a scarf, flowers, a hug, a prayer).
   - Move: step back and say no. "Never allow strangers to place jewelry, scarves or other items around their necks" (Sacramento County Sheriff, Jul 2026). Leave valuable jewelry home or covered when you shop (same source). If it happens, keep the fake piece for police and handle it as little as possible.
2. **Fake gold for gas money.**
   - Tell: a hard-luck story (gas, the airport, car repair) plus "real gold" at a steep discount, cash only.
   - Move: never buy jewelry in a parking lot. Walk away before money changes hands. Police in West Shore and Kauai say report it right away. Kauai PD also says to direct anyone in real distress to the authorities.
3. **Purse and wallet grab while loading.**
   - Tell: someone asks for directions or help, or says "is something wrong with your bumper?", while your purse sits in the cart or on the seat.
   - Move: put your purse in the car first and **lock the doors while you load the trunk** (King County Sheriff, 2019; Portland PD, 2024). Never leave a purse in the cart (Tukwila-area police, 2019 snippet). Men, button the back pocket (Portland PD). Carry one card, not eight (Cumming PD Sgt. PJ Girvan: "I wouldn't carry a large amount of cash on you, just a card on you"). If it happens, **call your card companies before you leave the lot**, because thieves bought gift cards within 30 minutes in Portland. Card-alert texts on your phone are a sensible extra **(verify issuer specifics)**.
4. **Car won't start or "your tire's flat" helper.**
   - Tell: your car suddenly won't start or has a "flat," and helpers are right there.
   - Move: stay in your locked car and call police or roadside assistance (Culpeper Sheriff via Fredericksburg Free Press). If someone flags you down while driving, **don't pull over. Drive to a busy, lit place or the store entrance and check there** (consistent with Culpeper and Penn's account; **verify a police source for "don't pull over"**). Never let strangers work on your car, and never hand your phone over after a payment (American Fork PD).
5. **Tracker on your car.**
   - Tell: "AirTag Found Moving With You," "[Product] Found Moving With You," or an unknown tracker alert.
   - iPhone (Apple Support, confirmed): needs iOS 14.5+ (17.5+ for non-Apple trackers). Turn on **Settings > Privacy & Security > Location Services** (and under System Services, Significant Locations), **Settings > Bluetooth**, and **Settings > Notifications > Tracking Notifications > Allow Notifications**. Keep Airplane Mode off. Tap the alert, then **Play Sound**. Past alerts are in **Find My > Items > Unknown Items Detected with You.** https://support.apple.com/en-us/119874
   - Android (Google Support, confirmed): Android 6+. **Settings > Safety & emergency > Unknown tracker alerts** (Android 11 and lower: Settings > Google > Personal & device safety > Unknown tracker alerts). Use **Scan now** for a 10-second manual scan. https://support.google.com/android/answer/13658562
   - Where to check: under or behind the license plate, inside the fuel door, deep in the wheel wells (KCRA/Rossen; 21Alive).
   - Move: if you feel unsafe, go to a public place and call police. Keep the device and screenshot the serial number (Apple). Don't drive home if you think you're being followed: drive to a police station or busy lot and call 911 (SPD Crime Prevention Coordinator Sarah Lawson, KIRO 7, Nov 16, 2023: call 911 "even if it turns out to be nothing").
6. **Fake ticket or "you hit me."**
   - Tell: a ticket with a QR code, a plate that isn't yours, a "meter" where there are no meters.
   - Move: don't scan it. Look up tickets on the city's official site (Hurst PD; Houston). For a "you hit me" demand, don't pay cash. Call police and insurance **(verify a 2025-26 police source)**.
7. **Costco pump membership ask.**
   - Tell: "I forgot my card, can you scan yours?"
   - Move: say no and point them to the attendant (Motley Fool, 2024; **verify Costco policy wording**).
- **General (Boone County Sheriff, Nov 2026):** put purchases in the trunk before you arrive, park in busy, brightly lit spots, lock every time, and call 911 for anything suspicious.
