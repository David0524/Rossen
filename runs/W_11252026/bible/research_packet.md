## Traps

- **Details the outline adds that the source doesn't say:**
  - A6 runway, "He was home in about half an hour", and the Out, "Before he even knew his wallet was gone": neither is in any source. The source says only that police say the cards were used within 30 minutes.
  - A4 "sitting in his car with his wife": the source says he sat in his car and his wife tried to get the watch back.
  - A7 "So what did police find on the parking lot cameras?": police haven't said; it's her account of the footage. A7 "said he could fix it, for two hundred dollars": $200 is what she says the scam asks.
  - A8 Out "Yours can too, if one setting is on": overclaims (fact 24).
- **People who are accused, not convicted (never "thief" or "thieves" for a named or shown person, in the tease too):** the Renton group (pleaded not guilty), the Lynnwood couple (arrested, charged), the Portland men (accused, unidentified), the Vallejo minors (arrested). The San Jose women and the Utah men aren't charged or identified at all. "Police say", "accused", "these crews" are safe. The A6 Out's "This is a business" is opinion about accused people: keep it tied to what detectives say they found.
- **Named companies whose non-response must be said aloud after their clip:** none of the stores is accused of anything. Costco is the host's store, never the villain: no line may suggest Costco lots are less safe than others. Motor1 asked Costco about parking-lot security and got no reply. We haven't asked, so **never "we reached out"** unless the producer does.
- **Refunds and outcomes that are partial, pending, or about something else:** San Jose: no arrest, medallion not recovered (never say they were caught). Renton: the necklace was recovered; the case isn't over (not-guilty pleas). Utah: no arrest; she lost nothing (don't imply she paid). Alabama: no arrest; whoever placed it is unknown.
- **Advice that fails the people the scam targets:**
  - "Lock the doors while you load": a purse locked in the car while you stand at the trunk is safe from a grab, but keep your keys on you, or a 55+ viewer locks the keys in.
  - "Let it go" (jewelry grab) fits the Lynnwood injury; don't extend it into "don't call police."
  - Card step: call the number on the back of your card, or use the bank's app, to freeze it (the card itself may be gone: the number is in the app or on a statement). Purchase alerts are set in each bank's app, so don't give a universal menu path.
  - Tracker: a phone alert catches Bluetooth trackers (AirTags and compatible), not every GPS tracker. If you find one, don't drive home; go somewhere public and call police (fact 26).
  - Car won't start: "stay in and lock the doors" holds, but don't tell people never to accept a jump from anyone. The move is to call for help yourself.
- **Words the copy must not use:** "panhandlers" or "panhandling" (dead list; every case takes money, jewelry, a purse or safety). Nationalities (the package names one; we say "an organized group" or "a crew"). "Thieves" for a shown or named accused person. No outlet names in spoken copy. No holiday, Black Friday, season or "this week" (evergreen, Jeff 11/11). The outline's tease line "a parking lot just like the one you'll be in this week" dates it.
