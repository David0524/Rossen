# 1. HE "ACCIDENTALLY" REFUNDED YOU THOUSANDS — AND NOW HE WANTS IT BACK

**Slot:** F2 A (recommended) · **Confidence: HIGH** · *Biggest audience fit on the slate; no local TV clip yet*

**SLOT FIT:** F2 A. A scam with a villain, our viewer as the target, and a brand everyone knows: Best Buy and Geek Squad were the most impersonated company in 2023, with about 52,000 FTC reports. The weak spot is footage.

> 🔥 **VIRAL CLIP** — TikTok, @nexttech57, 2.1M views (the story finder's figure; TikTok blocks requests from here), upload date unconfirmed
> https://www.tiktok.com/@nexttech57/video/7696128227668004126
> Appears to show: a scambaiter recording a whole fake Geek Squad "refund" call (from the story finder's description only).
> *Not watched — no timecode, no outcue, no confirmation of what's on screen.*

### WORKING TEASE

> IT STARTS WITH AN EMAIL. YOUR GEEK SQUAD PLAN JUST RENEWED. CALL THIS NUMBER TO CANCEL.
> YOU CALL. THEY OFFER YOU A REFUND. THEN: "OH NO. WE SENT YOU TOO MUCH."
> YOUR BANK ACCOUNT SAYS SO. RIGHT THERE ON THE SCREEN.
> AN 81-YEAR-OLD WOMAN IN NEW JERSEY BELIEVED IT. POLICE SAY SHE HANDED A STRANGER 20 THOUSAND DOLLARS IN CASH. IN A PARKING LOT.
> THE ONE THING NO REAL COMPANY WILL EVER ASK YOU TO DO.

### THE HOOK

In August, police in Hammonton, New Jersey, say an 81-year-old woman got an email saying her Geek Squad membership was due for renewal. A caller told her $20,000 had been deposited into her account by mistake and had to be returned. She withdrew $20,000, boxed it up and handed it to a man in a parking lot. Her bank later confirmed no money had ever come in. Two New York men were charged after being stopped in Ohio. In Texas this month, Kaye Allen's $455 "refund" showed up as about $45,500, and callers pushed her to send it back. She hung up.

### HOW IT WORKS

- A fake renewal or invoice email with a phone number to "cancel." You call them.
- They ask to get into your computer, to "process the refund."
- **The FBI says they move your own money between your accounts** (checking, savings, retirement), so it looks like you were overpaid.
- Then the begging starts: send back the "extra" in cash, a wire or gift cards.

### FOOTAGE — BE HONEST ABOUT THIS

- **The scambaiter TikTok** is the strongest visual, but it has to be pulled by hand and cleared. Ryan's rubric doesn't count scambaiter views as heat.
- **No 2026 local TV clip found.** The Hammonton case comes through Hoodline, citing Breaking AC News. Ask the Dallas Morning News for Kaye Allen.

### THE PAYOFF

- **No real company ever asks you to send back a refund.** That's the one thing from the tease.
- Never call a number from a "renewal" email. Best Buy's own number is 1-888-237-8289, and it says it generally doesn't make unsolicited calls.
- Never let a caller into your computer. If you already did, turn it off and call your bank from the number on your card.
- After Hammonton: anyone who wants cash in a box is a scammer.

# 2. THE CALL SAYS YOUR SON IS IN JAIL. HE IS. THE CALLER IS STILL A SCAMMER

**Slot:** F2 A option, or B · **Confidence: HIGH** · *13 sheriff warnings since Nov 2025; four families who lost money*

**SLOT FIT:** It works as an A or a B. It's the newest version of the grandparent scam, and it beats the usual tip, because calling your grandson won't help when he really is in jail. ⚠️ Audience fit is the question for the room: will a 55+ viewer think "my family doesn't get arrested"? The fix is to frame it around a grandchild, a traffic stop or a DUI.

### WORKING TEASE

> THE PHONE RINGS. YOUR SON'S BEEN ARRESTED.
> YOU CHECK THE JAIL WEBSITE. HE'S THERE.
> THE CALLER KNOWS THE JAIL. KNOWS THE CHARGES. WANTS THE BAIL MONEY NOW, BY CASH APP.
> EVERYTHING HE SAID WAS TRUE… EXCEPT WHO HE WAS.
> SHERIFFS IN AT LEAST SEVEN STATES ARE WARNING ABOUT THIS ONE. HERE'S THE ONLY PLACE YOU EVER PAY BAIL.

### THE HOOK

Steven Woodson's son really was in Hamilton County Jail in Indiana. A caller said Woodson was his son's emergency contact and took $1,100 by Cash App, to an account named "Pretrial Service Inc." (WISH-TV, Sept 14). In Oklahoma City, a mother lost $3,000 while her daughter was still being booked (News 9, April). A family in Birmingham lost about $2,000 (WBRC, January), and one in Cass County, Minnesota lost $1,800 (August). Sheriffs say scammers watch the public booking list and call within hours.

### HOW IT WORKS

- **The jail's online booking list is public.** That's where scammers get the name, the charge and the jail.
- They call the family posing as a bondsman, a deputy or "pretrial services," sometimes promising a discount or an early release.
- Payment goes by Cash App, Zelle, crypto or gift cards. Georgia's prison system believes some calls come from inmates on smuggled phones.

### WHAT'S NEW

Mar 25 covered fake arrest-warrant calls (Martha, $12,000), and Sep 4 had a grandparent clip. Neither used real arrests. This is the first story where the arrest is real.

### FOOTAGE WE NEED

- **The get: WISH-TV I-Team (Woodson)** and FOX 5 Atlanta's "Mother duped in bail bondsman scam" (embedded on its site). Views and dates are unconfirmed.
- YouTube candidates, checked by title only: Denver Sheriff, Trempealeau County, Dallas County, and St. Johns County shutting down its online jail roster.
- Jeff pulls up a real county jail roster on screen to show how public it is *(demo)*.

### THE PAYOFF

- **Bail is paid at the jail, the court or a licensed bondsman, never over Cash App, Zelle, crypto or gift cards.**
- Hang up and call the jail at the number on the sheriff's website.
- A caller who knows the name and the charges proves nothing (Clallam County Sheriff).
- After Woodson: "pretrial services" doesn't call families to collect.

# 3. YOUR DAUGHTER'S VOICE, SCREAMING. FIVE HOURS ON THE PHONE

**Slot:** F2 A option · **Confidence: MEDIUM** · *Strong victim and fresh coverage; voice cloning is on the dead list*

**SLOT FIT:** It could carry an A: a parent is the target, the villain is clear, and she lost real money. The problem is the dead list: Jeff cloned his own voice on May 13. It only works pitched as what's new since then: hours on the line, the caller ID, and the errands to wire the money.

### WORKING TEASE

> THE PHONE RINGS. IT'S YOUR DAUGHTER. SHE'S SCREAMING.
> THEN A MAN GETS ON. HE HAS HER. DON'T HANG UP. DON'T TELL ANYONE.
> THIS MOM STAYED ON THAT CALL FOR FIVE AND A HALF HOURS. SHE WIRED 54 HUNDRED DOLLARS.
> HER DAUGHTER WAS FINE THE WHOLE TIME.
> THE ONE QUESTION THAT ENDS THIS CALL IN SECONDS.

### THE HOOK

Deborah Del Mastro of Martinez, California, a Navy veteran, heard what sounded like her daughter Sarah, then a man claiming to be with a cartel. He demanded $20,000. Fox News says he kept her on the line for about five and a half hours, told her to keep her phone charged, gave her a cover story, and yelled when she muted the call to talk to her husband. She sent $5,400 in four wire transfers, starting at a Walmart (ABC7, May 24; Fox News, Oct 7). In Livingston, Montana, Victoria Bond lost more than $2,000 by Western Union at an Albertsons during a call of about an hour (MontanaRightNow, Aug 4).

### HOW IT WORKS

- A cloned voice of your child, built from clips online, then a "kidnapper" takes over.
- **They never let you hang up**: the call is the trap. You can't check on your daughter while you're on the line.
- They send you on errands to wire money or buy prepaid cards.
- The FBI warns some now send altered "proof of life" photos or video (IC3, Dec 5, 2025).

### WHAT'S NEW

May 13 was voice cloning itself (Jeff cloned his own voice). New: the hours-long call, the errands to Walmart and Albertsons, and the FBI's warning about faked photos and videos.

### FOOTAGE WE NEED

- **The get: ABC7 Bay Area's Del Mastro package** (about 5.5K views; observed 10/19). Fox's CyberGuy clip, "This scam call cost me $5,400."
- MontanaRightNow (Bond). WGAL's "fake kidnapping videos" (content unverified).

### THE PAYOFF

- **Hang up and call your loved one yourself.** No real kidnapper needs you to stay on the line.
- Set a family code word, in person.
- Text another family member to check while you stay calm.
- No ransom is ever paid by wire or prepaid card at a store.

**Slot:** F2 B · **Confidence: MEDIUM** · *Big new numbers from NPR; 14 days after our mall story*

**SLOT FIT:** A strong B, and an A on numbers alone. But the 10/14 A was mall skincare (Bee & Co), so this has to be pitched as the follow-up: one family company behind thousands of stores, and the fake decline.

### WORKING TEASE

> SHE AGREED TO SPEND 600 DOLLARS ON SKIN CREAM.
> THEN THE CLERK SAID: "YOUR CARD DECLINED. GOT ANOTHER ONE?"
> IT HADN'T DECLINED. HER STATEMENT SHOWED TWO CHARGES. 44 THOUSAND DOLLARS.
> AFTER OUR SHOW TWO WEEKS AGO, NPR FOUND WHO'S BEHIND THOUSANDS OF THESE STORES.
> THE ONE THING TO DO THE SECOND A CLERK SAYS "DECLINED."

### THE HOOK

NPR traced dozens of skincare brands, sold in what a former employee estimates are at least 2,000 stores, to one family company, Mazal Group of Chatsworth, California (Oct 14). In police reports, shoppers say a clerk told them their card declined when it hadn't and charged a second card too. In Santa Fe, a 67-year-old agreed to spend $600 and found two charges totaling $44,280. NPR lists Mary Ellen Eckhert, 82, at $53,500 across four cards. Mazal says it "does not own or operate any retail locations."

### WHAT'S NEW

The 10/14 show covered Bee & Co. NPR doesn't connect Bee & Co to Mazal. New: one company behind many store names, the fake decline, a federal case a decade ago, and a lab test that found markups of up to 130 times.

### FOOTAGE WE NEED

- Our own 10/14 clips for the callback.

### THE PAYOFF

- **"Declined"? Stop. Open your bank app before you hand over another card.**
- Ask for the total in writing before you pay.
- Already charged? Dispute it with your card company right away.

# 5. IF YOU GET THIS EMAIL FROM YOUR DOCTOR, DON'T CLICK IT

**Slot:** F2 B, or A option · **Confidence: MEDIUM** · *Strong viewer tip; lands 19 days after the Oct 9 Medicare show*

**SLOT FIT:** A strong B. A scam with a villain, and the viewer is the target (MyChart has about 190M patients, per The National Desk). It's time-sensitive: open enrollment ends Dec 7. As an A it's thin: the losses we know of are small ($9.95), and the Oct 9 show already had scammers posing as a victim's doctor.

> **CLIP** — YouTube, CBS Philadelphia, about 169,755 views (YouTube search, observed Oct 19), about one month old
> https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vATdvkX57eU
> Appears to show: a CBS "In Your Corner" warning about scammers posing as MyChart (from the title and description). It doesn't say whether it covers the kit.
> *Not watched — no timecode, no outcue, no confirmation of what's on screen.*

### WORKING TEASE

> IT LOOKS LIKE IT CAME FROM YOUR DOCTOR'S OFFICE. SAME LOGO. SAME PORTAL YOU USE FOR YOUR LAB RESULTS.
> "YOU'VE BEEN SELECTED. CLAIM YOUR FREE MEDICARE KIT."
> FREE… UNTIL THE LAST STEP. JUST PAY THE SHIPPING.
> 41 HEALTH SYSTEMS ARE NOW WARNING THEIR OWN PATIENTS.
> ONE OF OUR VIEWERS GOT IT TOO. HERE'S WHAT SHE DID RIGHT.

### THE HOOK

The emails and texts copy MyChart, the patient portal from Epic. Epic says it's not a breach, just scammers borrowing a name people trust. Click, and a countdown clock and a short survey lead to a "free" kit with a small shipping fee at the end. That fee is how they get your card (Denver Health, Oct 9). In an FTC complaint Gizmodo got through a records request, an Indiana patient wrote: "They then charge you the 9.95 and immediately try charging your card another 29.95." Pennsylvania's attorney general says no such kit exists. Our own viewer, 77, got the email on Oct 17, checked her real MyChart, found nothing there, and blocked the sender.

### WHAT'S NEW

Oct 9 ("They're Mailing THIS Card to Seniors") covered fake Medicare cards, spoofed calls and a fake call from a doctor. New here: a fake *email* that copies the portal itself.

### FOOTAGE WE NEED

- **The get: our viewer's screenshots**, with her permission.
- CBS Philadelphia (above); NBC10 Philadelphia, 12K. 13News Now and KSAT are about the kit by name (small clips).
- Jeff opens the real MyChart app next to the fake email *(demo)*.

### THE PAYOFF

- **Never open MyChart from an email or a text.** Open the app, or type in the address yourself, like our viewer did.
- MyChart doesn't run giveaways, and a countdown clock means it's fake (Epic).
- Already paid the shipping? Call your card company and get a new card.
- Report it to your doctor's office, and in Pennsylvania to scams@attorneygeneral.gov.

### THE HOOK

Monet Swann bought a $2 bottle of water from a vendor in Midtown Atlanta on Oct 1. She scanned his QR code and says she shared her screen to pay. "I screenshared and authorized a two-dollar purchase from Zelle, and unfortunately, he took $1,200 from me." Another $1,200 went out on Apple Pay. Atlanta police are investigating, and her bank hasn't refunded her (FOX 5 Atlanta, Oct 9).

### FOOTAGE WE NEED

- Check the web address a QR code opens before you go further (FTC).
