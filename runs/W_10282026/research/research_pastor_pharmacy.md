## (B) Pharmacy: insurance copay can cost more than cash/coupon price

### Seed
- TikTok https://www.tiktok.com/@anna.harris.rn/video/7693599394985200926 — TikTok refuses requests from this container (returned only "TikTok - Make Your Day"). The $200 vs $16 Walmart claim is UNVERIFIED; manual pull needed.

### Confirmed facts
- **Gag clauses banned, signed Oct 10, 2018.**
  - Know the Lowest Price Act of 2018 (Medicare Part D + Medicare Advantage; plan years on/after Jan 1, 2020): plans can't restrict a pharmacy "from informing" enrollees of a lower cash price, or penalize it. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-115publ262/html/PLAW-115publ262.htm
  - Patient Right to Know Drug Prices Act, Public Law 115-263 (group health plans/insurers, and their PBMs): can't block pharmacies from telling enrollees the difference between plan cost and cash price. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-115publ263/html/PLAW-115publ263.htm
  - Consumer Reports (Lisa L. Gill), Oct 10, 2018: "Pharmacists have long known that consumers can sometimes pay less for prescription drugs by not using their insurance." Laws don't force pharmacists to volunteer it — ask (California is an exception requiring disclosure). https://www.consumerreports.org/drug-prices/trump-signs-bill-banning-gag-clauses-helping-consumers-save-on-drugs
- **How often copay > cost — USC Schaeffer / JAMA research letter, Mar 15, 2018** (Van Nuys, Joyce, Goldman, Ribero). https://schaeffer.usc.edu/research/study-finds-consumer-copays-frequently-exceed-cost-of-common-prescriptions/
  - 9.5M commercial claims (Optum Clinformatics), first half of 2013: copay exceeded the drug's cost to the insurer in ~23% of claims; 28% of generic claims vs 6% of brand. Avg overpayment $7.69; >$135M total in 2013. CAUTION: data is 13 years old. (KFF Health News 2018 described the same data as "Part D claims" — Schaeffer's own page says Optum; use Schaeffer.)
- **"Clawbacks" explained — NBC News, Oct 17, 2017.** https://www.nbcnews.com/health/health-care/could-your-health-insurance-be-costing-you-money-pharmacy-n811171 — PBM collects copay higher than cash price and keeps part = "clawback." Examples: simvastatin $43 copay vs $19 cash; ondansetron $129 vs $18. NCPA survey: 83% of 640 pharmacists saw ≥10 clawbacks in prior month. (Old.)
- **Annals of Internal Medicine 2023 (UToledo, Patel/Vaidya), release Sept 15, 2023.** https://news.utoledo.edu/index.php/09_15_2023/utoledo-study-finds-insurance-doesnt-always-make-drugs-cheaper — Top 20 generics, MEPS data: >40% of the drugs cheaper via GoodRx, ~20% via Amazon card vs insured out-of-pocket; est. savings >$1.8B (GoodRx), $969M (Amazon). Biggest gains for uninsured and those who hadn't met deductible. Vaidya: "Insurance is needed." NOTE: ClearHealthCosts attributes a "79% of the time" Part D figure to this writeup; the UToledo page does NOT contain 79% — do not use it.
- **Cost Plus Drugs — Annals of Internal Medicine 2026 (Lin et al.), AJMC May 25, 2026.** https://www.ajmc.com/view/cost-plus-drugs-beats-commercial-insurance-co-pays-80-of-the-time — 62M generic commercial (employer) claims, 2024, vs 2025 Cost Plus prices: Cost Plus cheaper ~80% of the time when cost-sharing >$15; but cheaper on only 15% of all prescriptions (median saving $6). For cost-sharing >$100: median $140 via insurance vs $25 via Cost Plus. Does NOT generalize to Medicare. Cash buys don't count toward deductible/OOP max.
- **Checkbook / ConsumerMan (Herb Weisbaum), updated July 7, 2025.** https://www.checkbook.org/national/consumers-notebook/articles/Save-on-Prescription-Drugs-Why-Paying-Out-of-Pocket-Might-Be-Cheaper-Than-Your-Insurance-Copay-7410 — AARP's Leigh Purvis: "The world of prescription drug prices is very much a black box..." Coupon buys don't count toward deductible. Coupon sites aren't covered by HIPAA. Steps list (check a coupon site every fill; ask pharmacist for lowest price; 90-day supply; shop around; Costco/Sam's pharmacy usable without membership).
- **Cost Plus pricing ("cost + 15% + pharmacy fee", no insurance accepted):** costplusdrugs.com returned 403 — NOT confirmed from their own site. AJMC confirms only "fixed markup," no paid membership, $5.25 shipping used in the study.
- **GoodRx own site:** returned 403 — not loaded. GoodRx-specific claims unconfirmed.

### Medicare Part D: can enrollees use coupons? (confirmed pieces)
- Yes, you can skip the plan and pay the discount/cash price, but you can't combine both on the same fill — Healthline via AOL, updated Oct 18, 2024: https://www.aol.com/lifestyle/drug-coupons-medicare-know-124500953.html ("you cannot combine both in the same transaction"; coupon spending "will not count toward your Part D plan's out-of-pocket maximum").
- CMS official guidance (memo Oct 11, 2006, "Lower Cash Price Policy"): https://www.cms.gov/medicare/prescription-drug-coverage/prescriptiondrugcovcontra/downloads/qadiscountsandtroop_100606.pdf — a lower cash/discount-card price bought at a NETWORK pharmacy CAN count toward TrOOP if the enrollee submits documentation to the plan; but the policy applies only in the deductible or coverage-gap phase.
- KFF (Apr 20, 2023): coverage gap eliminated in 2025; $2,000 OOP cap in 2025. https://www.kff.org/medicare/changes-to-medicare-part-d-in-2024-and-2025-under-the-inflation-reduction-act-and-how-enrollees-will-benefit/
- CMS CY2026 Rate Announcement (Apr 7, 2025): 2026 Part D out-of-pocket threshold **$2,100**. https://www.cms.gov/files/document/2026-announcement.pdf
- Manufacturer copay coupons (vs. discount cards): Healthline cites the Anti-Kickback Statute; the CMS memo notes discount programs must comply with the anti-kickback statute. Could not load a current Medicare.gov page stating the rule directly — confirm with Medicare.gov / 1-800-MEDICARE before airing the exact rule.
- Practical on-air framing supported by sources: for 55+ on Part D, ask for both prices; a cheaper coupon price may not count toward the $2,100 cap unless you send the receipt to your plan (CMS 2006; current applicability after the gap's elimination not confirmed).

### Official steps / advice with sources
- Ask the pharmacist for the cash price every time (Consumer Reports 2018; Checkbook 2025).
- Check a coupon site (GoodRx, SingleCare, etc.) each fill (Checkbook 2025).
- Coupon/cash buys don't count toward deductible/OOP max (Checkbook 2025; AJMC 2026; Healthline 2024) — on Part D, submit receipts to plan (CMS 2006 memo).
- Don't drop Part D for coupons — late enrollment penalty (healthinsurance.org, undated: https://www.healthinsurance.org/faqs/whats-the-difference-between-prescription-discount-plans-and-prescription-drug-insurance/; Healthline 2024).
