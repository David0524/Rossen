## STORY A: Grandparent scam with a deepfaked FACE on a live video call

### KEY VERIFICATION (read first)
**Could not find a single real, documented 2025-2026 case of a scammer using a deepfaked/face-swapped LIVE video call
posing as a family member to an older person.** Every real family-emergency case I loaded is VOICE-clone only (or
not stated). Live deepfake video calls ARE documented, but for impersonating executives, officials, or government
leaders, not grandchildren. The "let me see you no longer proves it" premise is currently supported by official
warnings and expert statements, not by a named victim. If we pitch it, frame it as "the next step / officials warn,"
not "it's happening to grandparents."

### Official warnings / protection steps
- Agree on a family code word/secret phrase (FBI PSA241203; FBI PSA251205).
- Hang up and call the loved one back on a number you know (FTC page; FBI PSA241203).
- Call someone else in the family (FTC).
- Look for imperfections: "distorted hands or feet, unrealistic teeth or eyes, indistinct or irregular faces …
  inaccurate shadows, watermarks, lag time, voice matching, and unrealistic movements" (FBI PSA241203).
- "Always attempt to contact your loved one before considering paying any ransom demand"; "Screenshot or record proof
  of life photos whenever possible" (FBI PSA251205).
- Limit public video/voice of yourself; make social accounts private (FBI PSA241203).

### Official word on the real way to pay bail
- Dane County: "Bail can be only be posted directly in-person at the facility." Will never call to demand bail.
- Richmond County: legitimate options are a licensed bail bondsman, a property bond, or a signature/own-recognizance
  bond "when authorized by the court"; will never ask for Cash App, PayPal, Zelle, Apple Pay, crypto, gift cards.
- Brown County MN: verify warrant and bail amount at the sheriff's office; cash bail paid there with a signed receipt.
- Dallas County: bond is "handled strictly through the court system or licensed bail bond agencies."
- Thomas County: two approved bonding companies; legit bondsmen won't take Bitcoin or Cash App; no Marshal/deputy
  will threaten a warrant for not paying someone's bond.
- Caldwell County: inmates who want to post bond "are permitted to make direct phone calls themselves."
- Burke County: call a bondsman on their publicly listed number; verified list posted in jail lobby.
- OKC (News 9): bondsmen typically don't solicit families; bonds usually ~10% ("thousand-dollar bond ... $100").
- Common thread: hang up, call the jail on a number you look up yourself; the caller knowing the name/charges proves
  nothing (Clallam: "The fact that a caller knows the name of someone who is in jail does not mean the call is
  legitimate.").
