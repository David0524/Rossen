## (A) Mall skincare: "your card declined" double charge (NPR / Mazal Group)

### Confirmed facts
- **Main article.** NPR, "The skincare scheme that led to consumer complaints, lawsuits and a federal investigation." Published Oct 14, 2026, 5:00 AM ET, by Caley Fox Shannon and Monika Evstatieva. The page's browser title is "How mall skincare stores targeted hundreds of shoppers." Subhead: "Free samples, high-pressure pitches and $50,000 bills."
  https://www.npr.org/2026/10/14/nx-s1-5550974/skincare-scheme-mazal-group-investigation (raw HTML loaded with curl; datePublished 2026-10-14T05:00-04:00)
  Mirror loaded: https://www.kpbs.org/news/economy/2026/10/14/the-skincare-scheme-that-led-to-consumer-complaints-lawsuits-and-a-federal-investigation
- **The family business.** NPR: "Mazal Group, a California-based company managing dozens of brands sold in thousands of stores worldwide. And those brands are all owned by one family."
  - It is headquartered in Chatsworth, Calif. Owner Daphna Oded Weingarten runs it with her husband, Eli Weingarten, and the Oded side of the family (Avihu Oded, Adi Sharvit Oded).
  - A former employee estimated the company "contracts with at least 2,000 stores worldwide."
  - Per the KPBS mirror summary, the family has more than 50 skin and hair care trademarks. Brands named include Voupre, Lionesse, Introstem, Avinichi, Orogold, Vine Vera and HerStyler.
  - Per the KPBS mirror summary, Mazal's California business license was terminated, and the HQ sign now reads "INV Brands" (Innovation Brands Inc., founded 2024).
  - **Note on the seed's wording:** NPR says "dozens of brands" and "thousands of stores." It does not say "dozens of stores."
- **The declined-card detail, exactly as NPR states it (raw text):**
  - "In police reports, others allege a store employee told them their card declined when it had not and charged a second card as well."
  - "One woman said a salesperson kept her in the store for hours until she agreed to spend $600 on products from the skincare brand Voupre. The salesperson said her credit card was declined, so she provided another. Later that day, she was 'mortified' to see two charges totaling $44,280 in her banking app." She is 67, unnamed, and the case is at Santa Fe Spa Center by Voupre, N.M.
  - Another account from the raw text: a salesperson "added a zero," which turned $1,300 into $13,000.
- **Reporting base (raw text).** More than 50 people interviewed, nearly 500 consumer complaints obtained through FOIA, court records, and visits to stores in VA, MD and CA.
- **Federal history (raw text).**
  - Federal agents documented a "credit card overcharge scheme" more than a decade ago. That included the 2013 "Salty Winds" operation against Shai Cohen.
  - Omer Gur pleaded guilty in 2016. In total there were more than a dozen guilty pleas for visa fraud and money laundering.
  - DHS, Labor, State, IRS and FBI "would not say whether oversight or enforcement is ongoing."
- **Legal actions (KPBS mirror summary).**
  - The New Mexico AG sued Santa Fe Spa Center by Voupre. It settled in 2025 for $350,000 in restitution.
  - A Hawaii class action settled in June 2026 for $950,000, with $650,000 of that from Mazal and the family, who denied wrongdoing.
- **Company response (KPBS mirror summary).** Through attorney Brian Tilker: "Mazal Group does not own or operate any retail locations that sell cosmetic products..." and "Nor does Mazal Group manufacture any products." NPR's raw text says Mazal "publicly shifted responsibility ... onto individual store owners."
- **Lab test (KPBS summary).** NPR tested Voupre Anti-Aging Rescue Solution, which stores buy for $19.50 and which carries a $1,300 suggested retail price (raw text). The test found no vitamin A or E despite the label.

## (B) $2 water QR code drained $2,400 (Atlanta)

### Confirmed facts
- **11Alive article.** "Woman says Atlanta water boys stole thousands from her using Zelle, Apple Pay."
  https://www.11alive.com/article/news/crime/woman-says-atlanta-water-boys-stole-thousands-from-her-using-zelle-apple-pay-scam/85-a4355413-b645-46cc-b06a-023328fe25d7
  The page returned **403 to both WebFetch and curl, so I could not read it directly.** The title is confirmed by YouTube oEmbed. Its date is about Oct 5, 2026, inferred from 11Alive's Ian Hill Threads post of 10/05/26 linking it, and Hollywood Unlocked citing 11Alive on Oct 6.
- **Fox 5 Atlanta.** "Woman scammed out of $2.4K during Midtown water purchase," by Eric Perry. Published Oct 9, 2026; updated Oct 15, 2026.
  https://www.fox5atlanta.com/news/howard-graduate-scammed-out-2-4k-during-midtown-water-purchase
  - The victim is **Monet Swann**, a Howard University graduate.
  - It happened around 5 p.m. on Oct 1, 2026, at 10th St. and Techwood Dr. in Midtown.
  - Two $1,200 transactions: one Zelle, one found on Apple Pay. Total $2,400.
  - Her bank (not named) has not refunded her.
  - **Atlanta police are investigating.** No suspect has been identified.
  - The family set up a GoFundMe.
  - Fox 5's lede says "nearly $2,500," which conflicts with its own $2,400 total.
- **11Alive's Threads post** (@ianhillmedia, 10/05/26) says "$2,000." That conflicts with $2,400. Use $2,400.
- **Mechanism, as Swann reports it.**
  - She scanned a QR code for Zelle and **screen-shared** her phone. Quote via Daily Hodl (Oct 9, 2026), credited to 11Alive: "So, I screenshared and authorized a two-dollar purchase from Zelle and unfortunately, he took $1,200 from me."
    https://dailyhodl.com/2026/10/09/2400-abruptly-drained-from-georgia-womans-bank-account-after-she-purchases-2-bottle-of-water-from-street-vendor-report/
  - How the Apple Pay charge happened is **not explained** in any source. Hollywood Unlocked (Oct 6) says he "somehow got into her Apple Pay."
  - **No source says it was a fake payment page, and none says she handed her phone over.** Reported as: scanned QR, screen-shared, authorized what she thought was $2.
  - Hollywood Unlocked: https://hollywoodunlocked.com/woman-who-tried-to-support-water-boy-with-2-purchase-says-he-took-2400-from-her-using-zelle-apple-pay/
